# PRIORITY 2 — THE REPLANTING CLIFF

## 2.1 Estate size and age

**FACT** (LSIP disclosure, 31 Dec 2024): nucleus planted area **111,367 ha** —
**91,152 ha oil palm** (82% of planted area), 16,231 ha rubber, 3,984 ha cocoa
and tea. Plasma partnership adds 35,779 ha.

**FACT** (two independent analyst sources): LSIP's **average oil palm age was
18–19 years as of September 2022**. Kontan quotes analyst Yosua: *"umur tanaman
relatif tua, rata-rata kisaran 18 tahun."*

**INFERENCE:** with replanting running far below the required rate (below), the
average age has advanced roughly one year per calendar year. **Average palm age
as of mid-2026 is therefore ~21–23 years.**

Oil palm economics: immature 0–3 yrs (no yield, cash consuming); building 4–7;
**prime 8–18**; declining 19–25 (yield falls, height makes harvesting
progressively uneconomic); replant at ~25. **LSIP's average tree is past prime
and inside the declining band.** That is not a forecast; it is where the estate
already sits.

## 2.2 Replanting is running at roughly one-third of requirement

**FACT:** LSIP replants **1,000–1,500 ha per year** (reported practice, on palms
aged 25–30).

**Arithmetic:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Oil palm area | 91,152 ha |
| Economic life | 25 years |
| **Steady-state replanting requirement** | **3,646 ha/yr** |
| **Actual replanting** | **~1,250 ha/yr (midpoint)** |
| **Replant rate as % of requirement** | **~34%** |
| **Implied full-estate replanting cycle** | **91,152 ÷ 1,250 = 73 years** |

**A 73-year replanting cycle on a crop with a 25-year economic life is not a
capital-allocation choice — it is arithmetic impossibility.** Either the
disclosed replanting rate must roughly triple, or the estate progressively
converts into over-age, sub-economic hectares. There is no third path.

**INFERENCE:** at ~2,400 ha/yr of shortfall sustained for ~8 years, the estate
now carries on the order of **19,000 ha (≈21% of the oil palm area) that is past
economic life and still counted as producing**.

## 2.3 The yield evidence corroborates it

**FACT:**
- 2022 full-year TBS (FFB) guidance: **941,000 t**.
- H1 2026 nucleus TBS: **505,000 t**, described as *"relatively the same"* as H1
  2025 → annualised ~1,010,000 t.
- **Four years, essentially zero FFB growth**, despite a fixed and slightly
  growing planted area.

**INFERENCE:** implied nucleus FFB yield ≈ 1,010,000 t ÷ ~85,000 mature ha ≈
**~12 t/ha/yr**. Indonesian industry average is 18–20 t/ha; well-managed estates
reach 22–25 t/ha. **LSIP is producing at roughly 55–65% of a well-run estate.**
Kontan's analyst separately flags LSIP's *"low CPO yield per TBS compared to
peers."* Two independent yield deficiencies, compounding.

**FACT:** 2025 revenue rose 20.8% (4,562.5 → 5,511.7bn) and net income rose 27.9%
on **flat volume**. The entire 2025 earnings improvement — the improvement that
drives the screener's F-Score of 7 and its "Compounder" business
classification — was **CPO price, not operations.**

## 2.4 Reported FCF is borrowing from future yield

**THESIS:** LSIP's reported free cash flow is flattered by capex it should be
spending and is not. Replanting is uniquely expensive because you pay twice:
you spend the development capex *and* you surrender 4 years of production on
that land. Deferring it inflates FCF on both sides of the equation
simultaneously.

**Quantification (ASSUMPTION-heavy — the per-hectare cost is an industry
estimate, not an LSIP disclosure):**

| Component | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Replanting shortfall | ~2,400 ha/yr |
| Indonesian immature development cost | ~IDR 75m/ha cumulative (ASSUMPTION) |
| **Deferred capex, annual** | **~IDR 180bn/yr** |
| Additional area held immature at steady state (2,400 × 4 yrs) | ~9,600 ha |
| Producing area reduction vs today | ~7 percentage points |
| Revenue impact (7% of 5,512bn) | ~IDR 386bn |
| EBIT impact at ~35% incremental margin | ~IDR 135bn |
| **Total unreflected annual FCF drag** | **~IDR 315bn** |
| TTM reported FCF | IDR 1,564bn |
| **Sustainable FCF haircut** | **~20%** |

This 20% is the haircut applied at step 3 of the valuation waterfall. Treat the
point estimate as soft; treat the **direction** as established — LSIP's FCF
cannot be a steady state while its replanting cycle is 73 years long.

**Consequence for the screener:** both EPV and DCF are overstated, because both
capitalise a cash flow that is not repeatable. EPV in particular assumes
maintenance capex ≈ depreciation. Here maintenance capex is *structurally below*
what the biological asset requires, so EPV overstates by construction. The
screener's own "Normalised/Trailing EBIT = 0.685" flag partially catches the
price cycle but catches none of this.

**Insufficient evidence — requires further research:** I could not obtain a

**What I can establish:** the discount is not new, and it is not idiosyncratic
to LSIP.

### Naming the cause

The discount is **structural and group-level, not stock-specific**. Four
reinforcing causes, in order of weight:

1. **Trapped capital.** 84% of market cap sits in cash at a 21–30% payout ratio
   under a 59.48% controller. The market capitalises retained earnings at
   approximately zero, and has done so for five years — proven by the 8% book
   CAGR against the 1% price CAGR.
2. **Unverifiable transfer pricing.** 96% of CPO goes to the parent. Margin is
   set inside the group, not by the market.
3. **Group contagion.** SIMP, the controller, trades at 0.25–0.50x P/B. A
   subsidiary cannot re-rate through a controller that is itself discounted
   50–75%. **This is the strongest single piece of evidence that the discount is
   a structural cause and not a mispricing.**
4. **A depleting biological asset carried at historical cost.** Book value
   includes bearer plants that are past economic life. A 0.70x P/B against a
   book that overstates the productive estate is not necessarily a discount at
   all.

**A discount persisting for a decade, present across the whole group, with a
mechanically explicable cause, is a price — not an opportunity.**

# STRESS TESTS

## Bear case

CPO mean-reverts toward the World Bank's USD 1,089/t or below while the export
levy stays at 12.5%. EBIT reverts to the 5-year normalised IDR 1,529bn (−31%
from trailing). Volume stays flat because the estate is over-age. Replanting
finally accelerates, adding ~IDR 180bn capex and removing ~7% of producing area
for four years. Payout stays at ~30%; cash builds to IDR 11–12tn and is still
undistributed by 2029. BI holds at 5.75%+.

**Result:** EPS falls to ~IDR 190. At the 8.5x peer PE that is IDR 1,615/share
— *still above today's price*, which is the honest bull rebuttal. But at LSIP's
own realised 5-year multiple (which has never re-rated), the price simply sits
at IDR 1,200–1,500 for another five years while you collect a 5–6% dividend that
underperforms a government bond. **The bear case here is not a crash — it is a
decade of nothing, which is exactly what the last decade was.**

## The thesis-breaker

**A single AGM.** If SIMP announces a payout ratio of 30% again in 2027 — with
cash then above IDR 9tn and above 90% of market cap — the cash trap is confirmed
empirically for a seventh consecutive year, and the correct valuation of that
cash to a minority holder converges on the step-5 case (near zero). At that
point the stock is worth ~IDR 1,062/share and is **38% overvalued at 1,465**.

The thesis does not break on operations. It breaks on the controller doing
exactly what the controller has always done.

## Value trap test — why has the market NOT recognised this? (concretely)

This is the question that decides the case, and the answer is not "the market is
inefficient."

1. **The market has had ten years and repeatedly declined.** Price CAGR 5Y =
   +0.98% while book compounded 8.0%. The market has seen the cash grow from
   IDR 3.8tn to IDR 8.4tn and marked it at approximately zero the entire way.
2. **The stock is not even cheap by its own history.** 10-year price percentile
   **0.6889**. It sits in the upper third of its own decade. The "discount"
   widened because book grew, not because price fell — so there is nothing to
   revert to.
3. **Indonesian retail is fully aware of the EV argument.** An Indonesian
   financial outlet has run the explicit headline *"EV Paling Terendah"* (lowest
   EV) on LSIP. This is not undiscovered information sitting in a spreadsheet.
   It has been published to the domestic retail base and the stock did not move.
4. **The controller is itself discounted 50–75% to book.** SIMP trades at
   0.25–0.50x P/B. There is no plausible mechanism by which the subsidiary
   re-rates while the parent does not.
5. **No one can force the outcome.** 59.48% single holder, no compulsory
   dividend under Indonesian law, no activist path, no institutional buyer large
   enough given USD ~120k/day liquidity.
6. **Sell-side has already priced the operations, not the cash.** The most
   bearish analyst target in the sample was IDR 1,300 — below today's price —
   explicitly on tree age and yield. Analysts who look at the estate rather than
   the balance sheet arrive near my number, not the screener's.

**Every one of these is a concrete, checkable reason. Together they say the
market has recognised it, priced it, and disagrees with the screener about what
trapped cash is worth. The market is right.**

## Contrarian test — the strongest case that I am wrong

I take this seriously; it is not a throwaway.

1. **The dividend is actually compounding fast.** 20 → 51 → 53 → 39 → 65 → 83.
   That is a **~33% CAGR over five years**, and 2026's IDR 83 at a stated 29.99%
   payout is the highest nominal dividend in the recent record. The trend is
   toward distribution, not away from it.
2. **A payout step-change is genuinely plausible.** SIMP and IndoAgri have their
   own capital needs, and upstreaming from a debt-free subsidiary holding
   IDR 8.4tn is the cheapest capital available anywhere in the group. If payout
   moves to 60–70%, DPS = IDR 180–195, a **12–13% yield at today's price**, and
   the stock re-rates violently. **This is the one catalyst that would prove me
   wrong, and it is controlled by exactly the party I am criticising.**
3. **A special dividend costs SIMP nothing it does not receive back.** SIMP takes
   59.48% of any distribution. The stated 2026 dividend already routes
   IDR 336.85bn to SIMP. The group has an incentive to distribute that grows
   with the size of the pile.
4. **B50 is a real demand shock.** The mandate raises domestic energy-sector CPO
   consumption toward 16–19m t/yr, tightening supply. Some forecasters see
   USD 1,500/t. LSIP is unhedged, debt-free, and fully levered to that price.
5. **Replanting is a fixable, self-correcting problem.** Accelerating it costs
   near-term FCF but rebases the estate. LSIP can fund a decade of aggressive
   replanting out of cash without touching the dividend — which is arguably the
   *best* use of the pile and would convert my Priority-2 criticism into the
   Priority-1 solution.
6. **Downside is genuinely well protected.** NCAV/share IDR 1,103 is 75% of the
   price. Zero debt. Altman Z 14.3. You are unlikely to lose 50% here.

**Honest weighing:** points 1, 2 and 5 are strong. If the payout ratio steps up
decisively, I am wrong and the stock doubles. My objection is that this is a
*bet on a controlling shareholder's future generosity*, and a 49% MOS computed

is the thesis, dressed as an input.

## Answers to the required questions

**Strongest Bull Argument:**
A debt-free plantation with IDR 1,227/share of net cash (84% of market cap) and
IDR 1,103/share of NCAV is trading at IDR 1,465, meaning the market values
110,000+ hectares of Sumatran and Sulawesi estates plus mills at an enterprise
value of IDR 237/share — while paying a 5.67% dividend that has compounded at
33% a year for five years. Downside is genuinely floored by the balance sheet
(Altman Z 14.3, current ratio 7.1), and a single AGM decision to raise the
payout to 60–70% would produce a 12–13% yield and force a re-rating.

**Strongest Bear Argument:**
The entire 49% MOS is the cash, counted twice — once as a balance-sheet add-back
and once as the interest income embedded in the capitalised earnings stream —
and that cash has never been distributed at more than a 30% payout under a
59.48% controller with no legal mechanism to force it. Remove the cash and its
earnings and the plantation alone is worth ~IDR 1,062/share against a price of
IDR 1,465, a 38% premium. Book value compounded 8%/yr for five years while the
price compounded 1%/yr, which is the market stating explicitly and repeatedly
that it assigns near-zero value to LSIP's retained capital.

**Why The Stock Could Be A Value Trap:**
Because it already has been one for a decade, and the cause is structural rather
than cyclical: 96% of output sold to the controlling parent at unverifiable
transfer prices; the parent SIMP itself trading at 0.25–0.50x book; a 21–30%
payout against a cash pile equal to 84% of market cap; no compulsory-dividend
law, no activist path, and USD ~120k/day liquidity that keeps out any buyer
large enough to force change. The stock sits in the 69th percentile of its
10-year price range — it is not depressed, it is *permanently repriced*, and the
apparent discount has been widening only because book value grows while the
price does not.

**Biggest Hidden Risk:**
The estate is being liquidated in slow motion and it does not appear in the
accounts. LSIP replants ~1,250 ha/yr against a steady-state requirement of 3,646
ha/yr on 91,152 ha of oil palm — an implied 73-year cycle on a 25-year crop —
while the average palm is already ~22 years old and FFB output has been flat for
four years at ~12 t/ha against an industry 18–20 t/ha. Reported FCF is
therefore borrowing from future yield on both sides at once (capex not spent,
production not surrendered), so EPV and DCF are overstated by an estimated ~20%,
and the book value that anchors the 0.70x P/B includes bearer plants past
economic life.

**Most Fragile Assumption:**
That net cash is worth 100 cents on the rupiah to a minority shareholder. Every
lens in the screener — Acquirer's Multiple 0.72, EBIT/EV yield 138%, Value Score
100.0, EPV, DCF — inherits this one assumption, so they are not four
confirmations but one assumption counted four times. Five consecutive years of
evidence (8% book CAGR, 1% price CAGR) say the market marks it far below face.

**Most Important Thesis-Breaker:**
The 2027 AGM. If the payout ratio comes in at ~30% again with cash then above
IDR 9tn, the trap is confirmed for a seventh year and the correct minority
valuation of that cash converges toward zero — putting fair value near
IDR 1,062/share, i.e. 28% below today's price. Conversely a payout above 60% or
a special dividend breaks *my* case immediately. Everything else — CPO price,
B50, levy, weather — is second order to this single decision by a party that has
consistently chosen accumulation.

**What Evidence Would Change My Mind:**
1. A declared payout ratio ≥60%, a special dividend, or a buyback — the only
   direct evidence that trapped cash is not trapped.
2. The 2025 annual report showing **actual replanting ≥3,000 ha/yr** and a
   published age-profile with <20% of area above 20 years. That would falsify
   Priority 2 outright.
3. FFB volume growth ≥8% YoY sustained over two years, proving the yield
   stagnation is agronomic noise rather than demography.
4. A related-party note showing SIMP transfer prices benchmarked to the KPBN
   Dumai/Belawan tender price with an independent-commissioner attestation.
5. Evidence the cash is contractually committed to a disclosed replanting or
   downstream programme with dated milestones — that converts a sterile pile
   into deployed capital and my Priority-1 objection largely dissolves.
6. A structural event: SIMP privatising LSIP, a mandatory tender, or a
   group reorganisation that collapses the holding-company discount.

**Probability Thesis Is Fundamentally Correct: 25%**
*(i.e. ~25% probability that LSIP is genuinely worth ~IDR 2,870 and the market
is simply wrong.)* Decomposed: P(payout steps up decisively within 3 years) ≈
30%; P(replanting turns out adequate and my Priority-2 case is wrong) ≈ 35%;
P(CPO stays at or above current levels through 2028) ≈ 45%. The thesis needs
roughly the first and one of the others. I also assign ~40% to a distinctly
different and more likely outcome: **the stock is approximately fairly valued at
IDR 1,400–1,700 and does nothing for another five years while paying ~5.5%** —
which is the trap, not the thesis.

## Investment Committee Verdict: **REJECT**

**Vetoed as a BUY. Downgrade to WATCH.**

**Grounds for veto:** the screener's 49% MOS fails on its own arithmetic. It
capitalises the interest income from the cash pile *and then adds the cash pile*,
and it values a free cash flow that is inflated by replanting the company is not
doing. Correcting only those two errors — before applying any judgement about
whether trapped cash deserves a discount at all — takes the MOS from 48.9% to
36.0%. Applying the market's own five-year revealed view of that cash (book +8%
CAGR, price +1% CAGR) takes it to +12.6% at a 50% haircut and **−37.9% at zero**.
A margin of safety that requires the cash to be marked at face is not a margin of
safety; it *is* the thesis. Per the framework's non-negotiable rule —
**cheapness alone never produces a BUY** — and here the cheapness is not merely
insufficient, it is substantially an artefact.

**This is a deep-value / special-situation name, not a Compounder, and the
special situation has not been announced.**

**Re-entry conditions (any one promotes to a full Stage-2 re-underwrite):**
1. Declared payout ratio ≥60%, a special dividend, or a buyback.
2. Disclosed replanting ≥3,000 ha/yr with a published age profile.
3. Price below **IDR 1,100** — which is roughly the plantation-only value plus a
   30% haircut on cash, and would restore a genuine margin of safety even under
   my most hostile assumptions. (Note the 52-week low was 1,030.)
