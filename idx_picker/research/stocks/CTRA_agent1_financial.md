## 1. Is reported profit real cash? — the CFO collapse, verified and explained

**FACT — the collapse is real and is in the audited statements.** Consolidated Statements of
Cash Flows, FY2025 and FY2024:

| Operating cash flow line | FY2025 | FY2024 | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Receipts from customers | 9,113,315 | 10,860,873 | **−1,747,558** |
| Payments to contractors, suppliers and others | (6,625,636) | (5,887,734) | **−737,902** |
| Salaries and allowances | (1,064,731) | (980,070) | −84,661 |
| Other operating expenses | (861,889) | (422,528) | −439,361 |
| Interest income received | 478,352 | 531,682 | −53,330 |
| Interest and other financial charges paid | (763,945) | (656,789) | −107,156 |
| Final and income taxes | (330,488) | (373,605) | +43,117 |
| Restricted funds movement | 129,282 | (83,319) | +212,601 |
| Related-party movements | 5,122 | (3,058) | +8,180 |
| **Net cash provided by operating activities** | **79,382** | **2,985,452** | **−2,906,070** |

The bridge reconciles to the rupiah. Management's own MD&A gives the identical decomposition
(*Laporan Tahunan 2025*, "Arus Kas dari Aktivitas Operasi": receipts −Rp 1,747.6bn, contractor
payments +Rp 737.9bn).

**FACT — the screener's derived CFO series is correct.** `FreeCashFlow + |CapEx|` reproduces the
audited figures exactly for both years (FY2024: 2,095.5 + 890.0 = 2,985.5 ✔; FY2025: −642.0 + 721.4
= 79.4 ✔). The screener's numbers are not the problem; nobody read them.

### Which cause — construction overspend, or weakening presales?

**FACT — it is presales, by a factor of 2.4:1.** Falling collections account for **60%** of the
Rp 2,906bn swing; higher contractor payments account for **25%**; the remaining 15% is a doubling
of "other operating expenses."

**FACT — and CTRA was *not* overbuilding.** In accrual terms, additions to real-estate inventory
(land purchases and development Rp 1,788.7bn + production costs Rp 3,246.1bn = Rp 5,034.8bn) were
**below** the amount released to cost of sales (Rp 5,272.3bn). Inventory rose only Rp 522bn.
Separately, cash spent on land dropped sharply: land-for-development acquisitions in investing
activities fell from Rp 1,754,904 to **Rp 212,167**, and total investing outflow fell from
Rp 2,751,166 to Rp 1,123,414. **CTRA roughly halved its land buying in FY2025 and CFO still
collapsed.** That removes the benign explanation entirely.

### The mechanism, stated precisely

**FACT.** Cash receipts track **marketing sales**; revenue tracks **handovers**.

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1-2025 | H1-2026 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Reported revenue | 11,187,565 | 12,616,695 | 5,881,870 | 5,194,062 |
| Cash receipts from customers | 10,860,873 | 9,113,315 | 4,723,434 | 4,369,310 |
| **Receipts / revenue** | **97.1%** | **72.2%** | 80.3% | 84.1% |

**FACT — the gap is exactly the drawdown of the customer-advance book.** FY2025 revenue exceeded
cash receipts by Rp 3,503,380. Contract liabilities (customer downpayments, Note 21) fell by
Rp 3,190,494 over the same year. **INFERENCE (high confidence): roughly Rp 3.2tn of FY2025's
Rp 12.6tn of revenue was the release of cash collected in 2022–2024, not cash earned in 2025.**

**THESIS.** CTRA is not manufacturing earnings; it is *harvesting a backlog*. Reported profit rose
25% in the same year presales fell 14% because the P&L is running on a 2022–2024 vintage while the
cash box runs on the 2025–2026 vintage. When the advance book is exhausted, revenue must converge
down toward collections. That convergence is arithmetic, not a forecast.

### The one genuinely important nuance the bear case is missing

**FACT — this is a one-year event, not a chronic accrual problem.** Cumulative FY2022–FY2025:
CFO Rp 10,689bn vs parent net income Rp 8,498bn = **126% cash conversion over four years**.

**FACT — the collapse was entirely in H2 2025, and H1 2026 has already recovered.** H1-2025 CFO was
Rp 738,607 (comparative in the June-2026 statements); FY2025 was Rp 79,382; therefore **H2-2025 CFO
was −Rp 659,225**. H1-2026 CFO was **Rp 807,691, up 9.4% YoY** despite collections falling 7.5% —
because CTRA cut contractor payments 14.1% (Rp 2,708,310 → Rp 2,327,300).

**INFERENCE.** Management has a working cash lever and is pulling it. But the lever is
*construction spend*, and pulling it defers the very handovers that generate FY2027–28 revenue.
It buys cash today by borrowing from tomorrow's P&L.

## 2. Working capital, inventory, and the customer-advance signal

### Contract liabilities — the cleanest forward indicator in the filings

**FACT (Note 21 / Note 20).** Downpayments received from third parties:

| | Dec-2024 | Dec-2025 | Jun-2026 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Current | 9,283,648 | 7,144,700 | 6,355,964 |
| Non-current | 1,734,976 | 683,430 | 748,032 |
| **Total** | **11,018,624** | **7,828,130** | **7,103,996** |
| Change | — | **−29.0%** | −9.2% (6m) |
| **Coverage of TTM revenue** | **0.99x (11.8 months)** | **0.62x (7.4 months)** | **0.60x (7.1 months)** |

**FACT.** This balance *is* the disclosed "aggregate transaction price allocated to remaining
performance obligations" (Note 29 cross-references Note 21). It is the contractual backlog.

**THESIS.** Forward revenue cover has fallen from ~12 months to ~7 months in eighteen months. This
is the single most decision-relevant number in this report, and it corroborates the presales data
the other agents established, from an independently audited source. It also caps the plausible
FY2027 revenue: with 7.1 months of contracted backlog and presales still falling, a second
consecutive revenue decline is close to mechanically locked in.

### Inventory and land bank

**FACT.** Real-estate assets, Jun-2026: inventories Rp 12,968,412 + land for development
Rp 10,127,659 = **Rp 23,096,071 = 49.5% of total assets and 94% of parent equity.**

**FACT (Note 8).** FY2025 inventory movement: land purchases/development Rp 1,788,716 + production
costs Rp 3,145,059, against COGS release of Rp 4,798,547 (residential). Total real-estate COGS
Rp 5,272,283.

**FACT.** Implied holding period = 23,096 / 5,272 = **4.4 years ≈ 1,590 days**, not the screener's
706. The screener's `Inventory` field captures only the current-asset line and omits Rp 10.1tn of
land for development. See §7.

**FACT.** No impairment allowance on inventories or on land for development at Dec-2025 or
Dec-2024; management asserts full realisability (Notes 8 and 12).

**FACT — a large, disclosed, unrecognised asset.** Independent appraisers (KJPP Willson & Rekan,
KJPP Rengganis Hamid & Rekan, KJPP Susan Widjojo & Rekan; latest report **9 March 2026**) valued
*certain* land for development at **Rp 27,240,423** against a carrying amount of **Rp 9,906,248**;
and property & equipment at Rp 6,351,748 against a carrying amount of Rp 3,255,042.

**INFERENCE.** Disclosed appraisal surplus ≈ Rp 17,334bn on land + Rp 3,097bn on PPE ≈
**Rp 20,431bn ≈ Rp 1,102 per share, pre-tax** — against a share price of 630 and a book value of
Rp 1,322/share. **ASSUMPTION/CAVEAT:** "certain land" is undefined, an appraisal is not a bid,
Indonesian land/building sales attract 2.5% final tax on gross proceeds, and monetisation runs
15–20 years. This does not make the stock cheap on its own, but it is the strongest single fact
supporting the 0.48x P/B floor and it belongs in the record.

## 3. Joint-operation partner receivables — is capital being lent to partners?

**FACT (Note 7 / Note 6).** "Piutang mitra usaha operasi bersama" — joint operation partner
receivables:

| Dec-2024 | Dec-2025 | Jun-2026 |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,310,482 | **1,532,625 (+17.0%)** | **1,566,147 (+2.2% in 6m)** |

**+19.5% over eighteen months, while presales fell 14% then 18%.**

**FACT.** The note states the balance "mainly derived from the proportional elimination and
consolidation mechanism applied by the Group for Joint Operation projects categorised as
Operasi Bersama (Note 42j)." Management believes all are collectible; **no expected credit loss has
been recognised.** The disclosure does not state interest terms; total interest receivable across
the whole group is Rp 4,585, so **INFERENCE (high confidence): the balance is non-interest-bearing
or nearly so.**

**FACT (Note 42j).** Under the cooperation agreements, "**the Partner provides the land** for
development" while CTRA's subsidiary "**runs the affairs of and sells** the project's landlots and
buildings," with revenue sharing "based on a certain percentage of the profit." **The sharing
percentages are not disclosed.** — *Insufficient evidence; requires further research.*

**THESIS — yes, capital is being lent to partners, and it is growing counter-cyclically.**
Rp 1,566bn is **6.4% of parent equity, 13.4% of market capitalisation, Rp 84/share**. At a 10%
opportunity cost that is ~Rp 157bn/year of foregone return ≈ 5.9% of FY2025 parent net income —
which never appears anywhere in the P&L. In a downturn, a receivable from a landowner partner whose
only asset is illiquid land in a secondary city is the definition of an exposure that looks fine
until it does not.

**FACT — one partnership has already failed, and it has been frozen for thirteen years.**
Note 42i: PT Citra Swadaya Raya (CSDR, 55.9%-held, Kemayoran project with PT Sumur Rejeki). CMH
"has invested funds in a significant amount" but "**the Project nevertheless is unable to be
executed**"; CTRA is the plaintiff in multiple breach-of-contract and tort suits running since 2019.
CSDR's carrying value is Rp 350,429 (2024: Rp 350,531) — **unchanged, unimpaired, and unproductive.**

## 4. Capitalised interest

**FACT — policy permits it.** Note 2m: cost of land under development includes "direct and indirect
development costs related to real estate development activities **and borrowing costs, if any**."
Note 2n: cost of apartments and offices under construction includes "**borrowing costs**."

**FACT — the amount is not disclosed.** The FY2025 statements quantify capitalised borrowing costs
only for property & equipment, where they state there were **none**. There is no quantified
disclosure of borrowing costs capitalised into inventories or land for development.
**Insufficient evidence — requires further research.**

**INFERENCE (moderate confidence) — approximately Rp 138bn in FY2025, and approximately nil in
FY2024.** Reconciling cash to P&L:

| | FY2025 | FY2024 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest and other financial charges **paid** (cash flow statement) | 763,945 | 656,789 |
| Contractual finance costs in P&L (Note 35: bank interest 447,031 + bank other 16,807 + notes interest 115,617 + notes other 46,805) | 626,260 | 662,223 |
| **Unexplained excess of cash paid over expensed** | **+137,685** | −5,434 |

**THESIS — capitalised interest is *not* the accounting story here, and margins are not materially
flattered by it.** Rp 138bn is 1.1% of revenue. Because it sits in inventory rather than COGS, the
FY2025 gross margin is unaffected; expensing it would have cut pre-tax profit 4.8% and would reduce
FY2027–28 COGS by the same amount (~0.3pp of gross margin). It is a timing item.

**The material "hidden interest" runs the other way — and it is disclosed.** See §5.

## 5. The PSAK 115 significant financing component — 9.4% of gross profit is imputed interest

**FACT — explicitly disclosed in Note 29:** *"Included in the 2025 net sales are the finance income
representing significant financing component impact in accordance with PSAK 115 amounting to
**Rp 561,121** (2024: Rp 399,585)."* Repeated verbatim in the FY2025 annual report MD&A. H1-2026
statements disclose **Rp 261,661** (H1-2025: Rp 303,374).

**FACT — the matching expense (Note 35 / Note 34):** finance costs include "Contract liabilities —
significant financing component" of **Rp 439,821** in FY2025 (FY2024: Rp 568,920); H1-2026
Rp 144,843 (H1-2025: Rp 206,958). That is **36.7% of FY2025 total finance costs.**

**What this means, mechanically.** Where customers pay well in advance of handover, PSAK 115
requires CTRA to accrete interest on the customer advance (charged to finance costs, increasing the
liability) and then recognise a *correspondingly larger* revenue figure on handover. So:

- **Rp 561,121 of FY2025 revenue is imputed interest, not a property sale.** It carries **no COGS**.
- It is **4.45% of revenue**, **9.4% of gross profit**, and **14.6% of operating profit**.
- It grew Rp 161,536 YoY, which is **11.3% of the entire FY2025 revenue increase of Rp 1,429,130**.
- Stripping it: FY2025 gross margin falls from **47.5% to 45.1%**; operating margin from **30.4% to
  27.1%**; operating profit from Rp 3,833.8bn to **Rp 3,272.7bn (−14.6%)**.
- Net P&L effect is smaller — revenue side (561.1) less finance-cost side (439.8) = **+121.3bn
  pre-tax, 4.6% of parent net income.**

**THESIS — and it is now going into reverse.** This item is a function of the contract-liability
balance, which has fallen 35.5% in eighteen months. The finance-cost side already fell 30% YoY in
H1-2026; the revenue side fell 13.8%. **A shrinking advance book mechanically removes a
zero-cost, 100%-margin revenue line from FY2026–28 — a second-order drag on top of the presales
decline that no consensus model I can see has isolated.**

## 6. Minority leakage — and a correction to the brief

**The brief's premise does not survive the filings.** The brief posits that "JO projects consolidate
fully but a third of the economics belong to partners."

**FACT (Note 2l).** The JOs are accounted for under PSAK 66 as **joint operations**: "the Group as a
joint operator recognises **its assets, including its share of any assets held jointly; its
liabilities…; its revenue from the sale of its share of the output…; its share of the revenue from
the sale of output by the joint operation; and its expenses, including its share of any expenses
incurred jointly.**" Note 42j presents JO figures separately and expressly labels them
"**(100% portion)**" — i.e. as *additional* information, because that is not what is in the
consolidated accounts. Note 7 describes the partner receivable as arising from "**proportional
elimination**."

**INFERENCE (high confidence).** Consolidated revenue and profit are therefore **already net of the
JO partners' share**. Headline ROE is not inflated by unconsolidated partner economics. The leakage
is on the **balance sheet**, not the P&L: it takes the form of Rp 1,566bn of interest-free funding
extended to partners (§3).

**FACT — but the JOs are where the profit is.** Totalling the Note 42j / Note 40 tables (my
arithmetic on the disclosed line items):

| JO aggregate (100% portion) | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1-2026 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Total assets | 11,316,894 | 10,382,167 | 10,620,657 |
| Total liabilities | 10,852,737 | 9,484,067 | 9,169,034 |
| **Net assets** | 464,157 | **898,100** | 1,451,623 |
| Total revenues | 5,796,653 | **7,307,830** | 2,881,904 |
| Net income | 1,372,749 | **1,904,260** | 786,601 |
| JO revenue as % of consolidated revenue | 51.8% | **57.9%** | 55.5% |
| JO net income as % of consolidated profit | 59.0% | **67.6%** | 68.0% |

**INFERENCE.** (a) The JOs are **91% liability-funded** (net assets 8.7% of assets) — they are
structurally thin-equity vehicles, which is exactly why the partner receivable exists. (b) At the
100% level the JOs now generate **more than two-thirds of group profit**, so the undisclosed
profit-sharing percentages are the largest unquantified variable in CTRA's economics.
**Insufficient evidence — requires further research.** (c) **JO revenue is deteriorating roughly
twice as fast as the group**: H1-2026 annualised Rp 5,764bn vs FY2025 Rp 7,308bn = **−21.1%**,
against consolidated revenue −11.7%.

### Non-controlling interests

**FACT.**

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1-2026 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Profit attributable to NCI | 201,680 | 154,489 | 59,300 |
| NCI as % of total profit | 8.7% | **5.5%** | 5.1% |
| NCI balance (equity) | 2,753,955 | 2,849,905 | 2,878,949 |
| NCI as % of total equity | 11.2% | **10.6%** | 10.5% |
| Dividends paid to NCI | 39,910 | **92,976** | 30,280 |

**INFERENCE.** NCI holds **10.6% of consolidated equity but earns only 5.5% of consolidated
profit** — an implied return on NCI capital of ~5.5% against the parent's ~11.6% ROE. The minority
capital inside the group is sitting in project SPVs that are not yet earning. Two consequences:
(i) consolidated assets and consolidated cash are ~10.6% not the parent's, and (ii) **NCI is being
paid out faster than the parent** — a 60% payout of NCI earnings in FY2025 versus the parent's
20.9%. Cash is leaving the group through the minority door at a disproportionate rate.

**FACT.** Parent ROE FY2025 = 2,663,100 / average parent equity 22,965,103 = **11.6%**. Group ROE on
total equity = **10.9%**. The screener's 11.07% TTM ROE is in the right place.

**FACT — cash is not restricted.** Note 5: *"As of December 31, 2025 and 2024, **no cash and cash
equivalents are used as collateral for obligations or restricted in use**."* Restricted funds
(Rp 922,464) are separately classified in other non-current assets, and relate to collateral for

**INFERENCE (caveat, honestly stated).** The disclosure establishes that the cash is not
*contractually* restricted. It does **not** establish how much sits in partly-owned SPVs or in JO
bank accounts requiring partner/lender consent to upstream. Given NCI is 10.6% of equity and JOs
hold Rp 10.6tn of assets, a haircut of roughly that order to "parent-available cash" is prudent.
**Insufficient evidence for a precise figure — requires further research.**

**FACT — maturity profile is now benign.** The SGD 150,000,000 6% Notes 2026 (Fitch BB-, rating
lapsed Sept-2024) **matured and were fully repaid on 2 February 2026** (Note 44), and the associated
Deutsche Bank call-spread settled the same day. At Jun-2026 only **Rp 1,002,836 of debt falls due
within twelve months, against Rp 8,602,401 of cash — 8.6x covered.** Weighted-average effective bank
rates 6.85%–8.25%. The Dec-2025 ladder shows Rp 1,151,621 (1–2y), Rp 1,944,795 (3–5y),
Rp 2,698,637 (>5y).

**FACT — FX risk is now eliminated.** Rp 172,385 of FY2025 finance cost was FX loss on the SGD
notes (FY2024: Rp 199,802). Post-repayment all debt is IDR; H1-2026 recorded a Rp 23,446 FX *gain*
instead. This is a genuine, recurring, ~Rp 150–200bn/yr improvement to reported finance costs from
FY2026 onward that has nothing to do with operations.

**FACT — interest coverage, computed from audited lines (not the screener's 45.4x artifact):**

| Basis | FY2025 | H1-2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Operating profit / contractual interest (bank + notes) | 3,833.8 / 562.6 = **6.8x** | 1,507.1 / 248.8 = **6.1x** |
| Operating profit / total reported finance costs | 3,833.8 / 1,196.7 = **3.2x** | 1,507.1 / 398.6 = **3.8x** |

Adequate on both. Not "bulletproof." Agent 5's 6.4–6.5x estimate is confirmed as the right order.

evidence — requires further research.**

**FACT — I found no disclosed guarantee given by CTRA in favour of JO partners.** The economic
exposure to partners is the Rp 1,566bn receivable, plus Rp 9,169bn of JO liabilities (100% basis)
sitting inside project structures whose recourse terms are not disclosed.
**Insufficient evidence — requires further research.**

**FACT — related-party dealing is immaterial.** Due from related parties Rp 22,898 (0.05% of
assets); due to related parties Rp 20,042. Non-interest-bearing, unsecured, demandable. The only
related-party revenue in FY2025 was Rp 54,039 of office sales to PT Ciputra Internasional (0.43% of
revenue); no related-party purchases. **This substantially answers the governance concern raised by
Agent 5: I found no evidence of value transfer to family vehicles.** Tax disputes disclosed are
trivial (Rp 2,642 and Rp 1,842, both already paid).

## 8. Capital allocation

**FACT — dividend.** AGMS 26 June 2026 approved **Rp 36/share, Rp 667,285 total, 25.1% payout** of
FY2025 net income. Cum-date 6 July, payment **24 July 2026**. Prior year: Rp 24/share, Rp 444,857
(20.9% payout). **The dividend was raised 50% per share and 50% in total.**

**FACT — reconciled independently from the statements.** Unappropriated retained earnings
15,834,437 + H1-2026 profit 1,096,964 − 1,000 appropriation − 16,263,116 closing = **667,285
declared**, unpaid at 30 June (other payables jumped Rp 944,865 in the half).

**FACT — the coverage arithmetic:**

- FY2025 dividend declared Rp 667,285 vs **FY2025 CFO Rp 79,382 = 8.4x CFO**.
- FY2025 free cash flow (CFO − capex Rp 721,414) was **−Rp 642,032**; the dividend is **not covered
  at all** on FY2025 cash generation.
- FY2025 cash dividends actually paid (Rp 444,857 parent + Rp 92,976 NCI = Rp 537,833) were paid in
  a year in which the group **drew Rp 3,854,322 of new bank loans against Rp 2,136,682 of
  repayments — net new borrowing of Rp 1,717,640**.

**THESIS.** The FY2025 dividend was funded by the balance sheet, and the board then raised it 50%.
That is a deliberate signal of confidence — or a refusal to acknowledge the cash-flow signal. I
cannot distinguish the two from the filings. It is the clearest capital-allocation red flag in the
file, and it is *not* mitigated by the net-cash position, because net cash exists only because the
company borrowed to preserve it.

**FACT — there is no buyback.** Shares issued and fully paid are **18,535,695,255 at Dec-2024,
Dec-2025 and Jun-2026 — unchanged**. There is no treasury stock line on any balance sheet. There is
no share-repurchase line in financing activities in FY2025 or H1-2026. The only treasury reference
is a historical Rp 27,196 debit in additional paid-in capital from a past capital reduction.

**FACT — a group affiliate did buy back, and CTRA did not.** PT Jaya Real Property (JRPT, Ciputra
group) announced a Rp 100bn buyback running 4 June 2026 – 4 June 2027. CTRA's own AGMS coverage
records no buyback resolution.

**NOTE ON A CLAIM I COULD NOT VERIFY.** Aggregated web-search summaries assert a "Rp 4 trillion CTRA
buyback." I found **no primary source**, no AGMS resolution, and no supporting entry in the
financial statements; Rp 4tn would be 34% of market capitalisation, which is not credible.
**Treat as unverified and most likely a search-summarisation artefact.** Anyone repeating it must
source it to an IDX disclosure first.

**THESIS.** At **0.48x book**, with a disclosed appraisal surplus on land alone of ~Rp 17.3tn, a
buyback would be the single highest-return use of capital available to CTRA — mathematically
superior to buying land at market prices. The board instead raised the cash dividend 50% while
operating cash flow was Rp 79bn. **INFERENCE:** this is consistent with a controlling family that
prefers cash distributions (which it receives 53.31% of) to per-share value accretion (which it
already owns). It is not abusive. It does mean the discount to book has no corporate mechanism
working to close it — which is precisely Agent 5's structural objection, now corroborated from the
capital-allocation record rather than from ownership theory.

## 9. Accounting Concerns

1. **THESIS — revenue timing versus cash timing is the whole story, and it is legitimate but
   dangerous.** Revenue recognised on handover, cash collected on presales. Nothing improper; but it
   means the P&L reports a business 24 months in the past and will keep flattering CTRA for one to
   two more years before it stops. An investor reading the P&L alone is reading history.
2. **FACT — Rp 561,121 (9.4% of FY2025 gross profit, 14.6% of operating profit) of "revenue" is
   imputed PSAK 115 interest with no cost of sales**, and it is contracting as the advance book
   contracts. Disclosed, correct, and almost certainly not stripped out by anyone's model.
3. **FACT — capitalised borrowing cost into inventory is permitted by policy but never quantified.**
   Inferred at ~Rp 138bn for FY2025 (nil FY2024). Immaterial in size; a disclosure gap nonetheless.
4. **FACT — the JO profit-sharing percentages are undisclosed**, while the JOs at 100% generate
   ~68% of group profit. This is the largest unquantified economic variable in the company.
5. **FACT — Rp 1,566bn of interest-free JO partner receivables carry no expected credit loss
   allowance whatsoever**, and grew 19.5% over eighteen months of falling presales.
6. **FACT — no inventory or land impairment has ever been recorded**, on Rp 23.1tn of real-estate
   assets held at cost, in a market where volumes below Rp 2bn fell 22% YoY. A land-cost basis is
   genuinely conservative in Indonesia; but a zero-impairment record across a downturn is a
   statement of management judgement, not an observation.
7. **INFERENCE — presentational framing in the FY2025 annual report.** The Directors' Report
   highlights "a cash flow surplus of Rp 171 billion" and a "net cash position of Rp 528 billion."
   Both are true. Neither mentions that operating cash flow was Rp 79bn and that the Rp 171bn
   surplus exists only because financing activities contributed Rp 1,215bn. The MD&A does disclose
   the CFO decline fully and accurately three sections later. This is emphasis, not misstatement —
   but it tells you what management wants read first.

## Key Positive Findings

1. **The balance sheet survives full inspection.** Net cash Rp 662bn; gross debt/equity 32.4%; only
   Rp 1,003bn due within twelve months against Rp 8,602bn of cash (8.6x); interest cover 6.8x on

   and a ~Rp 170–200bn/yr recurring FX drag; unqualified Deloitte-affiliate audit opinion;

2. **The cash collapse is a one-year, half-year-specific event with a working management response.**
   Four-year cumulative CFO/net income is 126%. H2-2025 was the entire problem (CFO −Rp 659bn);

   purchases ~90%. Management has demonstrated it can defend cash, and it did so before it was
   forced to.
3. **A large, independently appraised, unrecognised asset backs the 0.48x book.** Certain land for

## Key Negative Findings

1. **FY2025 converted Rp 2,663bn of net income into Rp 79bn of operating cash — 3.0% cash
   conversion — and the cause is collections, not construction.** Receipts from customers fell
   Rp 1,748bn (60% of the Rp 2,906bn swing) while contractor payments rose only Rp 738bn (25%), and
   this happened *despite* CTRA cutting total land acquisition roughly in half. Reported revenue
   exceeded cash receipts by Rp 3,503bn, matched almost exactly by a Rp 3,190bn drawdown of the
   customer-advance book. **Roughly a quarter of FY2025 revenue was cash collected in 2022–2024.**
2. **The contracted backlog has fallen 35.5% in eighteen months and forward revenue cover has gone
   from ~12 months to ~7.** Contract liabilities Rp 11,019bn → Rp 7,828bn → Rp 7,104bn. This is the
   audited, contractual confirmation of the presales decline, and it makes a second consecutive
   revenue decline in FY2027 close to arithmetically locked. Compounding it, the PSAK 115 financing
   component — a zero-cost revenue line worth 9.4% of gross profit — shrinks in lockstep.
3. **Capital allocation is pointed the wrong way at every level.** The FY2025 dividend was raised
   50% to Rp 667bn — **8.4x FY2025 operating cash flow**, against negative free cash flow — while
   the group drew net Rp 1,718bn of new bank debt; there is **no buyback** at 0.48x book despite a
   Rp 17.3tn appraisal surplus and a group affiliate launching one; **Rp 1,566bn of interest-free,
   un-provisioned capital is lent to JO partners and grew 19.5% into the downturn**; and NCI capital
   earning a 5.5% return is being paid out at a 60% ratio versus the parent's 20.9%.

## Potential Screener False Positives

1. **Interest Coverage 45.4x and everything that leans on it.** Yahoo's Q4-2025 interest expense is
   a derived plug of **−331.6** (negative). True coverage from audited lines is **6.8x** on
   contractual interest and **3.2x** on total finance costs. Safety Score 95.6 and Altman Z 4.99
   both inherit the artifact. *Fix: reject any quarterly interest expense ≤ 0 and fall back to the
   annual line.*
2. **Days Inventory 706 and Cash Conversion Cycle 662 are understated ~2.3x.** The screener's
   `Inventory` field is the current-asset line only; it omits **Rp 10,128bn of "Land for
   development"** classified as non-current. True real-estate holding period is **~1,590 days**.
   *Fix: for Properties & Real Estate, add non-current development land to the inventory base.*
3. **EBIT, Normalised EBIT, EV/EBIT, Acquirer's Multiple and EPV all embed non-operating imputed
   interest.** Rp 561,121 of FY2025 revenue (14.6% of operating profit) is the PSAK 115 significant
   financing component — no COGS, no cash, and shrinking with the advance book. Ex-item FY2025
   operating profit is Rp 3,272.7bn, not Rp 3,833.8bn. Capitalising the unadjusted figure into
   perpetuity (EPV Rp 1,228/share) capitalises an accounting accretion as if it were earnings power.
4. **Net Debt −662 is credited 100% to the parent.** NCI is 10.6% of consolidated equity and the JOs
   hold Rp 10.6tn of assets in 91%-liability-funded structures; Rp 1,566bn of group capital is
   already out the door financing partners. The *net cash* conclusion survives; the *magnitude
   attributable to CTRA shareholders* does not.
5. **The missing 2025-09-30 quarter corrupts every YoY growth field** (confirming Agent 5). The
   quarterly series runs Q1-25, Q2-25, Q4-25, Q1-26, Q2-26; a four-column step-back lands on Q1-25.
   Reported revenue YoY −3.48% is Q2-26 vs Q1-25; true Q2-26 vs Q2-25 is **−16.3%**.
6. **Credit where due — one screener signal caught this correctly.** Piotroski `Accruals = 0`
   (CFO < net income) is the single field in the entire fact sheet that flagged the FY2025 earnings
   quality problem, and it was outvoted 7-to-2 inside an F-Score of 7. *Fix: treat CFO/NI below ~50%
   as a hard verdict gate for cyclicals, not as one point of nine.*

## Financial Verdict: **ACCEPTABLE**

Not STRONG, and not QUESTIONABLE. I want to be precise about why, because the two failure modes here
pull in opposite directions.

**Why not QUESTIONABLE.** I went looking for aggressive accounting and did not find it. The audit is
by a Deloitte member firm with an unqualified opinion. Related-party dealing is 0.05% of assets.
Cash is explicitly unrestricted. Inventory is at cost with a disclosed independent appraisal showing
a Rp 17.3tn surplus, not a deficit. The JO disclosure — 47 vehicles with full 100% assets,
liabilities, revenue and profit — is more transparent than most IDX developers provide. Crucially,
**the company disclosed the two facts most damaging to its own narrative in plain language**: the
Rp 561,121 imputed-interest component of revenue, and the 97.3% collapse in operating cash flow with
a complete variance bridge. Companies that are hiding things do not do that.

**Why not STRONG.** A developer that converts 3% of net income into operating cash, whose contracted
backlog has fallen 35.5% in eighteen months, that raised its dividend 50% to 8.4x operating cash
flow while drawing Rp 1.7tn of net new debt, and that has lent Rp 1,566bn interest-free and
un-provisioned to partners whose land is its only collateral, is not a strong financial profile.

**What this means for the committee.** The accounting is trustworthy; the *business the accounting
describes* is deteriorating faster than the P&L admits, and the filings say so if you read the cash
flow statement and Note 21. **I do not find the screener's BUY defensible on financial-quality
grounds, and I confirm Agent 5's condition #4 as satisfied on cash and related parties but
unsatisfied on JO economics** (the profit-sharing percentages are undisclosed and the partner
receivable is unprovisioned).

**Specific escalation trigger.** If FY2026 full-year CFO comes in below ~Rp 1,000bn — i.e. below
1.5x the Rp 667bn dividend — this verdict moves to **QUESTIONABLE**, because a second consecutive
year of dividends funded from the balance sheet stops being a timing artefact and becomes a policy.
H1-2026 CFO of Rp 807.7bn is on track to clear that bar, but only because construction spend was cut
14%, which defers FY2027–28 handovers. Watch the H2-2026 contractor payment line as closely as the
CFO line.

**Open items I could not resolve — flagged for whoever underwrites this:**
- JO profit-sharing percentages (Note 42j says "a certain percentage" and stops).
- Gross KPR/KPA/KPO buy-back guarantee exposure (only the Rp 922bn of pledged restricted funds is
  disclosed).
- Whether CTRA guarantees any JO or partner borrowings.
- How much of the Rp 8,602bn consolidated cash is trapped at SPV/JO level requiring partner or
  lender consent to upstream.
- The exact borrowing cost capitalised into inventory (inferred ~Rp 138bn FY2025; not disclosed).
