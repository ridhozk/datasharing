## 1. Price path — what drove 147→685→464

**FACT — a real shooting war, not a market rumor, is the dominant driver.** US/Israeli
military operations against Iran began **late February 2026**, with Iran retaliating by
threatening and attacking commercial shipping in the **Strait of Hormuz**. A ceasefire was
reached ~April 2026, a US-Iran memorandum of understanding was signed **17 June 2026**, but
the conflict **resumed in July 2026** with Iranian attacks on tankers deemed non-compliant.
As of **23 August 2026, Hormuz traffic remained ~95% below pre-war levels** (~5 vessels/day
vs ~100/day pre-war); Gulf crude exports down ~47% (17mbd → ~9mbd); oil prices ~20% above
pre-war levels (down from an April spike above $130/bbl).
[Britannica: 2026 Iran war](https://www.britannica.com/event/2026-Iran-war);
[Al Jazeera 9 Jul 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/9/strait-of-hormuz-what-has-happened-since-the-us-iran-mou-on-june-17);
[Al Jazeera 27 Aug 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/27/how-a-95-percent-drop-in-hormuz-traffic-changed-global-shipping).

**FACT — global tanker freight rates spiked to multi-decade highs on war-risk premiums.**
Platts: PG→China 270kt crude cargo rate up 82% in the ten days after the conflict began
(13→23 June 2026); PG→Med 140kt cargo up 29% over the same window. Breakwave Advisors
(2 Sep 2026): "Middle East crude tanker rates reached multi-decade highs in March 2026."
As of 21 Apr 2026 spot levels cited elsewhere: VLCC ~$175k/day, Suezmax ~$92k/day, Aframax
~$68k/day, MR ~$37k/day — all rich versus historical norms. **This is a sector-wide
re-rating, not a BULL-specific event** — every tanker owner globally re-rated on the same
news, and BULL is a beneficiary by virtue of being a tanker owner, not because of anything
company-specific in this leg of the move.

## 3. Tanker rate cycle, 2026

**FACT — this has been an extraordinary, war-driven year for tanker rates, and the cycle is
now showing early signs of turning from acute-shock strength toward supply-driven softening.**

- **The shock:** US-Israel-Iran war from late Feb 2026 drove Middle East crude tanker rates
  to **multi-decade highs in March 2026** (Breakwave Advisors, 2 Sep 2026). Spot-rate
  snapshot from 21 April 2026: **VLCC ~$175k/day, Suezmax ~$92k/day, Aframax ~$68k/day,
  MR ~$37k/day** — all well above normal-cycle levels (source: search-aggregated secondary
  data, **not independently verified against Baltic Exchange/Clarksons primary data** —
  treat as approximate).
- **Early 2026 whipsaw before the war:** VLCC Persian Gulf→Asia rates had fallen 43% from
  November 2025 to January 2026 on soft seasonal demand, then Aframax rates hit multi-year
  highs in January 2026 even before the war began (Kpler, 6 Feb 2026) — i.e., mid-size
  tankers were already tight on shadow-fleet/sanctions dynamics prior to the Hormuz shock.
- **Asset values have re-rated hard:** VLCC secondhand values up YoY by ~30% (5-year-old),
  ~42% (10-year), ~61% (15-year), ~90% (20-year-old) tonnage (Breakwave, 2 Sep 2026) — a
  classic late-cycle signal when the oldest, least efficient tonnage rallies hardest.
- **Orderbook has swollen materially — the clearest headwind for 2027-2029 rates.** Tanker
  orderbook reached **1,267 vessels / 173.32m dwt by July 2026, ≈25.0% of the existing
  fleet** — a large order-to-fleet ratio by historical tanker standards, driven mainly by
  **120 net new VLCC orders**. Active fleet grew 167 vessels (17.1m dwt) in H1 2026 alone.
  **THESIS:** an orderbook this size, ordered *into* a war-risk price spike, is the textbook
  set-up for owners overpaying for newbuilds at cycle-peak asset values — precisely the
  capital-discipline risk the owner's framework flags for BULL's own capex programme (fact
  sheet §5). If the Hormuz disruption resolves and rates normalise while this orderbook
  delivers from 2027, owners who bought yards' slots in mid-2026 could face a rate/asset-value
  reversal.
- **Sanctions / shadow fleet / OPEC+:** Kpler's Q1-2026 outlook (6 Feb 2026) is titled
  "Mid-Size Surge as Shadow Fleet Unravels" — **INFERENCE from title alone (full text not
  fetched)**: enforcement action against the Russia/Iran "shadow fleet" was tightening
  through early 2026, pushing displaced legitimate demand onto compliant mid-size tonnage
  (Aframax/MR), which supports BULL's fleet mix if its vessels are compliant/mainstream.
  Separately, redirected flows post-Hormuz-disruption have benefited Southeast Asian
  transshipment — Russian fuel oil shipments to Singapore/Malaysia rose 2.5x month-on-month
  in July 2026 (Al Jazeera, 27 Aug 2026) — a regional ton-mile tailwind, though not
  necessarily captured by BULL specifically (its charters are believed Pertamina-linked, not
  spot/shadow trade — **ASSUMPTION**, pending confirmation in §5).
- **Turning up or rolling over? Mixed — a genuine two-sided read, not a clean trend.**
  The acute war-risk premium (rates, oil price) had already begun normalising by late
  August 2026 (oil ~20% above pre-war vs >$130/bbl April peak; a workaround route restored
  some Hormuz flow). At the same time, an industry source warned inventories could tighten
  further over the following six months if the situation doesn't improve, and the swollen
  orderbook is a 2027+ overhang regardless of the near-term rate level. **THESIS:** the
  fairest single-line summary is *tanker rates are elevated versus history but past their
  acute 2026 peak, with a large orderbook now the dominant medium-term risk* — which cuts
  directly against reading BULL's Q1-2026 37% operating margin as the new sustainable
  run-rate (echoing the fact sheet's own bear case).

## 4. Indonesian macro as it bears on BULL specifically

**FACT — BI policy rate is steady, not a driver either way.** Bank Indonesia held the
BI-Rate at **5.75%** at its 18-19 August 2026 meeting (Deposit Facility 4.75%, Lending
Facility 6.50%); Bank Mandiri (3 Sep 2026 note) projects it stays at 5.75% through
year-end. A new BI Governor, **Destry Damayanti**, was confirmed around end-August 2026,
pledging closer government coordination "without compromising independence."
[IDNFinancials](https://www.idnfinancials.com/id/news/59695/bank-indonesia-beri-sinyal-pemangkasan-bi-rate-2026),
[Kontan](https://nasional.kontan.co.id/news/prediksi-bi-rate-2026-suku-bunga-bisa-turun-50-bps).
A stable-to-lower rate path is mildly supportive of BULL's vessel-debt refinancing economics
(interest expense already falling: 61.4bn → 38.2bn IDR/quarter per the fact sheet) but is a
second-order factor next to FX and charter rates for this name.

**FACT — the rupiah is broadly stable-to-firm, which is a modest headwind for BULL's
reported IDR earnings, not a tailwind (the reverse of most names in this review).** IDR
traded ~17,750/USD on 1 September 2026, after posting its **first monthly gain in six
months in August (+~1.4%)**; July inflation hit a 3-month low of 2.88%. Dollar strength
from a hawkish Fed (Chair Kevin Warsh) is a live offsetting risk.
[Search-aggregated, early-Sep 2026 reporting — treat point levels as approximate].
Per the fact sheet, BULL's statement currency is USD; a **firming** rupiah (17,908 →
17,684 over three weeks per the fact sheet) mechanically **shrinks** reported IDR revenue
and EBIT even if USD economics are unchanged — the opposite of the FX tailwind bull
narratives sometimes assume for exporters. **This cuts against, not for, the bull case
on any IDR-denominated multiple**, and the owner's framework note in the fact sheet
(never smuggle FX moves in as operating performance) applies directly here.

**FACT — Indonesia's crude/product import dependency remains structural, which is the
correct macro tailwind for ton-mile demand into Pertamina's system.** Indonesian domestic
crude output is ~570-610kbd against consumption of ~1.4-1.7mbd (mid-2026) — a gap of
roughly 1mbd that must be imported as crude or refined product.
[GK Group](https://gankapital.com/article/indonesias-oil-situation-in-2026-strained-production-and-a-deepening-import-dependence/).
**RDMP Balikpapan** (Refinery Development Master Plan), inaugurated by President Prabowo
Subianto on **12 January 2026**, is Indonesia's first major refinery revitalisation in 32
years; it is expected to end diesel imports by mid-2026 and cut ~IDR 68tn/year in fuel
imports once fully ramped.
[The Southeast Asia Desk](https://www.thesoutheastasiadesk.com/p/indonesias-rdmp-balikpapan-a-strategic-move).
**THESIS — two-sided for BULL:** less *refined-product* import volume (diesel, gasoline)
plausibly reduces some product-tanker cargo, but the refinery still needs a larger *crude*
feedstock intake to run at higher utilisation, and Indonesia's consumption growth continues
to outrun production regardless — net effect on BULL's specific charter volumes is
**ambiguous without knowing whether its fleet mix is crude carriers, product tankers, or
both** (not established in this research — see fact sheet gap #1, fleet composition).
**Insufficient evidence to sign this sub-factor with confidence; flagged, not resolved.**

**FACT — the cabotage principle (asas cabotage) is unchanged and continues to reserve
domestic inter-island shipping for Indonesian-flagged vessels with Indonesian crews**
(Law 17/2008 on Shipping, as amended by Perppu 2/2022; originally Presidential Instruction
5/2005). No 2026 policy change to this regime was found. This is a **structural, ongoing
tailwind** for Indonesian-flag incumbents like BULL versus foreign-flag competition on
domestic legs, though it does not protect BULL from *other Indonesian* owners (including
PIS's own fleet — see §5) competing for the same Pertamina charters.

## 5. Pertamina risk — PIS fleet build-out / in-sourcing

**This is the single most important structural risk to the bull case, and the evidence
found makes it concrete, not hypothetical.**

**FACT — revenue concentration on Pertamina is extreme and confirmed, not merely
"believed."** Pertamina/Pertamina-linked entities generated ~83% of BULL's revenue in a
recent disclosed period (Pertamina cited as BULL's largest customer at ~USD 74.6m of
services), with the remainder split across Epson, PetroChina and ChemChina; **BULL's own
director has stated a target to keep Pertamina-group contracts at a minimum of 85% of
revenue** — i.e., management is not diversifying away from this dependency, it is
targeting to sustain or deepen it. Source: search-aggregated secondary reporting citing
company disclosure; exact period not confirmed — treat the 83%/85% figures as
**directionally FACT, precisely dated as INFERENCE** pending a primary-source cite.

**FACT — Pertamina International Shipping's stated long-term fleet ambition is huge and
partly, but not wholly, an in-sourcing threat.** PIS director Eka Suhendra has stated an
ambition for **200 self-owned ships plus 300 on charter** — i.e., PIS's own long-term plan
still envisions the *majority* of its fleet (300 of 500) remaining on charter from
owner-operators, not proprietarily owned. Separately, PIS ordered **15 MR tankers
(~USD 700m+) from HD Korea Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering in early 2024**, deliveries
running through November 2026, and **two 23,000-m3 dual-fuel LPG carriers from HD Hyundai
Weipu** (delivery by October 2026). PIS currently reports 409 vessels including 21 tankers
and 9 gas carriers (one source), or 74 vessels/2.76m DWT (another, likely a narrower fleet
category) — sources conflict, **flagged as unreconciled**.
[TradeWinds](https://www.tradewindsnews.com/tankers/pertamina-international-shipping-targets-fleet-of-500-tankers-and-gas-carriers-within-a-decade/2-1-1706865),
[S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/crude-oil/070626-interview-pertamina-shipping-arm-prioritizes-diversified-fleet-in-global-push).
**THESIS:** the "200 owned / 300 chartered" framing means PIS's own newbuild programme
(the 15 MR tankers delivering through Nov 2026 in particular) is a **direct, dated,
near-term in-sourcing threat specifically in the MR product-tanker segment** — the segment
most likely to overlap with BULL's own fleet — even though PIS's *aggregate* strategy still
leaves room for third-party charter owners. Whether BULL's specific vessels compete
head-to-head with those 15 incoming PIS MRs is **not established — requires knowing BULL's
fleet composition by vessel class**, a gap already flagged in the fact sheet (§1, "Fleet
NAV").

**FACT — Pertamina's own house has been in serious disorder, which cuts both ways for a
chartered-in supplier like BULL.** The **2025 Pertamina corruption case**: fuel-blending
fraud at subsidiary Pertamina Patra Niaga (2018-2023), ~IDR 193.7 trillion (~USD 12bn)
estimated loss, arrests beginning 25 February 2025 expanding to 18 suspects by July 2025,
sentences of 9-15 years handed down by February 2026. **Critically, PIS's own CEO, Yoki
Firnandi, was implicated/arrested in this case**, and maritime-adjacent entities (PT
Navigator Khatulistiwa, PT Jenggala Maritim, PT Orbit Terminal Merak) appear in the case's
web of commissioner appointments.
[Wikipedia: 2025 Pertamina corruption case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Pertamina_corruption_case).
**THESIS:** leadership turnover and scrutiny at PIS itself plausibly (a) slows PIS's own
newbuild in-sourcing execution and decision-making in the near term (a mild offset to the
in-sourcing risk above), but (b) also raises the odds of **procurement-process reform,
re-tendering, or heightened compliance scrutiny** on all Pertamina-group vendors —
including BULL — which is a source of **event risk to contract renewal timing and terms**
that is impossible to size from public information. **Insufficient evidence to determine
whether this materially threatens BULL's specific charter book — flagged as an open,
material risk, not quantified.**

**FACT — BULL has a documented history of exactly this kind of procurement risk
materialising.** BULL was **blacklisted by Pertamina from procurement on 12 March 2018**
after two of its Large Ranger crude tankers (MT Bull Sulawesi, MT Bull Flores — a third,
MT Bull Papua, also named) were **detained by customs authorities**, a breach of contract
terms; Pertamina's own spokesperson confirmed blacklisted vendors cannot participate in
future procurement at all.
[Jawa Pos](https://www.jawapos.com/ekonomi/0197879/buana-lintas-lautan-terkena-blacklist-pertamina-dalam-pengadaan-barang).
**This is old (2018) and evidently resolved** — the company has since grown its Pertamina
revenue share to ~83-85%, so the blacklist was clearly lifted or was narrower/shorter than
first reported — but it establishes that **customer concentration risk with this specific
counterparty is not merely theoretical for BULL; it has a precedent of actually costing the
company its principal customer relationship once before.** No evidence of a repeat episode
was found in 2025-2026 search results.
