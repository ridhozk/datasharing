# INDY — Thesis Killer / Skeptical Investment Committee Review

**Scoring convention: higher Skeptic Score = MORE confidence the bull case survives scrutiny (i.e., higher = less concern / more approve-leaning). A low score means the thesis is fragile.**

*First pass, written after ~10 research calls per project discipline. Gaps marked "Insufficient evidence — requires further research" are real gaps, not filler, and should be closed before this name is sized in a portfolio.*

## 1. What the bull SOTP case must be claiming

The seductive version of the INDY bull case: 91% of Kideco (FACT — Indika acquired the remaining 45% from Samtan/Muji Inti Utama in Dec 2017, bringing its stake to 91%, Samtan retains 9%) is a cash-generative, low-cost thermal coal producer, consolidated into a holding company trading at 0.65x book (screener FACT). If Kideco alone is worth what recent deal chatter suggests, the market is pricing the rest of the group — coal services, logistics, and the green/EV/nickel/digital "ventures" — at approximately zero or negative, which the bull calls a free option.

## 2. Priority 1 — attacking the SOTP

### 2a. Has the discount to SOTP ever closed?
**INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE — requires further research.** I could not obtain a reliable multi-year P/B or EV/EBITDA time series for INDY from available web sources in this pass (search results returned only current-quarter snapshots, one of which — Investing.com's P/B ≈ 1 — actually conflicts with the screener's 0.65x, likely due to different equity-base or timing assumptions). What I can establish directly from the fact sheet: **10Y price percentile is 53.6%** (today's price sits almost exactly in the middle of its own 10-year range) and **10Y max drawdown is −85.7%** (FACT, from the screener data). A stock that has drawn down 86% from its own decade high and currently sits at the 54th percentile of its own range is not "cheap relative to itself" in any simple sense — it is a name whose per-share value has been repeatedly and violently re-rated, most plausibly tracking the coal price cycle, not a steady-state discount that compresses and expands around a stable book value. **INFERENCE:** the pattern is far more consistent with cyclical earnings volatility (net income fell from IDR 8.1tn in 2022 to IDR 0.108tn in 2025 — a 98.7% collapse, FACT from the fact sheet's annual history) driving price than with a persistent, closable holdco discount. Without the actual P/B time series I cannot confirm whether the discount is structural, but the burden of proof is on the bull case to show it, and the raw materials to check this were not obtained in this pass — flag for a dedicated valuation-history pull (company annual reports 2018–2025, or a terminal).

### 2b. Are the ventures worth their carrying value, or is that just spent capital?
This is where the first-pass research produced the single most damaging data point. **FACT** (from Indika's own 3M26 news release, per search-result summary): in Q1 2026, Kideco alone generated revenue of US$377.4M (72.8% of consolidated revenue of US$493.2M) and **net profit of US$42.4M**, while **Indika's consolidated net profit for the same quarter was just US$7M**. Arithmetic makes the conclusion unavoidable: **everything that is not Kideco — coal services, logistics, green/EV/nickel/digital ventures, and corporate holding-company costs (including interest on parent-level debt) — destroyed on the order of US$35M of value in a single quarter**, against Kideco's contribution. This is not a one-off: full-year 2025 net income was only IDR 107.9bn (~US$6M) against a >IDR 2.7tn EBITDA base and Kideco's own historically strong profitability, and net income CAGR-3Y is −76.3% while revenue CAGR-3Y is −22.3% — profit collapsing much faster than revenue, which is the signature of costs (including venture losses) rising or staying sticky while the core business shrinks.

**THESIS:** the "free option" in the ventures is not free — Kideco's cash is being spent to fund businesses that, on the one quarter of segment-level evidence available, are collectively unprofitable enough to erase most of the parent's consolidated profit even in a quarter where the coal business performed well. A pre-revenue or early-revenue nickel/EV venture is worth what it can be shown to earn or what a third party will pay for it, not the capital Indika has already sunk into it — and the evidence here points to that capital currently earning a return closer to zero or negative, not to it being an underpriced call option.

### 2c. Is there a mechanism to realise the parts?
**FACT:** Indika has appointed financial advisers to evaluate strategic options for Kideco, including a potential sale, with deal-press reports (Bloomberg Technoz, Stockbit/Petromindo, IDN Financials) citing a valuation "more than US$1 billion." Management has explicitly declined to comment on the reports (a standard non-denial, not a confirmation). CreditSights is reported to have suggested a partial (20–25%) minority-stake sale as more likely than a full divestment, which would let Indika retain control of Kideco.

This is the one piece of real evidence that a realisation mechanism might exist — SOTP is not purely an accounting exercise here if a transaction actually closes. But three qualifiers matter for the committee:
1. **This is process chatter, not a signed transaction.** Advisers being appointed and "exploring options" routinely produces headline valuations well above what actually clears in a subsequent sale, especially in a sector (thermal coal) with a shrinking buyer pool.
2. **Even taking the >$1B figure at face value**, it is unclear from available reporting whether it refers to 100% of Kideco, Indika's 91% stake, or an enterprise value including Kideco's own debt — each produces a materially different read-through to per-share value for INDY.
3. **If Kideco alone is worth >$1B against a total group EV of only ~$1.42B** (market cap ~US$778.6M + net debt ~US$645.3M, both computed from the fact sheet's IDR figures at FX 17,908), the market is implicitly assigning close to zero — or negative — value to the non-Kideco businesses net of parent-level debt and overhead. Section 2b's Q1 2026 numbers show why: those businesses are currently a profit drag, not dead weight sitting at zero. A rational market can hold both views (Kideco worth $1B+, group worth $778M) simultaneously precisely because the "rest of the group" is a demonstrated cash sink, not an idle asset.

**ASSUMPTION flagged for the bull case to defend:** that proceeds from any Kideco stake sale would be returned to shareholders or used to delever, rather than redeployed into the same ventures that are currently losing money. Management's capex pattern argues against optimism here — quarterly capex roughly quintupled from IDR −204.4bn (Q1 2025) to IDR −1,179.9bn (Q2 2026), a FACT from the fact sheet, at the same time FCF (TTM) is −IDR 831.0bn and FCF yield is −6.0%. Rising capex against negative and worsening free cash flow is consistent with continued investment in unproven ventures, not capital discipline in preparation for a return of capital.

### 2d. Conglomerate discount — is diversification destroying value?
Given 2b and the capex trend in 2c, the direct evidence available says **yes, provisionally**. This is exactly the pattern CLAUDE.md's own framing warns about: "taking cash from a declining but profitable coal business and burning it on ventures." The one quarter of segment math available (Kideco $42.4M profit vs. $7M consolidated) is a strong single data point but is **not multi-quarter confirmed** — insufficient evidence to state this as a settled multi-year trend rather than a single noisy quarter (coal prices, one-off venture losses, or FX could distort any single quarter). Recommend pulling at least 8 consecutive quarters of segment-level data before treating this as proven, but even as one data point it should worry anyone holding the SOTP thesis.

## 3. Priority 2 — the depleting asset problem

**FACT:** Kideco's original PKP2B (Coal Contract of Work) was set to expire March 13, 2023. Under Indonesia's revised mining law (UU Minerba, effective 2020), first-generation PKP2B holders were guaranteed automatic conversion to IUPK with two sequential 10-year extensions without area reduction. Indonesian press in the 2023 window (Indopremier) shows Indika was, at the time, awaiting the law's derivative regulations to confirm this treatment for Kideco specifically. The fact that Kideco is still operating, still the group's dominant profit engine, and reportedly attracting >$1B acquirer interest in 2026 is strong **INFERENCE** that the extension was granted (a lapsed concession would not command that valuation or advisor interest) — but I did not find a document that states the new expiry date explicitly. **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE — requires further research**: confirm the current IUPK term end-date directly from Indika's 2024/2025 annual report or the ESDM (Ministry of Energy) concession registry.

**On physical reserve life:** older public data (Global Energy Monitor / company profile sources, dated, possibly stale) cites proven reserves of ~531 million tonnes against ~30 million tonnes/year of production, implying a simple reserve-life arithmetic of roughly 17–18 years (i.e., depletion in the late 2030s/early 2040s) if reserves and production rate are both taken at face value. This is a back-of-envelope figure from a source of uncertain currency, not a reserve report — **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE — requires further research** to confirm against Indika's own JORC/SNI-compliant reserve disclosure in its latest annual report.

**THESIS-relevant point regardless of the exact year:** the underlying critique in the brief stands even with an extended concession. Every "cheap on EV/EBITDA" argument for INDY implicitly prices Kideco's cash flows as if they were a perpetuity. They are not — they are a multi-decade-but-finite annuity on a depleting reserve, subject additionally to global decarbonisation pressure on thermal coal demand and financing (see §4). A comps-based EV/EBIT of ~9.1x (screener FACT) or EV/EBITDA of ~8.4x applied to Kideco's current cash flow, without an explicit terminal decline/closure-cost adjustment, systematically overstates intrinsic value versus a proper depleting-asset DCF. This is exactly the kind of error the project's own hard-won-knowledge notes warn against ("never treat peak-cycle earnings as permanent earnings") — here the analogous error is treating a finite-life reserve's cash flow as a permanent one.

## 4. Supporting stress factors

**Debt and rate backdrop (FACT/INFERENCE):** Net debt/equity ~1.04x (screener FACT), interest coverage only 2.55x (screener FACT) — thin enough that a normal-sized swing in EBIT or coal prices could threaten covenant headroom (specific covenant terms **insufficient evidence — requires further research**). Indika's outstanding bond is a US$350M 8.75% note due 2029, rated BB-/Ba3 (sub-investment grade), refinanced/issued in 2024 with a 2024 notes' full repayment funded partly through a Barclays-type bridge facility structure per search results — the double-digit-adjacent coupon on that note is itself market evidence of how expensive coal-sector credit has become, consistent with general global lender retreat from thermal coal financing (**general market trend — INFERENCE, not confirmed specifically for INDY's bank lenders in this pass**). BI-Rate at 5.75% (up 100bp since May 2026, per the brief) raises the cost of any IDR-denominated refinancing and pressures IDR against the USD in which INDY's debt and financial statements are denominated — a currency mismatch that compounds refinancing risk even though the bonds are fixed-rate, because a weaker IDR raises the effective local-currency service burden and shrinks IDR-translated equity/book value (this is precisely the USD-reporting/IDR-trading mismatch flagged in the brief, and is handled correctly by the screener's `normalise_currency`, per CLAUDE.md — but the mismatch is a real economic risk independent of how the screener displays it).

**Minority interest absorption (FACT, partial):** Samtan holds 9% of Kideco directly, so 9% of Kideco's net profit is non-controlling and never reaches INDY shareholders even before the group-level drag documented in §2b. Full group-level minority interest as a share of consolidated net income before parent shareholders — **insufficient evidence — requires further research** (CLAUDE.md's own fact sheet flags that "net income is Yahoo's consolidated figure and may include minority interest" and that attributable profit needs filings-level verification; I could not complete that verification in this pass, though the one hard number obtained — Q1 2026 net profit attributable to owners of $0.48M / 9M25 vs $34.4M a year earlier — suggests the attributable figure is even worse than the consolidated one already shown in the fact sheet).

**Governance / related-party transactions:** **Insufficient evidence — requires further research.** Web search returned only Indika's own governance-page marketing language and generic OECD material on related-party risk in concentrated-ownership structures; no specific INDY related-party transaction, self-dealing allegation, or minority-oppression episode was found in this pass. Absence of evidence here is not evidence of absence — a targeted read of INDY's related-party-transaction footnotes in its 2024/2025 annual report is needed before this can be marked clear.

**Negative free cash flow (FACT):** FCF was negative in 2023 (−IDR 5.70tn) and 2024 (−IDR 2.86tn), barely positive in 2025 (+IDR 167.3bn), and negative again on a TTM basis (−IDR 831.0bn, FCF yield −6.0%) as of the latest quarter, driven by the capex ramp in §2c. There is no clearly disclosed, funded path back to sustainably positive FCF in the evidence gathered — this is exactly the setup CLAUDE.md's rules require flagging plainly rather than assuming a turnaround.

## 5. Value trap test — why has the market NOT recognised this?

Concrete answer, not a shrug: the market has almost certainly seen exactly what this review found — a strong, high-margin coal subsidiary bleeding value into unprofitable diversification, sitting on top of leverage that is expensive to refinance in a de-risking-from-coal credit environment, wrapped in a holding company whose reported consolidated profit ($7M) is a small fraction of what its core asset alone earns ($42.4M in the same quarter). That is not a market failing to notice hidden value — that is a market correctly pricing a going-concern holdco tax on top of a depleting, out-of-favor commodity asset. The 0.65x P/B is the price of (a) an ESG/financing discount rate premium for thermal coal, (b) genuine capital destruction in the ventures shown directly in the segment math, (c) finite reserve life not fully reflected in perpetuity-style comps multiples, and (d) minority-interest and holdco-cost leakage between Kideco's economics and what reaches INDY shareholders. Every one of these is a real, structural reason for a discount to persist, not a temporary mispricing.

## 6. Contrarian test — what would have to be true for the bulls to be right?

1. The Kideco stake-sale process would have to actually close, at or near the >$1B figure reported, on terms that let a meaningful portion of proceeds reach INDY shareholders (dividend/buyback) rather than being redeployed into the loss-making ventures.
2. The Q1 2026 segment gap ($42.4M Kideco profit vs. $7M consolidated) would need to prove to be a one-off (e.g., a specific impairment or FX loss) rather than a structural, recurring drag — this needs multi-quarter confirmation either way.
3. Kideco's reserve life and concession term would need to be long enough, and thermal coal prices resilient enough, that a perpetuity-style multiple is not a material overstatement versus a proper declining-annuity DCF.
4. Refinancing of the 2026/2027 debt maturities would need to clear at reasonable spreads despite sub-investment-grade ratings and a shrinking pool of coal-tolerant lenders.
5. Governance would need to hold up under a direct related-party-transaction review (not yet performed here).

None of these five is disproven by the evidence gathered — but none is confirmed either, and #1 and #2 in particular are exactly the kind of claims that "process chatter" and "one good quarter for the core asset" can manufacture without being true.
