# MOLI — Macro / News / Catalyst Agent

*Prepared 2026-08-11. Price IDR 274, market cap ~IDR 746bn.*

Macro Score: 52/100
Catalyst Score: 38/100

## Current Market Narrative

MOLI is being re-rated on H1 2026 results (released ~2026-08-11) showing net
profit +192% YoY on revenue +1.65% YoY, attributed by financial press to a
collapse in cost of goods sold rather than pricing power. Separately, the
company has publicly tied its FY2026 capex plan to Indonesia's incoming E5
bioethanol blending mandate, giving it a "biofuel play" narrative. [FACT,
sourced below] The two threads — the earnings beat and the mandate story —
are being reported together but are largely independent: the margin surge is
explained by an input-cost collapse (molasses), not by mandate-driven ethanol
demand or pricing, which has barely started. [INFERENCE]

## Recent Developments

1. **2026-08-11 | depok24jam.com (citing MOLI's H1 2026 interim financials)** |
   H1 2026 revenue IDR 730.66bn (+1.65% YoY); net profit IDR 75.33bn (+192.14%
   YoY, from IDR 25.79bn); profit attributable to owners IDR 63.22bn (from
   IDR 19.87bn). COGS fell 8.58% YoY; gross margin widened from 25.43% to
   32.94%. Finance expense fell >92% (consistent with the debt-free balance
   sheet already in the fact sheet). Ethanol = 89.16% of revenue; Ethanol +
   Fertilizer segment = ~92.05%. | **Implication:** confirms the fact sheet's
   "peak earnings" flag has a concrete, traceable cause — cheaper input, not
   higher ethanol prices or volumes. Revenue growth of 1.65% with a 23.85%
   prior-quarter YoY figure in the fact sheet suggests uneven quarterly
   comps; the margin move, not the top line, is doing the work.

2. **2026-04-09 | CNBC Indonesia ("Produksi Melimpah, Impor Tetap Banjir: Ada
   Apa dengan Gula RI?")** | Molasses (tetes tebu) farmgate price fell from
   ~IDR 1,900/liter to ~IDR 1,000/liter around March 2026 — a ~47% collapse —
   driven by a refined-sugar import glut that is crowding out absorption of
   domestic cane/molasses output; PTPN reported ~IDR 600bn and Sinergi Gula
   Nusantara ~IDR 680bn of 2025 losses tied to this dynamic. | **Implication:
   this is the single most important fact in this brief.** It is the direct,
   dated explanation for MOLI's H1 2026 margin expansion (COGS -8.6%, gross
   margin +7.5pp). Molasses is MOLI's main input; this is an input-cost
   collapse, not a structural repricing of ethanol.

3. **2026-06-09 | Bisnis.com / Kontan / Antara / Investor Trust (consistent
   multi-outlet reporting)** | MOLI/Molindo will spend ~IDR 350bn capex in
   2026 to raise molasses-based ethanol capacity from ~80m liters/year to
   ~100m liters/year, in stages through September 2027 (new distillation
   train, liquid-CO2 line, boiler). Company has been designated to supply
   East Java bioethanol demand to Pertamina. Fuel-grade capacity specifically
   is ~10m liters/year, of which ~4m liters/year is already absorbed by
   industrial (non-fuel) customers, leaving ~6m liters/year available for the
   E5 program. | **Implication:** MOLI's own fuel-grade contribution to the
   national mandate is small in absolute terms; the capex is a bet on volume
   growth (total capacity +25%) more than on a step-change in fuel-grade
   pricing. Most of the new capacity comes online near or after the 12-month
   catalyst window (through Sept 2027).

## What Changed

The screener's own trigger — trailing EBIT at 2.2x the 5-year median — now
has a specific, dated mechanism: a sugar-sector oversupply / import-policy
shock crashed MOLI's main input cost in March 2026, and the H1 2026 results
released today show that cost collapse flowing straight through to net
income. Nothing found in this search indicates the ethanol *selling* price
moved materially, or that mandate-driven volume has yet scaled — MOLI's own
fuel-grade capacity utilisation for the E5 program is still nascent.

## What Management Says

Public statements (via press, not a primary transcript — INFERENCE on tone)

grow the liquid-CO2 business, positioned explicitly against the E5 mandate
and East Java Pertamina supply role. No primary-source management

## What The Market Is Worried About

Financial press coverage of the E5 rollout is notably cautious, not
promotional: multiple outlets (Bisnis.com's "Jalan Berliku Mandatory E5,"
Suar.id) frame the mandate as constrained by (a) domestic fuel-grade supply
—national fuel-grade ethanol capacity is only ~60,000 kl/year against an
estimated 1.0–1.2 million kl/year required for a full E5 rollout — and (b)
the absence of a financing/subsidy mechanism analogous to the palm-levy-funded
BPDPKS that underwrites B50 biodiesel. An economist quoted in Suara Surabaya
coverage says the E5 mandate "belum bisa tekan impor BBM" (won't yet reduce
fuel imports) at current scale. [FACT, multi-outlet, dated 2026-06 to
2026-07]

## Potential Catalysts

1. **E5 mandate volume ramp** — already legally in force (see below); the
   open question is whether MOLI's Pertamina East Java allocation actually
   scales toward its ~6m liter/year available fuel-grade capacity over the
   next several quarters. Datable via future disclosures/quarterly filings,
   not yet observed.
2. **Molasses price reversion** — government is tightening refined-sugar
   import enforcement (Permentan 98/2013 domestic-cane-supply requirement)
   and funding a 100,000 ha (2026) / 150,000 ha (2027) cane replanting
   ("bongkar ratoon") program aimed at raising domestic cane productivity.
   If either measure firms up molasses prices, MOLI's spread compresses back
   toward its 5-year median — this cuts against the bull case and confirms
   the screener's peak-earnings flag. Timing of effect is likely multi-year,
   not within 12 months.
3. **New capacity coming online** — ethanol capacity 80m→100m liters/year in
   stages through September 2027; liquid-CO2 line expansion. Partially
   outside the 12-month window from today.

## Catalyst Timeline

- **2026-07-01 (already occurred):** E5 mandate legally effective for
  non-subsidised (non-PSO) gasoline, initial rollout across Java
  (Jakarta/Jabodetabek, East Java, West Java, Central Java, DIY, Banten,
  reported variably as including Bali/Lampung in some outlets).
- **2026 (ongoing, through Sept 2027):** IDR 350bn capex program, phased
  capacity additions.
- **2027–2028 (outside 12-month window):** government target for E10
  mandate — requires ~1.4 million kl/year of bioethanol nationally, ~8.7–9x
  current national fuel-grade production capacity. Treat as THESIS/ASSUMPTION
  at this distance, not a dated near-term catalyst.

## Catalyst Probability

- E5 mandate *staying* in force and being enforced at current (Java-only,
  non-PSO) scope: **high** — it is already binding law (Permen ESDM No.
  4/2025; Kepmen ESDM No. 113.K/EK.05/MEM.E/2026), not a proposal.
- E5 mandate materially lifting MOLI's realised fuel-grade volumes/pricing
  within 12 months: **low-to-moderate** — supply-side bottleneck (60,000 kl
  national fuel-grade capacity vs >1m kl needed) and absence of a funding
  mechanism make full-scale enforcement unlikely soon; multiple contemporary
  outlets describe implementation as constrained/gradual.
- E10 mandate arriving and mattering within 12 months of today: **low** —
  government's own timeline is 2027–2028, and the supply gap is roughly an
  order of magnitude larger than for E5.
- Molasses price reverting upward (margin headwind) within 12 months:
  **moderate** — replanting programs take years to mature; import-policy
  enforcement could move faster but no dated confirmation of effectiveness
  was found this pass.

## Macro: TAILWIND / NEUTRAL / HEADWIND

**MIXED — lean NEUTRAL.** Biofuel policy is a genuine, dated, legally-binding
tailwind (Permen ESDM No. 4/2025 + Kepmen 113.K/EK.05/MEM.E/2026, effective
2026-07-01) — unlike a mere aspiration, this is analogous in *form* to B50,
but critically **lacks B50's funding backbone**: biodiesel is subsidised via
the palm export levy through BPDPKS; no equivalent levy-funded pool exists
for ethanol (explicitly reported as a gap by Bisnis.com and Suar.id,
2026-06/07). Sugar/molasses policy cuts the other way: the same government
that is tightening refined-sugar import enforcement to protect cane farmers
is, if effective, also removing the cheap-molasses tailwind that drove
MOLI's H1 2026 earnings. Net: policy support exists on the output
(ethanol-demand) side but is unfunded, while policy risk exists on the input
(molasses-cost) side. IDR and rate backdrop (BI-Rate 5.75%, hike-biased) is
roughly neutral for a zero-debt company.

## Industry: TAILWIND / NEUTRAL / HEADWIND

**TAILWIND, with meaningful execution risk.** Indonesia's fuel-grade ethanol
industry faces a large structural supply deficit versus mandate targets
(~60,000 kl current fuel-grade capacity vs ~1–1.2 million kl needed for E5,
~1.4 million kl for E10) — a scarcity dynamic that favours incumbent
fuel-grade producers like MOLI on allocation and potentially pricing, *if*
the mandate is enforced at scale. No material import-competition threat to
ethanol itself was found (contrast with sugar, where import competition is
the dominant threat to MOLI's input cost). Offset: press coverage
consistently flags the mandate's rollout as constrained and gradual, and an
economist is on record doubting near-term fuel-import impact.

## Catalyst Verdict: WEAK

No credible, dated, *unpriced* catalyst inside 12 months was found. The one
big, legally real catalyst (E5 mandate) took effect over a month before this
report (2026-07-01) and is therefore already public information reflected in
whatever narrative currently surrounds the stock — it is not a forward
trigger. Its scale-up is gradual and supply-constrained, with no equivalent
of BPDPKS funding behind it, and the bigger E10 mandate is explicitly a
2027–2028 event, outside this window. Capacity expansion (80m→100m
liters/year) is real and self-funded but phases in mostly through September
2027, straddling rather than sitting inside the 12-month window. The dominant
near-term swing factor is not a catalyst at all but a **risk**: the
molasses-price collapse that drove the H1 2026 earnings beat is a
commodity-input windfall, and government policy (cane-supply mandates,
replanting program) is explicitly aimed at reversing the sugar oversupply
that caused it — supporting the screener's peak-earnings red flag rather than
contradicting it.
