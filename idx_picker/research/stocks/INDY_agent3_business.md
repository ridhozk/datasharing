### Kideco (thermal coal core)

**FACT**: Kideco's original PKP2B (Coal Contract of Work / "Perjanjian Karya Pengusahaan Pertambangan Batubara") contract expired 13 March 2023. Kideco was among seven PKP2B holders whose contracts were converted to IUPK (Special Mining Business License / Izin Usaha Pertambangan Khusus) continuation licenses under the 2020 Minerba Law implementing regulations. [indopremier.com research note; general Minerba Law PKP2B-to-IUPK conversion reporting]

**INFERENCE**: the IUPK conversion means Kideco's concession is not a hard cliff at 2023 — it continues operating under IUPK terms (typically renewable in 10-year increments, subject to government approval, with materially different royalty/tax terms than the original PKP2B generation-1 contract, generally less favorable to the operator). This is a *terminal-value-relevant* fact: the asset's economic terms reset in 2023 and the group is now running on a license structure with periodic renewal risk rather than a long, fixed-term contract. **Insufficient evidence — requires further research** on: exact new IUPK term length/expiry, reserve life under current mine plan, and government approval status for the next renewal window — need the 2025 Annual Report (indikaenergy.co.id/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/INDY-AR-2025.pdf) or Kideco-specific technical/reserves report, not yet fetched in this pass.

**FACT** (proxy data on the broader Pasir coalfield, of which Kideco is the dominant operator): Wood Mackenzie profiles cite proven reserves ~531m tonnes and total resources >1.5bn tonnes for the Pasir mine area, with ~30.1m tonnes produced in 2023. At Kideco's ~30m tonnes/year production run-rate (30.51m tonnes group-wide in FY25, essentially all Kideco), reserves-to-production on that reserve figure imply a mine life on the order of ~15-18 years at current extraction rates — but this Wood Mackenzie figure may not be Indika's own JORC/SNI-reported reserve statement. **Insufficient evidence — requires further research** to confirm against Indika's own disclosed proved+probable reserves and stripping ratio trend (higher strip ratio compresses margins even before mine life runs out).

## 3. Industry structure (preliminary)

**FACT**: Indonesia's 2026 coal production target was cut to 600Mt from 750-790Mt realized in 2025 amid weak prices; DMO currently requires ≥25% of approved production sold domestically, with DMO-for-power capped at US$70/t (well below export parity in strong-price years, a structural margin tax); export levies up to 5%; royalties scale with the government benchmark price (HBA). A proposed windfall tax has been discussed but not yet implemented. Indonesian thermal coal export volumes in 2026 are running ~9.5% below 2025's annualized pace, with China imports down ~13.8% YoY and India down ~17.0% YoY. ICI4 index was ~$63.80/t by April 2026, roughly half of 2022 peak pricing. [aseanbriefing.com, thecoalhub.com, argusmedia.com, jakartaglobe.id, discoveryalert.com.au — see search results]

**INFERENCE**: this is a structurally headwind industry backdrop — falling China/India demand, government-mandated price suppression via DMO on a rising share of output, and a global energy-transition-driven cost-of-capital penalty on coal assets generally. This directly corroborates the screener's Normalised/Trailing EBIT of 1.40 (current trailing earnings are below normalized, but "normalized" here is itself a 5-year window dominated by the 2022 supercycle year the CLAUDE.md explicitly warns against treating as representative).
