### Kideco (thermal coal core)

**FACT**: Kideco's original PKP2B (Coal Contract of Work) contract expired 13 March 2023. The Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources (ESDM) granted Kideco a 10-year IUPK (Special Mining Business License) continuation, permit no. 14/1/IUP/PMA/2022 effective 16 December 2022, **running to 13 March 2033**. As a condition of the extension, Kideco's concession area was **cut by 28.65%, from 47,500 hectares to 33,887 hectares**. [bisnis.com, investor.id, kontan.co.id, cnbcindonesia.com, ilmutambang.com — consistent multi-source Indonesian reporting, Jan 2023]

**INFERENCE — this is the single most important structural fact for terminal value.** As of today (2026-08-11) Kideco's license has **under 6.6 years left** before its next renewal decision point (March 2033), and the last renewal round already cost the company nearly 29% of its licensed mining area. The IUPK regime also carries different (generally less favorable to the operator) royalty terms than the original Generation-1 PKP2B. A DCF or comps-based valuation that implicitly assumes indefinite coal cash flows overstates Kideco's terminal value; a valuation that properly caps cash flows at the 2033 license horizon (with renewal-probability-weighted extension) will show materially lower intrinsic value than a perpetuity-style EV/EBIT multiple implies. This is squarely the kind of "cycle-recovery-dependent" and terminal-value risk the screener's Normalised/Trailing EBIT of 1.40 does not capture at all.

**FACT** (proxy data on the broader Pasir coalfield, of which Kideco is the dominant operator): Wood Mackenzie profiles cite proven reserves ~531m tonnes and total resources >1.5bn tonnes for the Pasir mine area. Kideco/group production was 30.7m tonnes in 2024 and 30.51m tonnes in 2025 (petromindo.com; Indika FY25 results release). **INFERENCE**: at a ~30-31Mt/yr run-rate, disclosed proven reserves alone (531Mt) imply a mine-life on the order of ~17 years — i.e., *reserves plausibly outlast the current 2033 license by roughly a decade*, meaning the binding constraint on Kideco's terminal value is very likely the **license term and its renewal terms, not physical reserve exhaustion**. **Insufficient evidence — requires further research** to confirm this reserve figure against Indika's own JORC/SNI-reported proved+probable reserves (not yet located) and the current stripping ratio trend.

## 2. Is the diversification value-creating or value-destroying? (preliminary)

**FACT**: FY2022–2025 shows FCF collapsing from +15,265bn IDR (2022, peak coal price year) to −5,696bn (2023), −2,863bn (2024), and roughly breakeven +167bn (2025), per the screener fact sheet — even as capex stayed roughly flat to rising (−1,319 → −2,131 → −1,874 → −1,339bn IDR/yr). TTM FCF yield is −6%.

**INFERENCE**: the swing from strongly FCF-positive to FCF-negative is driven overwhelmingly by the coal price/margin collapse (gross profit fell from 25,981bn IDR in 2022 to 4,841bn in 2025, an ~81% decline) rather than by the new-venture capex alone — coal EBITDA compression is the dominant driver, with diversification capex as a secondary drag on top of it.

**Insufficient evidence — requires further research** on: (a) segment-level ROIC/returns for Awak Mas, nickel, and Alva individually; (b) management's capital-allocation track record on prior diversification attempts (Indika has cycled through several non-coal bets over the past decade — mining services/Petrosea, Tripatra, prior renewable and telco/digital ventures — with mixed public record); (c) whether the US$6.1tn IDR loan reported to fund Awak Mas and refinance debt materially raises leverage risk given coal cash flow is simultaneously weak. This requires the full FY2025 Annual Report and possibly Petrosea (PTRO, listed subsidiary) filings, not yet reviewed.

## 3. Industry structure (preliminary)

**FACT**: Indonesia's 2026 coal production target was cut to 600Mt from 750-790Mt realized in 2025 amid weak prices; DMO currently requires ≥25% of approved production sold domestically, with DMO-for-power capped at US$70/t (well below export parity in strong-price years, a structural margin tax); export levies up to 5%; royalties scale with the government benchmark price (HBA). A proposed windfall tax has been discussed but not yet implemented. Indonesian thermal coal export volumes in 2026 are running ~9.5% below 2025's annualized pace, with China imports down ~13.8% YoY and India down ~17.0% YoY. ICI4 index was ~$63.80/t by April 2026, roughly half of 2022 peak pricing. [aseanbriefing.com, thecoalhub.com, argusmedia.com, jakartaglobe.id, discoveryalert.com.au — see search results]

**INFERENCE**: this is a structurally headwind industry backdrop — falling China/India demand, government-mandated price suppression via DMO on a rising share of output, and a global energy-transition-driven cost-of-capital penalty on coal assets generally. This directly corroborates the screener's Normalised/Trailing EBIT of 1.40 (current trailing earnings are below normalized, but "normalized" here is itself a 5-year window dominated by the 2022 supercycle year the CLAUDE.md explicitly warns against treating as representative).

## 4. Ownership, governance, and capital-allocation track record

**FACT** (MarketScreener shareholder register): founder/insider ownership is highly concentrated — **Agus Lasmono 37.98%**, **Wiwoho Basuki Tjokronegoro 28.08%**, **Pandri Prabono-Moelyo 5.09%**, company treasury 0.14%. Combined founder/insider stakes are roughly **~71%** of shares outstanding, leaving a free float on the order of ~29%. Agus Lasmono and Arsjad Rasjid are the founding Commissioner/Director team who built the company from 2000-2005 onward on Chinese coal demand.

**INFERENCE**: this is a founder-controlled company, not a widely-held one. High insider ownership can align incentives (founders bear their own capital-allocation mistakes directly), but it also concentrates control such that minority shareholders have limited ability to block related-party transactions or capital-allocation decisions they might disagree with — e.g., the decision to keep funding gold/nickel/EV ventures through a coal down-cycle rather than prioritizing balance-sheet repair or shareholder returns (dividend payout ratio is already low at ~20%, TTM yield ~0.4%). **Insufficient evidence — requires further research** on specific related-party-transaction disclosures (RPT notes in the Annual Report were not fetched successfully in this pass — the FY25 results PDF returned binary/unparseable content via WebFetch).
