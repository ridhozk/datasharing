## Current Market Narrative

INDY is a legacy Kalimantan thermal-coal producer (Kideco, ~73% of consolidated
revenue) attempting a self-funded pivot into gold (Awak Mas, South Sulawesi)
and nickel (Rockgeo), targeting 50% non-coal revenue by 2028. Coal earnings
have collapsed from the 2022 supercycle peak (EBIT down from IDR 19.7tn in
2022 to IDR 2.5tn in 2025 per the screener's own annual history) and the
market is pricing the stock as a melting, high-payout coal cash cow with
uncertain optionality on the gold project. FACT/INFERENCE mix below.

**DMO (Domestic Market Obligation):** FACT — Kideco allocated **3.1Mt (45%

national mandated minimum of 25% of annual output at government-set
benchmark prices; the remaining 3.9Mt (55%) was exported to China, India,
Japan and other markets. Domestic allocation at capped benchmark prices
structurally drags realized ASP below export parity — Kideco's Q1 2026 ASP
was **USD 51.1/ton** (down 1.7% YoY), and Q1 2026 Kideco revenue **declined
5.7% YoY to USD 377.4m** on a 4.1% volume decline to 7.0Mt, before recovering
in H1 cumulative terms (+10.2% YoY to USD 855.1m per the 31 Jul 2026
release, implying a stronger Q2). **FACT, dated 2026 (exact date not
independently confirmed):** Indonesia is reportedly weighing **raising the
national coal DMO requirement above 30%**, per Jakarta Globe reporting — if
enacted, this would push more of Kideco's volume onto capped domestic
pricing, a further structural headwind on realized ASP.
[Jakarta Globe](https://jakartaglobe.id/business/indonesia-weighs-raising-coal-domestic-market-obligation-above-30)

## What Changed

Since the last-known baseline (screener regenerated 2026-08-11, using data
through 6M26): Kideco's H1 2026 revenue grew (+10.2% YoY) on a modest ASP
increase, the Kideco divestment rumour surfaced and was explicitly
non-denied by management (17 Jul 2026), and coal prices have softened over
the trailing month even as they remain above year-ago levels. The
government's 2026 coal production quota cut (~600Mt) is a new supply
discipline signal not yet reflected in spot price action.

## What Management Says

Management has publicly committed to a 50% non-coal revenue target by 2028,
accelerated Awak Mas construction (43% complete as of Oct 2025), and
explicitly declined to comment on Kideco divestment speculation, stating any
material corporate action will be disclosed through official channels per
disclosure regulations (17 Jul 2026 statement). No public commentary located
on a specific coal price outlook or dividend policy change.

## What The Market Is Worried About

INFERENCE (from combination of screener red flags and news flow): (1) Kideco
is a depleting, DMO-capped cash cow with earnings 87% below its 2022 peak
EBIT; (2) the Awak Mas capex programme (USD 429m) is being funded substantially
by fresh debt even as interest coverage sits at a thin 2.55x (screener);
(3) the divestment rumour, if it materializes as a minority-stake sale rather
than an outright disposal, may not meaningfully de-risk the coal-cyclicality
exposure that dominates today's earnings; (4) India's rising domestic coal
output structurally caps upside in INDY's single largest export market.

3. A reversal of the current coal-price softening trend (India import
   substitution reversing, China demand re-accelerating) — no dated evidence

## Macro: HEADWIND

Coal price momentum is currently negative (trailing month down despite
positive YoY), India — a key seaborne demand source — is substituting
imports with rising domestic production, and global energy-transition
financing pressure continues to structurally cap valuation multiples for
pure-play coal producers. Indonesian government revenue targets from the
minerba sector are rising even as headline royalty rates for some licence
classes were cut, mirroring the palm-levy pattern of extracting more from
commodity producers — a mixed-to-negative regulatory signal. BI's tightening
bias (established elsewhere in this project) raises the cost of any future
IDR-denominated financing, though INDY's core debt is USD-denominated.

## Catalyst Verdict: WEAK-TO-MODERATE

The only catalyst with firm, dated, company-sourced evidence (Awak Mas)
delivers trial production within the 12-month window but is unlikely to
contribute material EBITDA inside that window — ramp economics for a new
open-pit gold operation typically extend well beyond first production. The
next most significant candidate (Kideco divestment) remains unconfirmed
market speculation that management has explicitly declined to validate.
Coal price recovery — the most direct earnings lever given Kideco's ~73%
revenue weight — currently shows negative, not positive, momentum. Given the
screener's -29% MOS is a substantial gap to close, and no single catalyst
here is both dated *and* large enough to plausibly close it within 12
months, the honest verdict leans toward WEAK, with MODERATE reserved only if
the Kideco divestment rumour converts to a confirmed transaction — an event
this report cannot presently substantiate as more than speculation.
