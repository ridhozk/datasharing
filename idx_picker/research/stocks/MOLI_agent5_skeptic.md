# MOLI — Agent 5: Thesis Killer / Skeptical Investment Committee

*Ticker: MOLI (Madusari Murni Indah Tbk). Price IDR 274. Screener verdict: SKIP
(MOS 9% vs 10% floor). Research date 2026-08-11.*

**Scoring convention: Skeptic Score is 0–100 where HIGHER = the thesis survives
scrutiny better / more bullish confidence; LOWER = more concern, closer to
value-trap / REJECT.** (Not a "kill score" — 0 means fully killed.)

## 1. Priority 1 — The peak-earnings case (the screener's own flag)

**FACT.** Screener: Normalised/Trailing EBIT = 0.46 (trailing EBIT is 2.2x the
5-year median of IDR 91.5bn). Annual OperatingIncome history: 73.5 (2022) →
109.5 (2023) → 55.2 (2024) → 152.0 (2025 = the current spike year). Quarterly
gross margin, per press coverage of the interim filings:

- Q3 2025 (9M, YoY): COGS **fell** 1.16% (Rp796.07bn → Rp786.74bn) while
  revenue rose 3.56%; gross profit +18.6%; net income +90.4-93%.
  ([StockWatch](https://stockwatch.id/kuartal-iii-2025-laba-madusari-murni-indah-moli-melonjak-93-ini-pemicunya/),
  [IPOTNews](https://www.indopremier.com/ipotnews/newsDetail.php?jdl=MOLI_Bukukan_Kinerja_Positif_Hingga_September_2025__Laba_Bersih_Melejit_90_4_Persen&news_id=210125&group_news=IPOTNEWS))
- H1 2026 (YoY): revenue +1.65% (Rp718.77bn → Rp730.66bn) but COGS **fell**
  8.58% (Rp535.98bn → Rp489.99bn); gross margin expanded from 25.43% to
  32.94%; net income attributable to owners +218% (Rp19.87bn → Rp63.22bn), net
  income (incl. minority) +192% (Rp25.79bn → Rp75.33bn).
  ([Depok24jam](https://www.depok24jam.com/2026/08/11/madusari-murni-indah-moli-semester-i-2026-laba-melonjak/))

**This is a cost story, not a volume or pricing story** — revenue is nearly
flat across both periods; the entire profit surge is COGS compression. That
points straight at input cost (molasses is 50-60% of COGS per a third-party
sell-side note — see below) rather than a durable operating improvement.

**FACT.** Molasses (tetes tebu) prices collapsed from a normal band of
roughly Rp2,400–3,000/kg to Rp700–1,000/kg starting around August-September
2025, following Trade Ministry Regulation (Permendag) No. 16/2025, which
opened molasses imports from Thailand. This devastated sugarcane-farmer and
sugar-mill income (farmers threatened protests; mills warned of shutdowns).
([Kompas](https://money.kompas.com/read/2025/09/20/201600126/harga-tetes-anjlok-ke-rp-700-produsen-terancam-hentikan-operasional?page=all),
[Koran Jakarta](https://koran-jakarta.com/2025-09-21/sadis-harga-tetes-gula-hancur-produsen-terancam-gulung-tikar),
[CNBC Indonesia](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/news/20250827155038-4-661862/tetes-tebu-petani-luber-tak-laku-meski-harga-anjlok-ini-penyebabnya))
As of March 2026, molasses was still reported around Rp1,000/kg — well below
normal. ([Hukumonline/search summary]) The government responded with a
tightened import/licensing regime for ethanol and molasses trade ("lartas"),
formalized as Permendag 18/2026, with most provisions effective 4 July 2026,
explicitly framed as protecting national sugar trade ("tata niaga gula") and
farmer income. ([Hukumonline](https://www.hukumonline.com/berita/a/4-pokok-perubahan-permendag-18-2026-soal-kebijakan-dan-pengaturan-impor-lt6a2fbfb9cf695/),
[Readers.id](https://www.readers.id/pemerintah-perkuat-tata-niaga-gula-larang-impor-etanol))

**INFERENCE.** The timing lines up almost exactly: MOLI's margin inflection
begins in the quarter after the molasses-import liberalization (mid/late
2025) and is still visibly elevated through H1 2026, the same window in which
molasses prices are independently reported as still depressed (~Rp1,000/kg in
March 2026). The screener's TTM window (latest filing 2026-06-30) sits
entirely inside this cost-collapse period. I could not fully disentangle from
public sources whether Permendag 18/2026 tightens the *molasses raw-material*
import channel that lowered MOLI's cost, the *finished-ethanol* import
channel that competes with MOLI's output, or both — the coverage of "larang
impor etanol berbasis molase" (ban molasses-based ethanol imports) suggests
it may target finished-ethanol imports specifically, which would be a
different (output-price-protective, not cost-inflating) mechanism. **This
needs primary-source regulatory text to resolve — flagged as insufficient
evidence for the precise mechanism**, but the fact pattern (crash → farmer
crisis → active government correction) is unambiguous, and it establishes
that the cost base MOLI enjoyed for the past ~4 quarters is a
policy-created anomaly under active political pressure to reverse, not a
structural or durable feature of the ethanol-molasses spread.

**FACT — quantitative valuation check, computed from the screener's own
numbers.** EV = IDR 807.72bn. Normalised EBIT (5Y) = IDR 91.49bn. **EV /
Normalised EBIT = 8.83x.** Peer group EV/EBIT median = 9.13x. **On mid-cycle
earnings, MOLI is trading in line with its peer median, not at a discount to
it.** The reported "4.1x EV/EBIT" cheapness exists only because it divides
enterprise value by the currently inflated trailing EBIT. This is the
cleanest, single number that kills the "screamingly cheap" framing: correct
for the earnings cycle and the statistical bargain disappears entirely.

**FACT.** The screener's own DCF (bear/base/bull IV = 59/61/64) already
prices this outcome — a huge internal contradiction the fact sheet itself
flagged. Those figures imply the stock is roughly **4x overvalued** at 274 on
a normalized-cash-flow basis. It is only the comparables lens (IV 719, built
from peer multiples × *trailing* earnings) that pulls the blended IV up to
300 and manufactures a 9% MOS. Blending a normalized DCF with a
peak-earnings-based comparables figure and calling the average "intrinsic
value" understates how contaminated the reported MOS actually is — the true
apparent cheapness is closer to zero-to-negative once EPV (273, essentially
= price) and normalized EV/EBIT (in line with peers) are weighted properly.

## 2. Priority 2 — Is the 48% FCF yield a working-capital illusion?

**FACT.** Inventory: IDR 596.2bn (FY2024) → 359.2bn (FY2025) → 282.1bn (Q1
2026) → 215.1bn (Q2 2026). Cumulative drawdown of **~IDR 381bn** over six
quarters, against TTM FCF of IDR 357.0bn. **A large share of the "48% FCF
yield" is inventory liquidation, not repeatable operating cash generation.**
This is consistent with a company that bought cheap molasses during the
import-driven price crash, ran it through production, and is now sitting on
a smaller (cheaper-cost-basis) inventory buffer that it will eventually need
to replenish — likely at a higher price if the government succeeds in
reflating molasses costs.

**FACT.** Total debt: IDR 430.5bn (FY2024) → 13.3bn (FY2025) → 0 (Q1 2026
onward). **Roughly all of the incremental free cash flow over this period
funded full debt elimination**, not shareholder distributions — this directly
answers why the payout ratio is only 17% despite an eye-catching headline FCF
yield: the cash was going to the balance sheet, not to owners. Interest
expense fell in parallel (37.4bn 2022 → 14.7bn 2025 → ~0 by Q2 2026),
mechanically inflating net margin further and adding to the apparent EBIT/
EPS acceleration independent of the operating story.

**FACT.** Capex commitment for 2026 is Rp350bn (second ethanol plant,
130,000 KL/yr additional capacity, tied explicitly to the E5/E10 bioethanol
opportunity). ([Antara](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5601951/moli-siapkan-capex-rp350-miliar-incar-peluang-program-bioetanol)) At
current cash of IDR 149bn, this capex plan alone explains low near-term
payout — the company is retaining cash to build capacity ahead of demand, a
reasonable capital-allocation story **if** the E5/E10 mandate volumes
materialize as planned, but it also means the "cash-rich, zero-debt, ready to
return capital" framing implied by a 48% FCF yield is not the operative
capital-allocation reality — most of the cash is earmarked for growth capex,
and debt paydown already consumed the prior tranche.

**Genuine operating cash flow quality is unverifiable from the fact sheet**:
`Operating Cash Flow (TTM)` is blank in every period in the source data
(Yahoo does not publish it for IDX names per CLAUDE.md's known gap), and the
figure used (FCF = FCF − CapEx per the pipeline's derivation) inherits
whatever noise sits in the reported `FreeCashFlow` line itself, which swung
from -95.8bn (2023) to +423.0bn (2025) — a magnitude and sign swing far too
large to be pure operating improvement. **Insufficient evidence to fully
decompose FCF into operating vs. working-capital components without the
primary cash flow statement** — this is a real gap; recommend pulling
MOLI's IDX-filed audited cash flow statement before sizing any position.

## 3. Priority 3 — The liquidity trap / closed shop

**FACT.** Ownership (per Cari Saham): PT Cropsco Panen Indonusa 52.15%,
Handjojo Rustanto 12.73%, Indra Winarno 6.93%, Henny Rustanto 6.90%,
Handayani Rustanto 5.44%, PT Sejahtera Investama Indah 5.41%, public
(non-controlling) **~9.27%**.
([Cari Saham](https://carisaham.com/emiten/profile/MOLI/shareholders)) That
is **~90.7% held by one family/group across a holding company and named
individuals**, with the Rustanto family name recurring across three of the
top six lines — this reads as a single controlling family bloc, not a
diversified institutional shareholder base.

**INFERENCE.** Free float of ~9.27% on 2.724bn shares ≈ 252.5m shares. At
IDR 274, that free float is worth only ~**IDR 69bn** — against the fact
sheet's own note of ~IDR 306mn/day traded value, that implies it would take
roughly **225 trading days (close to a full year)** to turn over the entire
free float once at current volume. This is not a "thin but survivable"
liquidity constraint for a IDR 100m portfolio — it is a structural feature of
a stock where price discovery is largely absent and a determined seller
(including the controlling family, should its incentives ever change) could
move the price sharply with modest volume. **This materially amplifies the
consequence of being wrong on the peak-earnings call**: if the thesis breaks,
exit is slow and costly.

**Insufficient evidence** on whether the Cropsco/Rustanto group has any
molasses-supply, sugar-mill, or trading interest that could create
related-party transfer-pricing exposure on the very COGS line driving this
thesis. This is exactly the kind of check that should be done from the
audited RPT footnotes before underwriting the current margin as clean.

## 4. Other factors

**Moat / licensing (INFERENCE, moderate confidence).** MOLI (via subsidiary
Molindo Raya) is Indonesia's largest food-grade ethanol producer (~80,000
KL/yr capacity, expanding by 130,000 KL/yr), founded 1965, serving food &
beverage, pharma, cosmetics, tobacco, hospital, furniture, ink/printing, fuel,
and welding industries — a genuinely diversified industrial customer base,
which is a mitigant against single-customer concentration risk (no
percentage breakdown found — **insufficient evidence** for a precise
concentration figure). ~40% of sales reportedly export. Ethyl-alcohol
production in Indonesia requires an excise-facility license (NPPBKC) — a real
regulatory barrier to entry — but a third-party sell-side note characterizes
the end product as "commoditylike" with minimal producer-to-producer pricing
power, consistent with the observation that MOLI's margin swings are
overwhelmingly cost-driven (molasses, coal) rather than pricing-power-driven.
([Arvest](https://arvest.co.id/article/show/135))

**Excise/tax risk (FACT, directionally favorable).** PMK 34/2026 (May 2026)
*expanded* excise exemption to cover petroleum-ethanol blending activity
specifically, easing the regulatory/tax friction for the E5 program rather
than adding risk. ([IKPI](https://ikpi.or.id/en/aturan-baru-pembebasan-cukai-etil-alkohol-kini-diperluas-untuk-energi-bersih/))
Beverage-alcohol excise (Rp20,000/liter, MMEA) is a separate regime and not
obviously MOLI's core exposure (its stated end-markets are industrial/food/
fuel, not beverage), but I could not confirm what fraction (if any) of MOLI's
volume is beverage-grade — **insufficient evidence**.

**Bioethanol mandate — the real, structural bull case (FACT).** Indonesia
will mandate E5 (5% ethanol-blended gasoline) in seven provinces from July
2026, with a domestic-feedstock requirement instructed by the Energy
Minister, rising to E10 by 2028 and a longer-term E20 ambition. National
ethanol production (~161,000 KL) is far short of the ~890,000 KL needed for
E10 nationwide — a genuine, multi-year supply gap that MOLI, as the largest
domestic producer, is capitalizing on with its Rp350bn/130,000 KL expansion.
([Fuels & Lubes](https://www.fuelsandlubes.com/indonesia-to-mandate-e5-ethanol-blend-in-july-2026/),
[Jakarta Post](https://www.thejakartapost.com/business/2026/05/22/govt-to-deploy-mandatory-e5-bioethanol-b50-biodiesel-program-in-july))
**This is the strongest reason not to dismiss MOLI outright**: unlike a pure
commodity-cycle peak, there is an identifiable structural demand catalyst
that could eventually justify raising the *normalization baseline* itself,
not just riding the current cost windfall. But mandate execution risk in
Indonesian biofuel policy has historically been high (biodiesel B30-B40
mandates slipped repeatedly for years) — treat the E5 timeline as
directionally real but execution-uncertain (**THESIS**, not FACT).

**Rate environment (FACT/context).** BI at 5.75%, +100bp since May 2026,
raises the discount rate used in any DCF and directly works against an
already-thin (or negative, per the screener's own bear/base/bull band)
margin of safety — tightening liquidity conditions also make it a worse
environment to be holding an illiquid, thinly-floated small cap.

## 5. Stress tests

**Bear case.** Molasses reverts toward Rp2,000-2,500/kg (still below the
pre-crash Rp2,400-3,000 norm, allowing for some genuine structural
efficiency gain) as Permendag 18/2026 tightening bites and farmer-protection
pressure continues. Gross margin compresses from the current ~33% back
toward the historical ~24-25% (2022-2024 average per the annual history
table). EBIT reverts most of the way toward the 5-year normalized base of
~91.5bn. At an unchanged EV/EBIT multiple in line with the peer median
(9.1x), fair EV ≈ 91.5bn × 9.1 ≈ 833bn — coincidentally close to today's EV
of 808bn, i.e., **even in the bear case the stock is roughly fairly valued,
not undervalued**; in a harsher bear case (multiple compression on top of
earnings reversion, as the market re-rates a formerly "cheap" stock once the
cheapness is seen to be illusory) downside to the DCF band (59-64) is very
real.

**Thesis-breaker.** Confirmation, via MOLI's own H2 2026/FY2026 filings, that
gross margin holds at ~30%+ *after* Permendag 18/2026 is fully in force
(post-July 2026) despite molasses prices recovering — this would mean the
margin gain has become structural (e.g., long-term supply contracts locked
in at low cost, or genuine efficiency gains as the Q3 2025 press coverage
claimed) rather than a pass-through of a temporary import price. Absent that
confirmation, the safest working assumption is reversion.

**Value-trap test — why hasn't the market recognized a 6.3x PE, zero-debt,
9/9 F-Score company?** Answered concretely above: **it has, correctly.** The
market is pricing MOLI on a normalized-earnings basis (EPV/share = 273 ≈
today's price of 274; EV/normalized-EBIT = 8.8x ≈ peer median 9.1x), not on
the trailing peak multiple. The 6.3x trailing PE and 4.1x trailing EV/EBIT
are optical illusions produced by dividing a stable price by a temporarily
inflated earnings number. There is no undiscovered bargain here for the
market to be missing — if anything the market (via the ~90% controlling
family stake and thin free float) has simply never bothered to re-rate a
stock whose free float is too small to matter to institutional price
discovery in the first place.

**Contrarian test.** The contrarian case is not "the market is wrong about
cheapness" — it's "the market is under-pricing the E5/E10 structural demand
catalyst because MOLI's free float is too thin for that thesis to be
reflected in the price yet." That is a plausible, distinct thesis from the
screener's cheapness-based BUY hypothesis, but it is a 2-3 year capacity-
and-policy-execution bet, not a "cheap today" bet, and it requires
underwriting Indonesian biofuel-mandate execution risk and a controlling
family that has shown no clear intent (17% payout ratio) to share upside
with minority holders in the interim.

## Bottom line

The screener's SKIP was not a 1-percentage-point coin-flip miss — it was
directionally correct for the right underlying reason, and the underlying
reason is more severe than "MOS came in 1pp light." The stock's headline
cheapness is a peak-earnings/peak-cost-collapse artifact with an identified,
dated, politically-contested cause (molasses import liberalization now being
reversed), a meaningful share of its FCF is inventory drawdown and debt
paydown rather than repeatable cash generation, and its ~9% free float makes
it a structurally illiquid, closed-shop stock. The one real offsetting
positive — the E5/E10 bioethanol mandate — is a genuine multi-year catalyst
but not a reason to buy at today's price on today's (mis-normalized)
multiple.

Investment Committee Verdict: **REJECT**
