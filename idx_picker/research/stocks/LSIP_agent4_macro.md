## Priority 2 — The dividend catalyst: TESTED AND LARGELY FALSIFIED

**FACT.** LSIP held its AGM in Jakarta on **Thursday 25 June 2026**. Shareholders approved a **final cash dividend of IDR 83 per share**, totalling **IDR 566.06 billion**, for financial year 2025. Cum-dividend 3 July 2026; recording date 7 July 2026 16:00 WIB; **paid 24 July 2026**. The same AGM replaced two directors. (Sources: IDX Channel, EmitenNews, Indo Premier/IPOTNEWS, PintarSaham, WartaFinansial.)

**FACT.** The stated **payout ratio was 29.99%** — i.e. an ordinary ~30% of net profit, explicitly framed by the company and press as a payout-ratio decision, not a return-of-capital event.

**Findings against the special-dividend hypothesis:**

1. **No policy change.** Payout was set at essentially exactly 30%, the same shape as prior years. No announcement of a revised dividend policy, no target payout uplift, no progressive-dividend statement was found.
2. **No special/interim dividend.** No evidence of any special dividend, interim dividend, or capital return declared in 2026.
3. **No buyback.** No share-buyback authorisation or programme was found for LSIP in 2026. *(Absence of evidence in public search; not proof of absence — but a buyback would be a material disclosure and would be reported.)*
4. **The cash pile grew anyway.** Cash rose from IDR 7,597bn (Dec-25) to **IDR 8,372bn (Jun-26)** — *after* paying the IDR 566bn dividend on 24 July, which is not yet in the 30 June balance sheet. Management is accumulating, not distributing. Net cash of IDR ~8.4tn against a market cap of IDR ~10.0tn means **84% of the market cap is cash the company has just demonstrated it will not return**.
5. **The window has closed for a year.** The 2026 AGM is done. Absent an EGM, the next scheduled decision point on distribution is the **FY2026 AGM, ~June 2027** — roughly ten months away.

**VERDICT on the dividend catalyst: the special-dividend thesis is HOPE, not evidence.** The 2026 AGM was the natural test, and it came and went with a routine 30% payout while the cash balance rose. Anyone underwriting LSIP on the expectation of a cash release must now explain what would change management's mind between now and June 2027, and no such evidence was found.

The *ordinary* dividend is nonetheless real and material: **IDR 83 on IDR 1,465 = a 5.7% trailing yield** (consistent with the screener's 5.67%), covered ~3.3x by earnings and effectively infinitely covered by cash. **That is a yield, not a catalyst.**

## Current Market Narrative

LSIP is priced as what it is: a well-run, debt-free, cash-rich but structurally minority-controlled plantation whose earnings are levered to a CPO price that is currently *high*, and whose enormous cash balance is treated by the market as belonging to the Salim group rather than to minorities. The stock is +6.2% over one year and sits 19% below its 52-week high despite CPO being strong and H1 profit up 25% — the market is refusing to capitalise the cash and refusing to capitalise peak-cycle earnings. The narrative is not "cheap and misunderstood"; it is **"cheap for a reason we have all agreed on."**

## What The Market Is Worried About

1. **The cash is not the minority shareholder's cash.** LSIP trades at 0.70x book with net cash equal to 84% of market cap — an EV/EBIT under 1.0x. The market has priced this discount persistently for years. The worry is not that the cash is fake; it is that **a 59.5% Salim-controlled subsidiary will never release it**, and the 2026 AGM just supplied another year of confirming evidence.
2. **Peak-cycle earnings.** CPO at RM4,678 with B50 support is a good environment. Investors are unwilling to pay 8x for earnings they believe are cyclically inflated. The screener's own normalisation agrees: **normalised/trailing EBIT of 0.685 says trailing EBIT is ~46% above the 5-year normalised level.**
3. **Margin compression despite good prices** — the 780bp gross-margin fall is visible to anyone reading the H1 release, and brokers flagged it as early as Q1.
4. **The tax and levy wedge** — 26.9% of the reference price, and rising. Indonesian planters are the funding source for the state's downstream ambitions.
5. **Flat nucleus production** — no organic growth engine.
6. **Loosening supply** into Q3/Q4 seasonal peak, with Malaysian stocks at a five-month high.
7. **EUDR at end-December 2026.**

## Potential Catalysts

**1. A special dividend or a step-change in payout policy — LOW probability, HIGH impact.**
The IDR 8.4tn net cash could fund a distribution worth more than half the market cap. Mechanism exists. **But the 2026 AGM (25 June) declined it, setting a 29.99% payout while cash grew.** Next natural window: **FY2026 AGM ~June 2027**, or an EGM (no evidence of one being convened). *THESIS, not evidence.*

**2. CPO price sustaining above RM4,700–5,000 into Q4 2026 on B50 offtake — MODERATE probability, MODERATE impact.**
B50 is now law and quotas are contracted. If effective blending genuinely lifts domestic absorption, Indonesian inventories draw down and price holds. **Counterweight:** July Malaysian stocks at a five-month high, and the export levy captures a rising share of any price rise. **Note the asymmetry — a higher CPO price is partly taxed away, while a lower CPO price is fully borne by the planter.**

**3. Corporate action within the Salim complex (IndoAgri/SIMP restructuring, tender offer, or privatisation) — LOW probability, HIGH impact.**
The persistent 0.70x-book, sub-1x EV/EBIT valuation is the classic setup for a controlling-shareholder buy-in. **No evidence of any such proposal exists.** *THESIS / ASSUMPTION — explicitly speculative and must not be underwritten.*

**Explicitly rejected as catalysts:** the ordinary 5.7% dividend (already paid 24 July 2026 — the buyer today is post-cum and waits ~11 months for the next); EUDR (a risk deferred, not a catalyst); "cheapness" itself (never a catalyst, per the framework).

## THESIS vs CATALYST — separated

**THESIS (what would make LSIP worth more than IDR 1,465):**
A debt-free plantation trading at 0.70x book with net cash equal to ~84% of market cap and an EV/EBIT under 1.0x is being valued as though the cash and the earnings both belong to someone else. If CPO holds near current levels and even a normalised share of that cash is ever attributed to minorities, the equity is worth materially more. The 5.7% dividend pays you to wait, and the balance sheet makes permanent capital loss unlikely. **This thesis is about asset value and downside protection — and it is genuinely strong on the downside.**

**THE COUNTER-THESIS the macro layer surfaces:**
Trailing earnings sit on an above-mid-cycle CPO price (normalised/trailing EBIT = 0.685). The state now takes ~26.9% of the reference price and has raised that take three times in eighteen months. Nucleus production is flat and gross margin fell 780bp in a year of *rising* prices. The controlling shareholder holds ~59.5% and just chose, at the June 2026 AGM, to pay 30% and keep building cash. **Cheap + no catalyst + controlled + peak-cycle earnings + deteriorating unit economics is the textbook composition of a value trap, and every one of those five elements is evidenced above rather than assumed.**

**CATALYST (what would force the market to re-price it):**
Essentially nothing dated inside six months. The one high-impact trigger — a capital-allocation change — was tested at the 25 June 2026 AGM and did not fire; the next window is ~June 2027. CPO strength is a *support*, not a re-rating event, and it is already in the price and in the trailing earnings.

## Information Edge

1. **The H1 margin story is public but under-weighted.** Almost all Indonesian coverage led with "profit +25%". Very few connected it to **nucleus FFB flat at 505kt** and **gross profit down 7% in absolute rupiah**. Anyone reading only the profit headline is buying growth that is bought-in, not grown.
2. **The 26.9% export tax-plus-levy wedge is the missing variable in most LSIP valuations.** Screens read the headline BMD price; LSIP realises a price net of a wedge that widened again on 1 March 2026. A CPO-price-driven DCF that ignores this is systematically too optimistic.
3. **The dividend catalyst is testable and was tested.** Rather than debating whether Salim "might" release cash, the 25 June 2026 AGM provides a dated, observed answer: 29.99%, cash up. This converts a speculative debate into evidence.
4. **B50 is law but not yet reality.** The gap between the legally mandated 50% and a credible effective ~B42–43 in 2026 is the difference between a transformational demand shock and an incremental one. Most bullish sector commentary does not make this distinction.
5. **The bullish El Niño narrative is contradicted by the July production data** (+9.4% m/m Malaysian output, five-month-high stocks). Forecasts citing USD 1,500/t on El Niño scarcity are not currently corroborated by observed supply.
6. **The screener data reconciles exactly to the company's reported H1** — so disagreement with the BUY verdict must come from interpretation (peak-cycle earnings, cash attribution, catalyst absence), not from data quality.

## Catalyst Verdict: **WEAK**

The one plausible high-impact catalyst — a release of the IDR 8.4tn cash pile — was put to the only body that could authorise it, at the 25 June 2026 AGM, and the answer was an ordinary 29.99% payout while cash continued to build. There is **no stated policy change, no management commentary on capital allocation, no buyback, no EGM, and no corporate action.** The next decision window is roughly ten months away. CPO strength is real and supportive but is already reflected in both the price and the trailing earnings, and is partly confiscated by a rising export levy. **The nearest dated event in the calendar — EUDR on 30 December 2026 — is a risk, not a catalyst.**

**"No catalyst" is the finding.** LSIP may still be a sound asset-backed, dividend-paying holding with unusually low risk of permanent capital loss. But the macro and news layer supplies **no mechanism by which the discount closes**, and it supplies **several reasons — flat nucleus volume, 780bp margin compression, a widening state levy wedge, and above-mid-cycle earnings — to expect the discount to persist or the earnings to fall toward it.** The Thesis Killer should treat the value-trap classification as the base case unless the valuation agent can defend intrinsic value on *normalised* CPO and *unattributed* cash.
