## 1. Asset quality — Batu Hijau

- **FACT**: Batu Hijau is an open-pit copper-gold porphyry mine on Sumbawa, Indonesia, operated by PT Amman Mineral Nusa Tenggara (AMNT), formerly Newmont Nusa Tenggara. [amman.co.id](https://www.amman.co.id/amman-mineral-nusa-tenggara)
- **FACT**: The mine is now in "Phase 8" — Phase 8 reserves are ~442–460 million tonnes at above-average grades of **0.38% Cu and 0.36 g/t Au**, commenced end of 2024, and are expected to extend Batu Hijau's operating life to **2030**, with stockpile processing potentially extending output to 2033. [Djakarta Mining Club](https://www.djakarta-miningclub.com/news/amman-continues-batu-hijau-phase-8-operations-unlocking-460-million-tons-of-reserves), [Jakarta Post](https://www.thejakartapost.com/business/2025/05/14/amman-extends-batu-hijau-copper-gold-mines-life-until-2030.html), [amman.co.id](https://www.amman.co.id/article/phase-8-batu-hijau-amman-s-strategic-transition-for-sustainable-mining-and-national-competitiveness)
- **INFERENCE**: The screener's observed **-13.3% revenue CAGR (3Y and 5Y identical, suggesting a short/volatile history)** despite a strong metals price environment is consistent with Batu Hijau's well-known phase/grade cyclicality — output moves from stripping/waste-heavy low-grade phases to ore-rich high-grade phases in a saw-tooth pattern. The AMMN.md quarterly history shows this directly: FY2025 Q1 revenue crashed to essentially zero (IDR 38bn) with a negative operating margin quarter, before rebounding to IDR 23.3tn in the Dec-2025 quarter and IDR 14.5tn in Mar-2026 — an order-of-magnitude swing within one year, not smooth cyclicality from price alone.
- **INFERENCE**: This grade profile means near-term (2026-2027) production/revenue should be read against the phase schedule, not against trailing-year comparisons — a name that looks like a broken growth story on a 3-year CAGR chart may simply be mid-transition between phases.
- **Insufficient evidence — requires further research**: total remaining Batu Hijau reserve tonnage/grade profile broken out by phase beyond Phase 8; precise year-by-year production guidance 2026-2030. (C1 cost curve position is now confirmed by primary source — see Section 4.)

## 2. Asset quality — Elang

- **FACT**: Elang is a porphyry copper-gold deposit ~60km east of Batu Hijau, undeveloped, widely described as one of the world's largest undeveloped copper-gold deposits. [amman.co.id — Exploration and Development](https://www.amman.co.id/exploration-and-development)
- **FACT**: As of FY2024 (Dec 31, 2024) JORC reserves, Elang ore reserves rose 79% YoY to **2.5 billion tonnes**, containing **17.8 billion lb copper** (some sources cite 23.4bn lb — likely resources vs. reserves distinction, see gap below) and **26.4 million oz gold** (one source cites 33.2m oz). [amman.co.id](https://www.amman.co.id/article/amman-copper-and-gold-reserves-in-the-elang-deposit-surge)
- **FACT**: A definitive feasibility study (DFS) for Elang was targeted for completion in H1 2025. Development/exploitation targeted to begin around 2027, with mining activity spanning **2031–2046**. [Ugems](https://www.ugems.id/web/guest/b/2024-02-27084910-amman-mineral-ammn-sets-sights-on-future-elang)
- **THESIS-relevant FACT**: Elang capex is estimated at **~USD 3 billion**, expensing beginning ~2028. This is a very large capital commitment relative to AMMN's current balance sheet (Q1-2026 net debt already ~IDR 101tn / ~USD 5.7bn per the screener baseline, with negative FCF every year since 2023).
- **FACT (financing track record)**: AMMN/AMNT has already funded its smelter buildout with large syndicated bank debt: it sought up to a **USD 1.5 billion loan** from Indonesian state banks (Mandiri, BNI) in mid-2024, and separately closed a **USD 750 million loan facility** (advised by Milbank) for capex/general corporate/expansion purposes. [Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/web/amman-mineral-seeks-up-to-1-5-billion-loan-for-mine-expansion/), [Milbank](https://www.milbank.com/en/news/milbank-advises-pt-amman-mineral-nusa-tenggara-on-a-750-million-usd-loan-facility.html) — **INFERENCE**: this demonstrates AMMN can access large-scale bank financing (including from state banks) for capex, which somewhat de-risks the Elang funding question, but it also means the ~USD 3bn Elang capex will most plausibly be funded with more debt on top of an already leveraged balance sheet, rather than internally generated cash.
- **INFERENCE**: Elang is the core of any long-duration bull case — it would roughly double or triple AMMN's reserve base and extend mine life well past Batu Hijau's 2030/2033 horizon — but it is pre-permitting/pre-construction, multi-billion-dollar, and years from cash generation. The stock today is being asked to pay for an option, not a producing asset.
- **Insufficient evidence — requires further research**: reconcile the conflicting reserve/resource figures (17.8bn vs 23.4bn lb Cu; 26.4m vs 33.2m oz Au — likely reserves vs. total resources); Elang's permitting status (AMDAL/environmental permit, forestry/land use, IUPK conversion) — not established; financing plan for the $3bn capex (debt capacity looks stretched given current leverage); confirmation of DFS completion/outcome.

## 3. Smelter and precious metals refinery (downstreaming)

- **FACT**: AMMAN's copper smelter and precious metals refinery (PMR), built under Indonesia's mineral downstreaming (hilirisasi) mandate, achieved mechanical completion in May 2024. First copper cathode production: March 2025. First refined gold: July 2025. Ramp-up reached 71% (smelter) / 55% (PMR) of design capacity by Q4 2025. Commissioning declared complete April 2026. [Quartr / amman.co.id FY2025 earnings release](https://www.amman.co.id/rails/active_storage/blobs/proxy/eyJfcmFpbHMiOnsiZGF0YSI6NDk4MCwicHVyIjoiYmxvYl9pZCJ9fQ==--9fe52aa807bb394abea52787b8ebc2d6723c9f42/FY%202025%20EARNINGS%20RELEASE.pdf)
- **FACT**: Design capacity is up to **900,000 tonnes of copper concentrate/year**, producing copper cathode, gold, silver, sulphuric acid, and selenium — i.e., a fully integrated mine-to-metal operation, not a concentrate exporter. [Indonesia Miner](https://indonesiaminer.com/news/detail/amman-completes-copper-smelter-project-boosting-annual-processing-capacity-to-900000-tonnes)
- **FACT**: Capex was heavy through this build: $1,392m in Q3 2024 (+52% YoY), and $1,372m in FY2025 (down 23% YoY as the major downstream projects completed) — this matches the AMMN.md FreeCashFlow line, which has been negative every year 2023-2025 (-29.4tn, -29.4tn, -33.1tn IDR) despite strong operating margins, confirming heavy capex is the driver of negative FCF, not weak operations.
- **INFERENCE**: The smelter/PMR is the direct mechanism by which AMMN captures (a) compliance with the Jan-2025 concentrate export ban (see Section 5) and (b) a refining margin previously paid to third parties. The FY2025-2026 FCF inflection (Dec-2025 and Mar-2026 quarters both turned FCF-positive per AMMN.md, +1.77tn and +1.42tn respectively) is consistent with capex rolling off as the smelter completes and revenue/margin scaling in as ramp-up progresses.
- **Insufficient evidence — requires further research**: exact total project capex (multiple partial figures cited, no single reconciled total found); quantified uplift to realized price/margin per tonne from domestic refining vs. historical concentrate export economics.

## 4. Is a low-cost, long-life copper asset a genuinely great business?

- **FACT (industry)**: Copper demand context — 2025 global demand ~27.2 million tonnes; multiple forecasters (S&P Global Jan-2026 study citing AI data-center and defense demand, ICSG) project a supply deficit re-emerging around 2026, with S&P Global flagging a "substantial shortfall" and other estimates of 500kt-1.5% of global supply by 2026; longer-dated forecasts (to 2040) show demand growing toward 42m tonnes with a potential 10m tonne deficit if new supply doesn't materialize. [S&P Global](https://press.spglobal.com/2026-01-08-Substantial-Shortfall-in-Copper-Supply-Widens-as-the-Race-for-AI-and-Growing-Defense-Spending-Add-to-Accelerating-Demand,-New-S-P-Global-Study-Finds), [TradingKey](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/commodities/metal/261726965-copper-outlook-2026-tradingkey), [Crux Investor](https://www.cruxinvestor.com/posts/from-surplus-to-scarcity-how-slower-production-growth-is-driving-a-structural-copper-deficit-by-2026)
- **INFERENCE**: This deficit narrative is now widely consensus among sell-side and industry commentary (multiple independent outlets citing the same S&P Global study in Jan 2026) — it is priced into copper equities broadly, not a proprietary insight specific to AMMN. The screener's PB of 3.29x and PE of 41x likely already reflect a chunk of this macro tailwind being extrapolated onto AMMN specifically.
- **FACT (primary source — AMMAN FY2025 Earnings Release, filed on IDX)**: AMMAN's adjusted **C1 cash cost was NEGATIVE US$(3.37)/lb copper sold in 2024**, moving to **negative US$(0.54)/lb in 2025** — i.e., gold and silver by-product credits more than fully offset the entire cash cost of copper production in both years, though the credit shrank in 2025 due to lower by-product volumes and the Phase 7→8 production transition. [AMMAN FY2025 Earnings Release, IDX filing](https://www.idx.co.id/StaticData/NewsAndAnnouncement/ANNOUNCEMENTSTOCK/From_EREP/202603/89227d4222_9f506973e0.pdf) — this **confirms and upgrades** the earlier single-broker "lowest-quartile" claim to primary-source fact: a negative net cash cost is not merely low-quartile, it is effectively the most advantaged possible position on the cost curve (by-product credits alone cover full production cost).
- **INFERENCE**: The narrowing of the negative C1 cost from -$3.37/lb (2024) to -$0.54/lb (2025) directly corroborates the phase/grade-transition story in Section 1 — Phase 7→8 transition reduced by-product (gold/silver) output, shrinking the credit. This is a real operating deterioration to track, not merely a "one bad quarter" — the company's own disclosure attributes it to the structural mine-phase transition, which should partially reverse as Phase 8 fully ramps into higher-grade zones.
- **INFERENCE**: With the negative-C1-cost claim now confirmed from primary company disclosure, Batu Hijau + Elang + integrated downstream processing is a genuinely advantaged structural position: long reserve life, gold by-product credits that (in most years) fully offset net copper cost, and a demand backdrop with real (if consensus) tailwinds. But "advantaged" and "cheap" are different claims — the market is not ignoring this story; it is paying up for it (PB 3.29x vs peer median 2.23x per the screener).
- **Jurisdiction**: **INFERENCE** — Indonesia is not a top-tier "safe" mining jurisdiction by global standards (resource nationalism, shifting export/royalty policy — see Section 5 — and a history of forced divestment/ownership disputes, as evidenced by the Newmont Nusa Tenggara → Amman Mineral Nusa Tenggara ownership transition itself). This is a real, non-trivial risk factor against the low-cost-asset thesis.
- **Insufficient evidence — requires further research**: primary-source AISC/C1 cost figure in USD/lb from AMMN's own investor materials; peer-set cost curve comparison (Freeport, Southern Copper, BHP Escondida, etc.) to verify "lowest quartile" claim independently.

## 5. Indonesian resource nationalism, downstreaming, and divestment

- **FACT**: Indonesia banned copper concentrate exports effective **January 1, 2025** (Permendag 10/2024, Permen ESDM 06/2024) as part of its downstreaming (hilirisasi) policy, after earlier planned bans (mid-2024) were postponed. Temporary relaxations were granted in 2024/early 2025 to firms with smelters >50% complete (which included AMMAN). [Indonesia Business Post](https://indonesiabusinesspost.com/3447/policy/finance-ministry-affirms-ban-on-copper-concentrate-exports-starting-2025), [Indonesia Miner](https://indonesiaminer.com/news/detail/2024-11-05232111-copper-concentrate-export-ban-set-for-2025-ministry-of-industry-announces-expectations)
- **FACT**: A comparable, larger domestic peer (Freeport Indonesia) received extended export permits into 2025 under a higher export tax (on top of a 7.5% duty) as a bridge while its own smelter ramped — illustrating that Jakarta actively uses export permits/duties as policy levers for miners, not a fixed, predictable regime. [Jakarta Post](https://www.thejakartapost.com/opinion/2025/03/28/analysis-freeport-copper-ore-export-permit-against-downstream-policy)
- **INFERENCE**: AMMAN's own smelter completion (see Section 3) means it is now largely insulated from the concentrate-export-ban risk that hit peers — this is a structural positive versus a miner without domestic processing. But it also means AMMN's economics are now hostage to whatever domestic refining margin, royalty, and tax regime the government sets, since export optionality is largely closed off.
- **FACT (divestment law)**: Indonesian mining law (as amended, most recently via 2025 amendments per secondary sources) requires IUP/IUPK Operation Production holders to reduce foreign ownership to a maximum 49% (51% domestic) over time, though the rigid "by year 10" timeline was replaced with a staged approach whose specifics depend on subsequent regulation. [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/b545e479/indonesia-amends-the-mining-law), [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/mining/1440058/foreign-mining-divestment-requirements)
- **FACT**: The 51% domestic divestment requirement was satisfied historically — Amman Mineral Internasional (the Medco/Panigoro-led consortium with AP Investment) acquired an 82.2% stake in the former PT Newmont Nusa Tenggara for USD 2.6bn, finalized November 2, 2016, explicitly to meet the government's divest-51%-and-build-a-smelter condition for CoW-to-IUPK conversion and continued export rights. [Jakarta Post, Nov 2016](https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2016/11/04/medco-officially-takes-over-newmonts-arm), [Jakarta Post, Feb 2017 — CoW-to-IUPK conversion approved](https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2017/02/11/indonesia-approves-freeport-amman-contract-conversion.html)
- **INFERENCE**: Because Indonesian ownership was already secured well before the 2023 IPO, AMMN does not appear to face an outstanding *foreign* divestment overhang today — the current ownership structure (Section 6) is already >95% Indonesian-domiciled entities. Residual regulatory risk is therefore less about a looming forced-divestment event and more about the general policy-instability pattern documented above (shifting export permits/duties, royalty terms) that Jakarta has applied even to compliant, domestically-owned miners like Freeport Indonesia.
- **Insufficient evidence — requires further research**: current royalty rate applicable to AMMN post any 2025 mining law/royalty amendments; whether any further divestment tranche (e.g., regional government participation rights, which IUPK terms sometimes carry alongside the 51% national threshold) remains outstanding.

## 6. Governance and ownership structure

- **FACT**: Pre/at-IPO major shareholders (Jan 2023 disclosure): PT Sumber Gemilang Persada 35.6%, PT Medco Energi Internasional Tbk (MEDC) 23.1%, PT AP Investment 17.1%, plus PT Alpha Investasi Mandiri 7.9%, PT Pesona Sukses Cemerlang 7.2%, PT Sumber Mineral Citra Nusantara 5.1%, PT Medco Service Indonesia 4%. [IDN Financials](https://www.idnfinancials.com/news/49042/purchasing-15-billion-new-shares-of-amman-mineral-ammn-medco-energi-internasional-controls-20-92-of-the-stakes)
- **FACT**: More recent figures show PT Sumber Gemilang Persada 32.17%, Medco Energi 20.92%, AP Investment 15.45% (dilution consistent with post-IPO share issuance, e.g., Medco's follow-on purchase of 15bn new shares reported by IDN Financials).
- **FACT**: PT Amman Mineral Internasional is described as under Salim Group control/influence, evidenced by increased stake and board appointments, layered on top of the Medco (Panigoro family) stake. [Indonesia Business Post](https://indonesiabusinesspost.com/824/Politics/unveiling-salim-groups-strategic-vision-for-amman-minerals-ipo)
- **FACT**: Public free float post-IPO (IPO date 2023-07-07 per AMMN.md) was reported at **17.3%**.
- **INFERENCE**: This is a tightly controlled group structure — three or four related domestic conglomerate/family vehicles (Sumber Gemilang Persada / Salim-linked, Medco/Panigoro, AP Investment) hold roughly 68-75%+ of the company, with public float in the high-teens percent. This concentrates governance risk: minority shareholders have limited practical influence, and related-party transactions between AMMN and Medco/Salim-affiliated entities (e.g., shared logistics, energy, or trading arrangements) are structurally more likely and harder for outside shareholders to police.
- **INFERENCE re: PB 3.29x**: Given (a) low free float amplifying volatility and scarcity premium, (b) the market pricing in the Elang optionality and copper macro tailwind discussed in Section 4, and (c) the smelter/downstream integration story, the market is paying for growth optionality and control-group credibility (Medco is an established, respected operator on IDX) rather than for current book earning power — current ROE is only 8.0% (screener) against a 3.29x P/B, which is not obviously justified by ROE alone and rests on forward assumptions (Elang, cost curve, gold price) not yet in the trailing financials.
- **Insufficient evidence — requires further research**: specific disclosed related-party transactions (RPT) in AMMN's annual report/financial statements (e.g., intercompany loans, offtake or service agreements with Medco Energi, logistics/fuel supply); any history of minority-shareholder disputes or OJK enforcement actions; governance scorecard (independent commissioner ratio, audit committee composition).
