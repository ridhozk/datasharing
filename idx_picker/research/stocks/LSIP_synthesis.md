## Resolving the disagreement

The spread is wide and genuine: Valuation says UNDERVALUED at 72/100, the Thesis
Killer vetoes. Averaging would be meaningless. They differ on **one** question.

**Both agree the assets are worth more than the market cap.** Crediting cash at
*zero*, LSIP trades at ~USD 7,100 per mature hectare against MP Evans' July-2025
Indonesian transaction at USD 12,600/ha. That is not in dispute.

**They differ on whether a minority shareholder can ever reach that value.** Agent 2
values the estate as an asset; Agent 5 values what the holder actually receives. On the
evidence assembled, Agent 5 is right, for three reasons that are facts rather than
interpretation:

1. **The asset is being consumed, not held.** Agents 3 and 5 arrived independently at
   the same conclusion from different disclosures. Immature oil palm is 6,054 ha of
   90,798 (6.7%) and has fallen every year since 2021 — implying **~1,250–2,018 ha/yr of
   replanting against a 3,646 ha/yr steady-state requirement**, a 45–73 year cycle on a
   25-year crop. Nucleus FFB yield has fallen five years running to **12–13.4 t/ha against
   peers at 20–24**. Planted area has shrunk 4.3% since 2021. A per-hectare comp assumes a
   maintained estate; this one is not being maintained, which is exactly why Agent 2 had
   to apply a 50% haircut to the transaction comp.

2. **The discount is widening, not closing.** Book value compounded **8%/yr while the
   price compounded 0.98%/yr** over five years. The stock sits in the **69th percentile of
   its own 10-year range** — permanently repriced, not depressed. A discount that has
   failed to close for a decade while the underlying compounds is a structural verdict.

3. **The distribution mechanism was tested and failed.** This is the decisive fact. The
   25 June 2026 AGM approved IDR 83/share at a **29.99% payout while cash rose to
   IDR 8.37tn**. The payout was *cut* from ~35% (FY2021–23) to 30.0% to 29.99% precisely
   as earnings and cash doubled. FY2025 dividends were **17.2% of free cash flow**.
   Rp4,171bn of five-year retained earnings went into bank deposits earning 4.23% — of
   which **Rp1.64tn (10.55% of assets) sits at PT Bank Ina Perdana, a Salim-affiliated
   related party, up 81% year on year**. No Indonesian law or IDX rule can force
   distribution. At a 5.67% dividend yield the holder is paid **less than BI's 5.75%
   policy rate** to wait.

The cash is 78–84% of market cap. If it is not distributable, the investment is the
plantation alone — worth ~IDR 1,062/share against a IDR 1,465 price.

**A correction to the screener came out of this and has been fixed.** Agents 1 and 2
independently found that the screener's EBIT was Yahoo's `PretaxIncome + InterestExpense`,
folding ~IDR 276–447bn of interest income into operating profit. Normalised EBIT was
26.8% too high, and EPV valued the cash at ~124% of face by adding it *and* capitalising
the interest it earns. Fixed universe-wide; 8 names left the BUY bucket as a result.

## 1. Investment classification

**Asset value / controlled-company discount — with high value-trap risk.**
Not a compounder: nucleus volumes are flat to falling and all 2025 earnings growth came
from price. Not a clean net-net: the assets are real but illiquid and controlled.

## 2. One-line thesis

> The market is pricing LSIP as a cash box it will never open, and on the evidence the
> market is right — the plantation alone is worth ~IDR 1,062 against a IDR 1,465 price,
> and the 49% margin of safety exists only if you credit cash the controlling shareholder
> has spent five years demonstrating it will not distribute.

## 3. Why is it cheap?

- 84% of market cap is cash the parent retains at an *increasing* rate.
- Nucleus FFB yield 12–13.4 t/ha against peers at 20–24; five straight years of decline.
- Replanting at roughly half to a third of the steady-state requirement.
- 63–96% of sales to a 59.5% parent with no disclosed independent pricing verification.
- The export levy (12.5% from March 2026, a USD 276.69/t wedge ≈ 26.9% of reference
  price) transfers the upcycle away from upstream growers: LSIP's realised price rose 8%
  in 2025 against Rotterdam's 15%.

## 4. What is the market missing?

**Very little — and that is the finding.** The asset backing is real and the balance
sheet is genuinely clean (bearer plants at cost, biological fair value confined to
Rp245.7bn, zero funded debt for five years, audited CFO tying exactly to the screener).
The market is not mispricing the assets; it is correctly pricing the *access* to them.

## 5. What changes?

Nothing identified inside twelve months. The next AGM window is ~June 2027. A renewed
take-private at the IndoAgri layer is the one live possibility (2019 precedent at S$0.28
failed; fresh March-2026 analyst speculation) — but the 2019 attempt was a *low-ball*, so
that catalyst plausibly caps the upside rather than releasing it.

## 6–8. Macro / Industry / Micro

**Macro: marginal TAILWIND (60/100)** — CPO Oct-26 at RM 4,678/t (7 Aug 2026), B50 in
force from 1 July 2026, weak IDR ~17,900 — heavily clawed back by the levy.
**Industry: NEUTRAL (50/100)** — Malaysian July stocks at a five-month high of 2.63mn t
on +9.4% m/m output; EUDR delayed again to 30 Dec 2026 (deferred risk, not catalyst).
**Micro: HEADWIND.** 1H2026 gross margin 32% vs 40%; core profit +2% yoy against a
reported +25%.

## 9. Fundamental earnings engine

CPO price × own-estate FFB volume, less cash costs, less a levy wedge of ~27% of
reference price. Volume is flat-to-declining and structurally so; the engine is therefore
**price alone**. Reported growth flatters this: the +25% H1 2026 headline is +2% core,
and FY2025's +29.5% operating surge is +12.2% once the FY2024 rubber-CGU impairment
(Rp296bn) and write-offs (Rp138bn) are removed from the base.

## 12. Downside

Not a solvency risk — zero debt, real assets. The downside is **the asset eroding while
you hold it**: under-replanting at half the required rate compounds a yield deficit that
is already 40% below peers, so the estate's value decays even if CPO holds.

## 13. Thesis-breaker

Already broken. Formally, re-entry requires **any one of**: a declared payout ≥60%;
disclosed replanting ≥3,000 ha/yr; a firm take-private at a full price; or a price below
**IDR 1,100**, where the plantation alone provides the margin of safety.

## 14. Research gaps

- Actual tree-age bracket table — not disclosed anywhere; the whole replanting analysis is
  inferred from immature-area ratios.
- Independent verification of transfer pricing on the 63–96% of revenue sold to SIMP.
- Terms and counterparty risk of the Rp1.64tn held at a related-party bank.
- Whether the March-2026 take-private speculation has substance.

## Verdict

**ELIMINATE from the portfolio; retain on a price-triggered watchlist.**
Downgraded from the screener's BUY. Thesis Killer veto upheld.

This is the cleanest value trap the screen has produced: a real, unencumbered asset,
honestly accounted for, that a minority shareholder cannot reach — trading at a discount
that has widened for a decade while book value compounded eight times faster than the
price.

The screener's 49% margin of safety was a measurement error compounded by a governance
assumption. The measurement error is now fixed universe-wide. The governance assumption
was never the screener's to make.

**Actionable: no position. Set an alert at IDR 1,100, and a second at any RUPS
announcement proposing a payout above 60%.**
