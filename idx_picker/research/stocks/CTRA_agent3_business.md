## Business Type

**FACT.** CTRA divides itself into two segments (AR 2025 p.39):

1. **Residential development** — township-scale (kota mandiri) projects selling land
   plots, landed houses, shophouses, apartments and strata offices. 70 of the 90
   projects. 2025 revenue **Rp10,259.5bn = 81.3% of total**, gross margin **48.6%**
   (2024: 47.1%).
2. **Commercial property development & management** — mall leasing, hotels, rental
   apartments, offices, hospitals/clinics, golf, waterpark, theme park. 2025 revenue
   **Rp2,357.2bn = 18.7% of total**, gross margin **42.8%** (2024: 46.9%, so recurring
   margin *fell* 4.2pp).

**FACT.** How a developer actually gets paid here: CTRA sells on a **presales
(marketing sales)** basis — cash comes in before construction finishes and funds the
build. Three payment methods: cash, bank mortgage (KPR/KPA), and in-house instalment.
On a KPR sale CTRA receives 90–100% of presale value at loan signing, of which up to
90% is immediately usable and 10% sits in escrow until the deed (AJB) is signed.
Revenue is recognised only on handover, so **reported revenue lags presales by roughly
one to two years** — 2026's reported P&L is being written by 2024–25 presales.

**FACT — the single most important structural fact about this business.** ~61% of
presales come through **joint operations (KSO)**, not owned land. Under the KSO scheme
the partner contributes the land, CTRA does planning, construction, marketing and
sales. 2025 presales split: direct ownership Rp3,711bn / joint operation Rp5,750bn
(2024: Rp4,342bn / Rp6,675bn). JO is used mainly for projects outside Jakarta and
Surabaya.

**INFERENCE.** This is a genuinely capital-lighter model than the classic Indonesian
land-banking developer. CTRA buys strategic land access without the multi-year carry
cost and permit risk, which is why it can operate 90 projects in 34 cities on a
Rp48tn balance sheet. The price is a permanent leak of profit: minority interest is
~Rp200bn/yr (about 8% of pre-minority profit) and there is Rp1,532.6bn of "joint
operation partner receivables" on the balance sheet (2024: Rp1,310.5bn) — capital
lent to partners that shows up as a receivable, not as land.

**FACT.** What drives margin and ROIC: gross margin is set by land cost basis
(historical, often decades old at CitraRaya Tangerang / CitraLand Surabaya) versus
current selling price, and CTRA runs **phased development**, releasing clusters into
demand so it can reprice. That is why gross margin has held at 47–49% through a
demand collapse. ROIC is set by *velocity*, not margin — and velocity is terrible:
days inventory 706, cash conversion cycle 662 days (screener). A 48% gross margin
earned once every two years on capital is a ~10% ROE business. That is exactly what
CTRA reports: ROE 5Y average 9.9%, TTM 11.1%.

**THESIS.** The reason CTRA trades at 0.48x book is not that the market thinks the
land is fake. It is that **through-cycle ROE (~10%) sits below Indonesian cost of
equity (INFERENCE: risk-free ~6.5–7% at BI rate 5.75%, plus 5–6% ERP → 12–13%)**. A
business that earns less than its cost of capital is *rationally* worth less than
book. Any thesis on CTRA must explain why that gap closes, or must be an explicit
asset-liquidation/re-rating argument rather than a compounding argument.

## Industry Structure

**Demand.** FACT: 2025 presales fell 14.1% industry-wide for CTRA (Rp11,017.4bn →
Rp9,461.1bn) on "weakening purchasing power across most regions." H1 2026 presales
Rp4.69tn, **-18% yoy**. H1 2026 revenue Rp5.19tn (-11.7% yoy), net profit Rp1.10tn
(-11.2% yoy).

**Credit.** FACT: landed-housing KPR **NPL reached 3.56% in Aug 2025, the highest
since June 2020** (2.56%) — company-cited. FACT: **~70% of CTRA presales are
mortgage-financed**, and that dependence has *risen*: mortgage share of payment mix
went 55% (2021) → 62% → 63% → 70% (2024) → 72% (est. 9M25), while cash held ~19–23%
and in-house instalment collapsed 22% → 9%.

**INFERENCE — this is the key industry mechanic.** CTRA has become structurally *more*
rate-sensitive over the last five years, not less. Rising KPR NPLs cause banks to
tighten approvals, which throttles CTRA's sales funnel independently of consumer
desire. Company explicitly flags this in its 2026 outlook.

**Monetary policy.** FACT: Bank Indonesia **raised BI-Rate 25bp to 5.75%** and held it
there at the 21–22 July 2026 RDG. Rupiah has weakened past **Rp17,800/USD**; June 2026
inflation 3.34% yoy (up from 3.08% in May), approaching the upper 2.5%±1% band. FACT:
CTRA's own AR 2025, written when BI-Rate was 4.75%, assumed a "still-favorable
macroeconomic environment" — that assumption has since broken. FACT: in June 2026
management publicly opened the door to revising the 2026 marketing-sales target.

**Regulation / policy.** FACT: the **PPN DTP** (VAT borne by government) incentive was
extended to **31 December 2026** — 100% VAT relief on ready-to-occupy new homes up to
Rp2bn, and relief on the first Rp2bn of price for homes Rp2–5bn (PMK 13/2025, PMK
60/2025). FACT: PPN DTP contributed **Rp2,705.0bn to 2025 presales = 28.6% of the
total**, and management explicitly plans to "maximise" it again in 2026 via
accelerated construction of eligible units.

**THESIS.** Nearly 30% of presales rest on a tax incentive with an announced expiry
date. It has been rolled repeatedly since 2021 so extension is the base case, but
this is a policy crutch, not a competitive advantage, and it creates a genuine cliff
risk into 2027. It also distorts CTRA's product mix towards sub-Rp5bn units at
precisely the moment the company says it wants to move upmarket.

**Secular demand.** FACT: BPS reports households without homes fell from 12.75m (2020)
to **9.29m (2026)**; ownership backlog ratio 17.52% → 12.39%. The government's
3-million-homes programme had delivered 324,213 units by mid-June 2026 against a
3m/yr ambition. INFERENCE: the backlog is overwhelmingly a *subsidised/low-income*
phenomenon. CTRA's 2025 presales mix was Rp2–5bn (44.2%) and Rp1–2bn (28.8%), with
sub-Rp1bn down to ~11% of mix from 25% in 2021. **The national housing backlog is a
real secular tailwind for Indonesia, but it does not accrue to CTRA's price points.**
Do not use it as a CTRA bull argument.

**Supply / competition.** FACT: fragmented listed field — BSDE, SMRA, PWON, ASRI,
LPKR, MTLA, DMAS and dozens of unlisted regional developers. FACT: on 2025 target
achievement, **BSDE and SMRA exceeded their marketing-sales targets; CTRA achieved 95%
and PWON 83%.** 2026 targets are uniformly conservative (BSDE Rp10tn, CTRA Rp9.5tn).
INFERENCE: no player has pricing power over the cycle; competition is at the level of
individual catchments, and CTRA's advantage is location-by-location, not sector-wide.

## Competitive Advantage

**FACT.** Scale and track record: founded 1981, listed 1994, **90 residential and
commercial projects across 34 cities**; townships developed spanning **>5,700
hectares**; **>160,000 housing and apartment units sold**; ~155,000 sqm of office
developed. Management describes CTRA as "the most diversified property company in
terms of geographic location, products and target buyer" (AR 2025 p.40) — INFERENCE:
that claim is defensible against BSDE (Serpong-concentrated), SMRA (Jakarta/Bandung/
Bekasi), and PWON (Surabaya/Jakarta malls).

**FACT.** 2025 presales by geography: Greater Jakarta 48.2% (**+2.0% yoy**), Greater
Surabaya 21.9% (**-20.2%**), Sumatra 10.1% (**-53.8%**, normalising after the CitraGarden
City Sampali Medan launch), other Java 9.1%, Sulawesi 8.0%, others 2.9%.

**INFERENCE — the diversification argument is weaker than it looks.** In the 2025–26
downturn, geographic diversification did not stabilise the book; it diversified CTRA
*into* the weakest regional markets while the resilient Jakarta core carried the
result. Diversification is a growth-optionality asset in an upcycle and roughly
neutral in a downcycle. It is not a defensive moat.

**FACT.** Brand: CitraLand / CitraGarden / Citra City are used as the naming system
across all 34 cities; top 2025 projects were CitraGarden Bintaro (Rp1,359.7bn),
CitraLand Surabaya (Rp1,294.9bn), CitraGarden Serpong (Rp885.1bn), CitraGarden City
Jakarta (Rp665.8bn), Citra City Sentul (Rp570.0bn). New launches generated Rp3,834.5bn
of presales at a **50% take-up rate** on 4,126 units offered.

**INFERENCE.** A 50% take-up on new launches in the worst demand year since 2020 is
respectable and indicates the brand does carry real trust in secondary cities, where
buyer fear of an abandoned project is the binding constraint. This is CTRA's most
credible genuine advantage: **a 45-year completion record is the currency that lets it
sell off-plan in Makassar, Medan and Palembang, where an unknown developer cannot.**

**FACT.** Recurring-asset quality: Lotte Group leases ~79,500 sqm at Ciputra World
Jakarta 1; JLL manages CWJ1 retail/office; Raffles and The Ascott run hotels/serviced
apartments under long-term management agreements. Healthcare was the only recurring
line growing in 9M25 (+11.0% yoy to Rp519bn) while malls (-3.3%), hotels (-3.0%),
offices (-8.3%) and golf (-5.1%) all shrank.

**INFERENCE.** The recurring portfolio is decent but sub-scale and — critically — was
*not* countercyclical in 2025. It shrank alongside development. This undercuts the
standard "recurring income is the stabiliser" argument. Only healthcare behaved
defensively, and management is leaning into it (target +300 hospital beds, +81%, by
2029).

**FACT.** Land bank capacity: Land for Development Rp9,906.2bn (2024: Rp9,590.6bn;
2023: Rp7,553.3bn); inventories Rp13,059.6bn. CitraRaya Tangerang alone spans >2,700
hectares. A conditional Rp1,236.4bn purchase of ~165 ha from EMDE at Rp750k/sqm is
recorded within Land for Development. Land is carried at historical cost.

**INFERENCE.** Historical-cost carrying is the core of the asset-value case — a
decades-old Surabaya/Tangerang land basis is worth a multiple of book. Brokers put
CTRA at a ~73% discount to 2025F RNAV. This is real but it is *unmonetisable except
through 20 years of development*, which is precisely the slow-velocity problem that
caps ROE. The discount to RNAV and the sub-COE ROE are two descriptions of the same
fact, not two separate reasons to buy.

## Management

**FACT.** Ownership: **PT Sang Pelopor 53.31%** (9.88bn shares, as at 30 Sep 2025);
affiliates of the controller 3.49% (648m shares); public 42.83% (7.94bn). Ultimate
beneficial owners are the Ciputra family: Rina Ciputra Sastrawinata, Junita Ciputra,
Candra Ciputra, Cakra Ciputra. Founder Dr. (HC) Ir. Ciputra died in 2019.

**FACT.** Second generation runs the company operationally: Candra Ciputra has been
President Director since December 2002 (joined 1988); Cakra Ciputra is MD Division 3;
Rina and Junita sit on the Board of Commissioners. Long-serving non-family directors
include Budiarsa Sastrawinata, Harun Hajadi, Sutoto Yakobus and Nanik Santoso; Tulus
Santoso Brotosiswojo is Independent Director / Corporate Secretary (~10.5 yrs).

**FACT — governance, and it is better than the IDX family-conglomerate norm.** As at
31 Dec 2025, total **due from related parties Rp25.598bn (0.05% of assets)** and **due
to related parties Rp20.020bn (0.09% of liabilities)**. The AR states no related-party
transaction in 2025 involved a conflict of interest and none required independent
shareholder approval under OJK affiliated-transaction rules.

**FACT.** The 2017 merger folded the two separately listed subsidiaries — PT Ciputra
Surya (CTRS, listed 1999) and PT Ciputra Property (CTRP, listed 2007) — into CTRA,
including a buyback of 24,608,142 shares at Rp1,350 from dissenting holders.

**INFERENCE.** The 2017 merger removed the structural conflict that plagues Indonesian
family groups — three listed vehicles in one family with overlapping assets and
constant scope for value transfer between minority pools. Combined with the trivially
small related-party balances, **minority-shareholder treatment at CTRA is a strength,
not a risk.** That is a meaningful and under-appreciated positive.

**FACT.** Guidance credibility, 2025: presales target Rp10,000bn → realised Rp9,461.1bn
(94.6%); revenue target Rp12,285.9bn → Rp12,616.7bn (102.7%); net profit target
Rp2,445.2bn → Rp2,663.1bn (108.9%); net gearing target ≤0.3x → **-11.1% (net cash)**.
Note the presales target itself had already been revised down from Rp11,000bn.

**INFERENCE.** Management guides honestly on the P&L and conservatively on the balance
sheet, and pre-announced a bad 2026 (revenue -5–10%, residential -10–15%, commercial
flat to +5%, net profit -10–15%) rather than letting the market find out. That is a
governance positive and materially reduces the risk of a nasty surprise.

**Insufficient evidence — requires further research:** no formal published succession
plan for Candra Ciputra (b. ~1958), and no disclosure of a designated third-generation
successor. For a 53%-controlled family business this is a real unquantified risk.

## Capital Allocation

**FACT.** 2025 capex Rp947.0bn, **down 64.6%** from Rp2,678.3bn in 2024. Breakdown:
land for development Rp628.3bn (2024: Rp2,224.3bn, **-71.8%**), property & equipment
Rp308.7bn, investment properties Rp10.0bn. 2026 capex guided at ~Rp1tn, "mostly to
strengthen the land bank rather than expand into new locations."

**INFERENCE — this is the most important capital-allocation observation, and it cuts
against the bull case.** CTRA holds a net cash balance sheet in the worst property
downturn since 2020, i.e. exactly the moment when land should be cheapest and
distressed sellers most numerous. It responded by **cutting land acquisition by 72%**.
That is prudent risk management, but it is emphatically *not* counter-cyclical land
buying. The optionality that the fortress balance sheet theoretically provides is not
being exercised. Do not underwrite CTRA on "they can buy land counter-cyclically"
unless management demonstrates it.

**FACT.** Dividend policy: **maximum 30% of consolidated net profit after tax**,
unchanged. 2025 cash dividend Rp444.9bn (Rp24/share) paid July 2025 vs Rp389.2bn prior
year; Rp667bn declared from 2025 net profit. Screener payout ratio 26.5%, dividend
yield 5.76% at Rp630.

**FACT.** No dilution — diluted share count flat at 18.5bn across 2022–2025 (Piotroski
"No Dilution" = 1). No current share buyback programme was found.

**THESIS.** With the stock at **0.48x book and 4.3x earnings**, a rupiah spent
repurchasing CTRA shares buys ~Rp2.10 of book value and a ~23% earnings yield, versus
~10% ROE on land. **Buying back stock is roughly twice as accretive as buying land at
these prices, and management is doing neither aggressively — it is accumulating cash.**
Cash rose to Rp8.6tn (Q2 2026) with net debt at -Rp662bn. This is the clearest
identifiable gap between what management does and what a value-maximising allocator
would do, and it is the most plausible catalyst if it ever changes. THESIS, not FACT —
there is no evidence management is considering it.

## Industry Outlook

**Near term (12–24 months): negative.** FACT: BI-Rate 5.75% and on hold with a weak
rupiah (Rp17,800) constraining any easing; landed-housing KPR NPL at a 5-year high;
70% KPR dependence; H1 2026 presales -18%; company guiding revenue -5–10% and profit
-10–15%. INFERENCE: the earnings trough is 2026–27, because revenue recognition lags
presales — even a presales recovery starting in 2027 would not show in reported
revenue until 2028.

**Policy: knife-edge.** FACT: PPN DTP runs to 31 Dec 2026 and supplied 28.6% of 2025
presales. ASSUMPTION: given it has been extended every year since 2021 and is a
politically cheap consumption stimulus, extension into 2027 is more likely than not —
but this is an assumption, and non-extension would be a material downgrade.

**Medium term: genuinely cyclical, not structurally impaired.** INFERENCE: Indonesian
property is a rate-and-purchasing-power cycle, not a Chinese-style oversupply
unwind. There is no evidence of a national inventory glut in CTRA's segments, no
developer default cascade, no presold-but-undelivered scandal, and mortgage
penetration remains low by regional standards. When BI eases and real wages recover,
presales recover — CTRA's own 2020→2024 record (Rp5,493bn → Rp11,017bn, +19% CAGR)
shows the amplitude of the recovery leg.

**Long term: mediocre.** THESIS: the structural ROE ceiling does not move. Land
velocity, phased development, and the necessity of holding decades of inventory cap
this at ~10–12% ROE at the top of the cycle and ~8% at the bottom. Recurring income at
18.7% of revenue and shrinking in 2025 is not yet large enough to change the character
of the business, and management's own 2026 plan is to spend capex on land, not on
recurring assets.

**Industry: HEADWIND**

Rates rising and on hold at 5.75%, rupiah at Rp17,800, mortgage NPLs at a 5-year high,
70% mortgage dependence rising, purchasing power weak in the sub-Rp2bn segment, and
~29% of presales resting on a VAT incentive expiring 31 Dec 2026. The one genuine
tailwind — a 9.29m-household backlog and urbanisation — sits below CTRA's price points.

**Company: NEUTRAL**

Best-in-class balance sheet (net cash, Z 4.99, no dilution) and the widest geographic
footprint on the IDX, offset by mid-pack downturn execution (95% of a *revised-down*
2025 target, versus BSDE and SMRA beating theirs) and a defensive rather than
opportunistic capital-allocation stance. CTRA is not losing share; it is not gaining
it either.

**Structural Advantages**

1. **A 45-year, 160,000-unit completion record across 34 cities.** In Indonesian
   off-plan property the binding customer constraint is fear of an unfinished project;
   CTRA's brand is the collateral that lets it presell in secondary cities where
   unknown developers cannot. This is the one advantage that is hard to replicate.
   (FACT on the record; INFERENCE on the mechanism.)
2. **The joint-operation model (61% of presales).** Partner supplies land, CTRA
   supplies development capability and brand — strategic land access without the
   carry cost, which is why 90 projects fit on a Rp48tn balance sheet. Capital
   efficiency an owned-land developer cannot match. (FACT.)
3. **A net-cash balance sheet in a leveraged industry** (net gearing -11.1%, interest
   cover 45x, Altman Z 4.99, no dilution 2022–25). Guarantees survival of the cycle
   and removes the forced-seller / rights-issue risk that kills most EM developers.
   Combined with negligible related-party balances (0.05% of assets) and the 2017
   collapse of three listed vehicles into one, minorities are treated well. (FACT.)

**Structural Problems**

1. **Through-cycle ROE (~9–10%) sits below cost of equity (~12–13%).** Slow inventory
   velocity — 706 days inventory, 662-day cash conversion cycle — means a 48% gross
   margin converts into a single-digit-to-low-double-digit return. A sub-COE business
   is *rationally* worth less than book, so the P/B discount need never close. This is
   the reason CTRA has been cheap for years and can stay cheap. (FACT on inputs,
   THESIS on conclusion.)
2. **Rising, not falling, dependence on mortgages and on a temporary tax incentive.**
   KPR share of payment mix 55% (2021) → 72% (9M25); ~70% of presales KPR-financed;
   PPN DTP supplied 28.6% of 2025 presales and expires 31 Dec 2026. CTRA has become
   *more* macro- and policy-levered over five years, not less. (FACT.)
3. **Capital allocation is defensive when it should be offensive.** Land capex cut 72%
   in the trough; cash piling to Rp8.6tn; no buyback at 0.48x book where repurchase is
   ~2x as accretive as land. Retaining ~73% of earnings to reinvest at ~10% ROE
   compounds book value slowly and does nothing to close the discount. Recurring
   income (18.7% of revenue) shrank in 2025 alongside development, so it is not yet
   the stabiliser management describes. (FACT on the numbers, THESIS on the critique.)

**Business Verdict: CYCLICAL**

(with a substantial secondary ASSET VALUE character — land at historical cost,
~73% discount to broker RNAV — which is real but only monetisable over decades of
development, not through any identifiable near-term event.)

Explicitly **not a value trap in the balance-sheet sense**: no leverage stress, no
dilution, no fraud markers, no related-party looting, honest guidance, real cash
dividends at a 5.8% yield. But equally **not a compounder**: the returns on capital do
not support it, and management is not allocating to change that.

**Answer to the key question:** *a decent-quality, honestly-run, financially fortress
CYCLICAL that is mediocre as a business and is currently cheap for identifiable
cyclical reasons — rates, purchasing power and mortgage credit — rather than
structural decline.* The right frame is "average business, very cheap, at or near a
cyclical trough," not "good business temporarily mispriced." Whether that is
investable depends entirely on whether the entry price discounts a trough-earnings,
sub-COE business — which is Agent 2's question, not mine. The trap risk here is not
insolvency; it is **duration**: a stock that is statistically cheap, structurally
sub-COE, and reliant on a rate cut that BI has signalled it is in no hurry to deliver.

**Watch items that would change this assessment**
- A share buyback authorisation, or a step-change in land acquisition into weakness —
  either would upgrade Capital Allocation and the verdict materially.
- PPN DTP extension (or not) into 2027 — a ~29%-of-presales swing factor.
- BI easing cycle beginning, and the landed-housing KPR NPL rolling over from 3.56%.
- Recurring income crossing ~30% of revenue with the healthcare build-out — that
  would begin to change the business's character, not just its mix.
