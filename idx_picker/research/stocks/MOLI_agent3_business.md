## 1. What MOLI actually makes and sells (Priority 1)

**FACT.** MOLI is the holding company (listed 2018, 34th IDX issuer) for **Molindo
Group**, a Malang / East Java operator founded 1965 (some sources say 1959).
Operating subsidiaries: PT Molindo Raya Industrial (ethanol plant), PT Molindo
Inti Gas (liquid CO2 plant), PT Sumber Kita Indah (trading/distribution).
[bareksa.com](https://www.bareksa.com/en/text/2018/04/25/produsen-ethanol-food-grade-terbesar-di-indonesia-bakal-go-public-tahun-ini/19118/news),
[kominfo.jatimprov.go.id](https://kominfo.jatimprov.go.id/berita/kembangkan-produk-ethanol-madusari-murni-indah-jadi-emiten-ke-34-di-bei)

**FACT.** Product lines sold under the Molindo brand: **Molindo Ethanol**
(industrial/technical grade and food grade), **Molindo CO2** (liquid carbon
dioxide, a co-product of fermentation), and **Molindo Fertilizer** (a
stillage/vinasse-derived by-product). End markets cited by the company: food
& beverage, pharmaceutical, cosmetics, cigarette, hospital, furniture, ink/
printing, fuel, and welding industries — i.e. a genuinely diversified
industrial-chemical customer base, not a single commodity offtake.
[idnfinancials.com](https://www.idnfinancials.com/moli/pt-madusari-murni-indah-tbk)

**FACT.** Reported annual ethanol production capacity ≈ **80,000 kilolitres/year**.
**INFERENCE** (self-reported via press, not independently verified): Molindo
Group claims ~55% of the *national* ethanol market; ~40% of MOLI's total sales
are exported. Capacity utilisation and a rated-vs-actual breakdown were not
found — **insufficient evidence — requires further research** (would need the
annual report's operational review section).

**Margin durability read:** the product mix (food-grade ethanol, pharma/cosmetic
grade, CO2) is more defensible than pure industrial-grade commodity alcohol, but
**FACT** from independent commentary: "MOLI's profit margin is highly unstable
because ethanol is a commodity-like product... the company has limited ability
to control selling prices due to minimal product differentiation between
ethanol producers," and MOLI is additionally exposed to coal cost swings (likely
boiler fuel for distillation). [Arvest analysis via search summary]
This directly undercuts the thesis that food/pharma grade insulates margins —
treat the "premium grade = sticky margin" framing as **THESIS**, not fact, until
a grade-level margin breakdown is found (not located in this search pass).

## 2. The input side — molasses (Priority 2)

**FACT.** Molasses (tetes tebu), a by-product of sugar milling, is MOLI's main
feedstock. **FACT**, dated 2022–2024: press coverage explicitly ties MOLI's
cost base to El Niño-driven sugar-cane harvest shortfalls, which pushed molasses
prices up and forced the company to keep 2022's targets conservative despite
staying expansion-minded, and again flagged expected molasses supply declines
into the 2024 harvest season.
[market.bisnis.com](https://market.bisnis.com/read/20220725/192/1559068/bisnis-etanol-tertekan-moli-pasang-target-konservatif)

**INFERENCE / gap.** No evidence found that MOLI has *integrated* upstream
sugar-mill ownership or captive molasses contracts — all signals point to MOLI
buying molasses on the open market from third-party sugar mills, which is a
structural disadvantage relative to the state sugar-milling group (see §4).
Current (2026) molasses spot pricing was **not found** in this pass —
**insufficient evidence — requires further research** (would need Kemendag/
sugar-association price bulletins or the FY2026 interim financial statement
notes on cost of goods sold).

**FACT, additional context found.** Indonesia's 2026 sugar balance: government
projects domestic *consumption-sugar* production of 2.7–3.0 million tons against
2.836 million tons of consumption demand, with **zero imports for consumption
sugar** in 2026 (only industrial raw sugar is imported, ~3.124 million tons,
which is a different market — refineries, not cane mills). This means
molasses availability for MOLI is tied to **domestic cane-crush volume**, not
to imported sugar, so weather/harvest-driven swings in the domestic cane crop
(the El Niño dynamic already observed in 2022-24) remain the dominant driver of
molasses supply and price — the government's sugar self-sufficiency push does
not obviously de-risk MOLI's feedstock cost. [tempo.co](https://www.tempo.co/ekonomi/indonesia-akan-impor-gula-industri-3-124-juta-ton-pada-2026-2103186),
[badanpangan.go.id](https://badanpangan.go.id/blog/post/ketok-neraca-komoditas-2026-pemerintah-pastikan-tak-ada-impor-beras-dan-gula-untuk-konsumsi-serta-jagung-pakan)

**The ethanol-price/molasses-cost spread is the entire business**, exactly as
flagged in the task brief. The annual history shows the mechanism directly:
OperatingIncome collapsed from 109.5bn (2023) to 55.2bn (2024) and then more
than doubled to 152.0bn (2025) on revenue that was flat-to-down across the
period (1,448bn → 1,392bn → 1,498bn) — this is **margin volatility from the
spread, not volume growth**, which is exactly the pattern a molasses-cost /
ethanol-price commodity-processor should produce. This is the mechanical
explanation for the screener's "peak earnings" flag — 2025's spread was
unusually wide, not necessarily unsustainable, but also not provably durable
without current molasses spot data (gap above).

## 3. The bioethanol policy question (Priority 3) — the central re-rating variable

**FACT, and this is real, current law, not aspiration.** Indonesia's Ministry of
Energy (ESDM) issued **Permenteri ESDM No. 4/2025**, mandating a 5% ethanol
blend (**E5**) in non-subsidised (non-PSO) gasoline. Implementation began
**1 July 2026**, staged starting in Java (Jakarta, East/Central/West Java,
Yogyakarta, Bali, Lampung named as first-wave regions), with **E10 targeted for
2028**.
[en.tempo.co](https://en.tempo.co/read/2104879/indonesia-to-mandate-5-ethanol-blended-gasoline-from-july-2026),
[esdm.go.id](https://www.esdm.go.id/id/media-center/arsip-berita/kementerian-esdm-segera-implementasikan-bahan-bakar-nabati-bioetanol-e5),
[industri.kontan.co.id](https://industri.kontan.co.id/news/mandatori-e5-bensin-non-subsidi-wajib-campur-bioetanol-5-mulai-semester-ii-2026)

**FACT, as of the current date (11 Aug 2026).** Actual rollout is still
**limited, not national**: ~170 Pertamina stations across East Java and Jakarta
are currently blending E5 (branded Pertamax Green 95), priced Rp16,600/litre as
of 1 Aug 2026 (down from Rp17,000 in July). The government sets a **regulated
monthly reference price (HIP)** for bioethanol feedstock: Rp11,695/litre for
August 2026, up from Rp10,933/litre in July.
[cnbcindonesia.com](https://www.cnbcindonesia.com/news/20260811102243-4-758181/ri-sudah-punya-bbm-bioetanol-cek-segini-harganya-di-spbu)

**Reading this correctly:** the mandate is *regulated and partially enforced
today*, not merely discussed — this is a genuine, dated fact that distinguishes
it from a policy aspiration. But **enforcement is currently narrow** (one
non-PSO product, ~170 stations, two provinces) and the demand-pool estimates in
press are inconsistent and unattributed to firm dates: one figure cites
**1.2 million KL/year fuel-grade ethanol demand by 2030**; another cites **~4
million KL/year at full E10** implementation. Both are **government
projections, not contracted offtake**, and neither should be read as "arrived."
Treat the mandate as a **real, in-force, but still-small tailwind with credible
optionality to scale** — this is the single most important corrective to the
screener's "peak earnings, therefore fade the number" framing: 2028's E10 step
is now underwritten by a July-2026 E5 base already in the CLAUDE.md-relevant
sense of "law," which the screener's backward-looking 5-year median cannot see.

**FACT.** MOLI has responded with **Rp350 billion of 2026 capex** — new
distillation capacity, a new liquid-CO2 line, a boiler, and supporting
equipment — explicitly framed by management as positioning for the E5/E10
opportunity.
[antaranews.com](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5601951/moli-siapkan-capex-rp350-miliar-incar-peluang-program-bioetanol)
**FACT, same source, management's own stated risks**: domestic oversupply of
*non-fuel-grade* ethanol, weak export controls on raw feedstock, and rising
energy costs — management itself flags oversupply risk in the segment it isn't
targeting, and says it is aiming capex at premium/export segments rather than
assuming it wins the fuel-grade opportunity by default.

## 4. Competitive position

**FACT.** The most important competitive fact found: **PTPN I** (state-owned
sugar holding) operates bioethanol production via subsidiary **Enero**
(PT Energi Agro Nusantara) and via **Sinergi Gula Nusantara (SGN)**, and is
explicitly described as backed by **42 sugar factories** — i.e. a
*vertically-integrated, captive-molasses* competitor. PTPN has separately
pledged to supply Pertamina **30,000 KL of bioethanol** for Pertamax blending.
[inilampung.com](https://www.inilampung.com/2026/07/industri-bioetanol-ptpn-i-ditunjang-42.html),
[aprobi.or.id](https://www.aprobi.or.id/ptpn-akan-pasok-pertamina-bioetanol-30-000-kl-untuk-campuran-pertamax/)

This matters directly for the thesis: **if the fuel-grade opportunity is real,
the state-owned, molasses-integrated competitor is structurally better placed
to win it** than MOLI, which appears to buy molasses on the open market (§2).
No evidence was found of a MOLI–Pertamina supply agreement (the antaranews
piece describes intent/capex, not a signed offtake contract) —
**insufficient evidence — requires further research** on whether MOLI has, or
is close to, a fuel-grade supply contract.

**FACT.** Alcohol production in Indonesia is licensed and restricted: Kemenperin
Regulation 17/2019 governs the alcoholic-beverage industry (classes A/B/C by
ethanol content), and industrial licensing (IUI) requires a recommendation from
Kemenperin's Direktorat Jenderal Industri Agro before BKPM will issue the permit;
food-grade ethanol additionally must meet BPOM standards.
[kemenperin.go.id](https://www.kemenperin.go.id/artikel/9705/Kemenperin-Terbitkan-Regulasi-Bagi-Industri-Minuman-Beralkohol),
[legalist.id](https://legalist.id/izin-produksi-alkohol/)
This is a **real, verifiable regulatory barrier to entry** — it supports MOLI's
incumbency (60 years of operating history, existing licenses, existing BPOM
certifications) but does **not** protect MOLI from the *other already-licensed*
incumbent (PTPN/SGN), which is the actual competitive threat in the fuel-grade
segment.

**Import substitution**: not established in this pass —
**insufficient evidence — requires further research** on whether imported
ethanol competes with domestic supply, and under what tariff/quota regime
(management's own complaint about "insufficient export controls on raw
materials" suggests feedstock, not finished ethanol, is the leakier border).

## 6. Answering the key question

Is MOLI a genuinely good business at a cheap price, or a commodity processor
enjoying one good spread year? The evidence supports **neither pole cleanly**:

- It is **not** a low-quality one-off: MOLI has a 60-year operating history, a
  real (if imperfectly verified) domestic share position, licensing barriers
  that are real and hard to replicate, product diversification (ethanol/CO2/
  fertiliser) across defensive end-markets, and — as of literally this month —
  an *in-force, regulated, if partial* government mandate that did not exist a
  year ago. That is a genuine structural change, not a hopeful narrative.
- It is **not** a durable compounder either: management's own words concede
  minimal pricing power and margin instability in the core segment; the
  best-positioned competitor for the one clearly-growing sub-market (fuel-grade
  ethanol) is a vertically-integrated state player with captive molasses supply
  that MOLI does not have; and the family/insider ownership structure with a 9%
  float is a standing minority-treatment risk this pass could not clear.
