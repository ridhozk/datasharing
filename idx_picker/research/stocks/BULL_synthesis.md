## The headline: the screener reached the right verdict for entirely the wrong reasons

BULL is the inverse of every other name in this review. The screener said **SKIP** — and on
the numbers it had, that SKIP was **unearned**. Its data was **two quarters stale**, its DCF
was **silently dead**, and its comparables came from the **wrong industry**. Three broken
lenses pointing at a two-quarter-old snapshot.

Then Q2-2026 arrived and the stock screened at **5.74x trailing earnings, 1.56x book, 27%
ROE**. Every trailing lens flipped from "not cheap" to "cheap."

**And the research still says do not buy.** Not because it is expensive on Q2 — on Q2 it is
not — but because **Q2 is a war**, and underneath it sits a company its own auditor has
qualified four years running with an explicit going-concern paragraph.

## What actually happened in Q2 — and my premise was wrong

I told the agents that a 52.12% net margin exceeding the operating margin proved a large
below-the-line gain, and that revenue cannot double on charter rates. **Agent 1 refuted the
first claim and Agent 3 refuted the second.**

**On the margin:** the operating-margin tile I compared against was a **stale Q1 value** — my
own note said so, and I then reasoned as if it were Q2. It was never a same-quarter
comparison, so **no hidden gain is established.** Agent 1 was right to refuse to call a
stale-tile artifact a forensic red flag.

**On the revenue doubling — there is a sourced, mechanical explanation:**

> **FACT.** US/Israeli military operations against Iran began late **February 2026**. Iran
> attacked shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. Middle East crude tanker rates hit
> **multi-decade highs in March 2026**. Aframax spot earnings reached **USD 145,343/day
> (+57% MoM)**. As of **23 August 2026 Hormuz traffic remained ~95% below pre-war levels.**

> **FACT (management, reported pre-filing).** *"Average TCE revenue through Q2-2026 had
> reached more than double compared to Q1-2026."*

Revenue went 741 → 1,487bn. That is **1.9x**. Management said TCE more than doubled. **The
magnitude matches, on an unchanged fleet.** No acquisition is required to explain it.

**Two smaller contributors are real and one is unresolved:**
- LNG: BULL bought two secondhand LNG carriers (Dec-2025, Q1-2026, ~USD 60-70m, 75% debt
  funded). LNG spot rates spiked **~18-fold to USD 300,000/day** from late February. The
  second vessel's first full quarter was Q2.
- **UNRESOLVED and material:** subsidiary Swordfish Maritime completed the purchase of
  **Offshore Maritime Holdings Pte Ltd for up to USD 58.8m on 27 March 2026** — the last
  business week of Q1, making Q2 its first full quarter of consolidation. A second deal
  (**Saras Rescue FZE, USD 61.2m**, USD 23.7m already advanced) is due to close within 2026.
  With secondhand vessel values at record highs, **PSAK 22 would force any excess of fair
  value over price straight through P&L as a bargain-purchase gain.** Neither the
  consolidation-scope effect nor a possible gain can be sized without the Q2 notes.

**So: Q2 is mostly a war, partly LNG, and possibly partly an acquisition.** The first is
temporary by nature. The third would be non-recurring by definition.

## Resolving the agents' one real disagreement — is BULL contracted or spot?

This matters more than anything else in the file, and the three agents flatly contradicted
each other. **I side with Agent 3, and it overturns the fact sheet's central premise.**

| Source | Claim |
|---|---|
| Agent 3 — Samuel Sekuritas initiation, Apr 2026, citing company data, 9M25 | **95% spot / 2% time charter; 97.6% international** |
| Agent 4 — search-aggregated secondary | Pertamina ~83% of revenue, management targeting 85% |
| Agent 5 — FY2025 audited statements | "95% of revenue is contracted... not spot" |

**Agent 5's reading is a terminology error, and it inverts.** The audited statements say
*"freight revenue from contracts with customers... recognised on a percentage-of-completion
basis."* "Revenue from contracts with customers" is the **IFRS 15 / PSAK 72 caption** — it
does not mean "contracted" in the charter-cover sense. Time charters are **lease income
under IFRS 16**; it is **voyage (spot) charters** that are recognised percentage-of-
completion. So that 95% figure **confirms Agent 3's spot finding rather than refuting it.**

**Agent 4's 83%/85% Pertamina figures trace to 2020–21 reporting** — the same era Agent 3
dates and then shows collapsing: 1H-2022 time-charter revenue **−43.9% YoY**, Pertamina-group
revenue **−58.8% to USD 14.4m**. And the FY2025 audited statements disclose a named Pertamina
charter at just **USD 4,900/day, expiring August 2026** — a trivial sum for a company earning
USD 84m a quarter.

**Conclusion: BULL pivoted between roughly 2022 and 2025 from a Pertamina-dominated domestic
time-charter model to international spot trading.** 5 of 11 vessels were tracked by AIS in
the Mediterranean/Black Sea in April 2026; **none transit Hormuz.**

**This kills the fact sheet's own hypothesis.** I had read the 9.9% five-year revenue
volatility as evidence of contracted, infrastructure-like earnings. It is nothing of the
kind — **it is a trailing artifact of a business model that no longer exists.** Forward
volatility should be expected to track Aframax and MR spot rates approximately one-for-one,
**with no charter backlog to cushion a decline.**

The screener classified BULL "Cyclical." It was more right than it knew, and for the wrong
reason.

This is the part of the file that decides the verdict, and it comes from primary documents.

> **FACT — BULL's FY2025 audited consolidated financial statements (issued 30 April 2026,
> BDO Indonesia) carry a QUALIFIED opinion, for at least the fourth consecutive year.**

| Finding | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Qualified opinion** | Five offshore non-bank creditors sold collateral vessels; balances **never reconciled since the 2021 confirmation cycle**. BULL carries **USD 7.97m** of "receivables under reconciliation" (USD 9.93m at FY2024) the auditor "was unable to satisfy ourselves concerning the completeness, accuracy, validity, valuation and classification" of. |
| **Going concern** | Explicit *"Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern"* paragraph. **Current liabilities exceeded current assets by USD 55.1m** at FY2025 (USD 72.4m at FY2024). |
| **Covenant breach** | Required: current ratio **≥100%**, TIE ≥2x, D/E ≤2x, DSCR ≥1x, collateral/loan ≥125%. The statements say plainly: *"the Group was unable to meet the required financial ratios for the years ended 31 December 2025 and 2024."* A waiver ran **only to 31 January 2026** — already expired, with no renewal sourced. |
| **The reclassification the auditor wanted** | At FY2024 the auditor stated compliant classification would move **USD 70,721,758** from non-current to current liabilities (FY2023: USD 88.1m). Management declined. |
| **Accumulated deficit** | **USD 217.9m** at FY2025 (USD 244.7m FY2024, USD 309.5m at 1 Jan 2023) — origin: a 2011 default to Merrill Lynch, restructuring in 2014, an **8:1 reverse split**, and COVID-era forced vessel sales. |

**Read the improved Q2 current ratio against this.** It rose 0.597 → **0.92** — genuine
progress, and I will not undersell it. But the covenant minimum is **1.00**. On the face of
it BULL was **still in technical breach at Q2-2026**, just less severely. Add the auditor's
preferred reclassification and it is far worse.

**This is why the zero dividend is not capital discipline.** Indonesian company law (UU PT
Art. 71) bars distributions against an accumulated deficit. BULL is **actively planning a
quasi-reorganisation** — an RUPSLB resolution to reset the deficit to zero — **explicitly to
create a legal path to dividends.** It has not been approved. And the loan covenants
separately restrict payments to shareholders without creditor consent. **A cyclical that
cannot return cash gives shareholders no way to realise the cycle.**

## NAV — and a correction to my own earlier reading

**FACT (Note 2j/3b):** BULL carries vessels on the **revaluation model** under PSAK 16, not
historical cost. Useful life **5–35 years**, depreciation rates 2.85%–20%, residual value
**USD 470/LDT**. At FY2024, historical cost would have carried the vessels at USD 154.0m
against USD 184.7m reported — a **~USD 30.7m unrealised revaluation surplus** in book equity.

**Agent 5 read that as book being marked to a cycle peak, and I repeated it. Agent 2 showed
the direction is probably the opposite.** The last formal appraisal was **31 December
2022** — it *predates the entire 2026 war-driven asset-value spike.* A book frozen at 2022
marks is more plausibly **stale-low** than stale-high against 2026 comps, where a 22-year-old
Aframax set a record at USD 33m. The surplus is real and not market-tested; its direction is
not what I said it was.

**The NAV work then converged from two directions.** Agent 2 priced the fleet bottom-up
against 2026 broker comps at **~USD 210–220m**, which lands almost exactly on the reported
**NetPPE of USD 219m** — so the "fleet too small for the balance sheet" puzzle I flagged in
the fact sheet was **my own size-intuition error, not a data defect.** Converting to NAV:

> **NAV ≈ IDR 295–301/share** (band 210–390), against reported **BVPS 299.49** — two
> independent methods within a few rupiah of each other.

**At IDR 466 that is P/NAV ~1.55x.** The global crude-tanker peer set trades at **0.85–1.05x**
(Nordic American ~0.88, Torm ~1.02, Hafnia at a small discount), with **Okeanis the richest
of the set at ~1.6x**. BULL is not catching up toward the peer cluster from a discount — **it
has already run past it to sit beside the most expensive name in it.** That is a late-cycle
position on the lens shipowners are actually valued on.

**And the fleet is old:** 11 owned vessels, oil sub-fleet averaging **~22 years** against a
typical 20–25 year commercial life. High forward capex is required **to replace age, not only
to grow**.

### The LNG story is smaller and weaker than it first looked

This is Agent 2's most valuable single find, and it qualifies Agent 1's LNG explanation:

- BULL bought **Gas Polaris (2002-built, 140,500 cbm) for USD 14.9m in March 2026** — press
  described the purchase as **"counter-cyclical," because secondhand LNG carrier values have
  been falling for four consecutive years.**
- **Both LNG carriers are old steam-turbine tonnage** — the segment 2026 shipping press
  describes as struggling against modern dual-fuel vessels. **~100 steam units are expected
  to be scrapped industry-wide by 2030**, and **60+ LNG carriers were idle in February 2026,
  70% of them steam.** Comparable steam LNGCs sold for **USD 13.8–19.2m for recycling**.

So BULL bought cheap, semi-obsolete assets opportunistically near scrap value — a sensible
trade, but **not premium tonnage positioned to capture the headline USD 300k/day spot rate**,
which accrues disproportionately to modern efficient ships. Whether BULL's specific vessels
fixed anywhere near that rate is **not established.**

**And there is a dated precedent against capitalising the spike at all: LNG spot rates hit
USD 200,000/day in late 2022 and collapsed ~85% to ~USD 30,000/day.** Same decade, same
segment, same shape. Meanwhile 2026 LNG *commodity* fundamentals are bearish — 37 mtpa of new
liquefaction capacity arriving, Kpler seeing Asian spot prices fall from ~USD 12 to
~USD 10/mmBtu.

## Screener defect #7 — confirmed in code, and a third failure category

Agent 2 reproduced the failure exactly from source and cache:

```
NOPAT            622.9 × 0.78  =  +485.9bn
implied D&A      740.7 − 717.5 =   +23.2bn
normalised capex               =  −623.6bn
                                  ---------
cash-flow base                 =  −114.5bn  →  dcf_per_share returns None
```

Depreciation is inferred as `EBITDA_TTM − EBIT_TTM`. The suppression guard fires only when
the gap is **under one rupiah**, so a 23bn stub sails through. All three scenarios inherit
the same negative base. **EPV is unaffected** — it uses no D&A add-back.

**The real number is now sourced.** FY2024 depreciation was **USD 15,598,365** (FY2023: USD
15,782,350) — **~IDR 276bn/year, about 12x the inferred figure.** A direct probe confirmed
Yahoo carries **no depreciation line at all** for this filer (`ReconciledDepreciation` = 0.00
in every period), so the fix cannot be "fetch the field."

**Beyond that, this name exposes a failure mode distinct from the six logic defects fixed so
far: staleness.** Correct arithmetic on two-quarter-old inputs. `Quarters Stale` is computed
(**1.72**) and printed — **but it does not gate, downgrade, or flag the verdict.** A
confident SKIP was published on data that predated a quadrupling of earnings.

**Correcting the DCF changes its answer but not its conclusion.** On real D&A the cash-flow
base flips from **−114bn to +140bn** — but bear and base still return *no positive equity*,
because IDR 1,907bn of net debt swamps a thin, capex-hungry cash flow. Only bull clears, at
IDR 38/share. An independent model agrees the downside is severe.

**Fixes — one shipped, two queued:**

1. **Staleness gate — SHIPPED.** Above `STALE_QUARTERS_WARN` (1.5) `classify` now raises a red
   flag, which surfaces the row for refresh and blocks the BUY gate. **209 of 956 rows now
   carry it; 9 BUYs dropped to WATCH (41 → 32) and no BUY rests on stale filings.** A second
   bug surfaced while verifying: `Red Flags` was serialised in `derive_rows` *before*
   `classify` ran, so the flag altered verdicts while every row showed an empty cell. Fixed,
   with an integration test anchored on `Reasons` that would have caught it. **Agent 2
   recommends going further — tightening to >1.0 quarters for `BUSINESS_CYCLICAL` names**,
   since a stale cyclical risks being out of date *and* at the wrong point in the cycle at
   once. Not yet implemented.
2. **D&A — Agent 2's recommendation, not yet implemented:** a **sector-aware NetPPE ÷
   useful-life proxy**, always tagged `D&A Source: Proxied`. Validated against BULL's audited
   figure: a 15-year life gives USD 13.9m against the real USD 15.6m — **11% out, versus the
   current method's 12x.** Crucially it must *not* be applied universally: **toll roads**
   (concession intangibles, often units-of-usage) and **coal** (units-of-production against
   reserves) should route to "declare unavailable + red flag" instead, because there a flat
   time-based proxy is the wrong *model*, not merely the wrong parameter. Shipping, towers and
   plantations are good fits.
3. **Peer group** — "Passenger Marine Transportation" (EV/EBIT median 15.0x) is wrong for a
   tanker owner. Not yet implemented.

**A warning worth recording: the two defects partially cancelled.** Had the peer-group bug
been fixed *alone*, a correctly-matched higher Comps number combining with a still-blank DCF
and unchanged EPV could plausibly have pushed the blend across the watch threshold into a
**false BUY**. Fixing one bug at a time is not always safe.

## The cycle is past its acute peak, and the orderbook is the real problem

- Rates hit multi-decade highs in **March 2026**; by late August the war premium was
  **already normalising** (oil ~20% above pre-war, down from >USD 130/bbl in April).
- **The tanker orderbook reached 1,267 vessels / 173.3m dwt by July 2026 — ~25.0% of the
  existing fleet**, driven by 120 net new VLCC orders. Ordered *into* a war-risk spike, at
  peak asset values, delivering from 2027.
- **PIS in-sourcing** is real but gradual: a 500-vessel target by 2034, **200 owned / 300
  chartered** — so PIS still plans to charter the majority. But **15 MR newbuilds deliver
  through November 2026**, directly overlapping BULL's MR segment.
- **FX cuts against BULL's reported numbers,** unlike the consumer names in this review: a
  *firming* rupiah (17,908 → 17,684) mechanically shrinks reported IDR earnings on unchanged
  USD economics.

Management calls its vessel purchases "mid-cycle" while the industry press reports secondhand
values at record highs. **Both cannot be true of the same market.**

## Verdict

**NO POSITION. The SKIP stands — but as CORRECT-BY-ACCIDENT, not correct.**

The screener got here through a dead DCF, a wrong peer group and two-quarter-old data. Every
one of those was a defect, and on corrected, current data the cheap-looking multiples are
real. **The verdict survives anyway, on grounds the screener never touched.**

**What the research establishes:**
1. **Q2-2026 is a war.** Revenue doubled because Middle East tanker rates hit multi-decade
   highs after February 2026, on a fleet that is **95% spot with no charter cover**. When
   Hormuz normalises, revenue follows it down one-for-one.
2. **The balance sheet is in a condition the price action does not reflect.** A qualified
   audit opinion four years running, an explicit going-concern paragraph, current liabilities
   exceeding current assets by USD 55.1m, and a covenant breach whose waiver expired in
   January 2026.
3. **The cheapness partly measures itself.** Book value is on the revaluation model, marked
   to vessel prices at decade highs, on an appraisal last performed in December 2022.
4. **Shareholders cannot realise the cycle.** No dividend is legally possible until a
   quasi-reorganisation clears a USD 217.9m deficit — not yet approved, and covenants may
   block distributions even then.
5. **The controlling shareholder sold at 468** — today's price — days after an IDX UMA notice.

**What I could not kill, stated honestly:**
- The deficit is genuinely shrinking (USD 244.7m → 217.9m), interest expense is genuinely
  falling (61.4 → 38.2bn/quarter), the current ratio genuinely improved (0.597 → 0.92).
- **The LNG/FSRU pivot is real, not vapourware** — two vessels delivered, gas is already a
  reportable segment, three more planned for H2-2026.
- **Nobody proved a one-off gain.** My "net margin exceeds operating margin" argument was
  built on a stale tile and does not stand. The OMH bargain-purchase hypothesis is
  unconfirmed either way.
- Seven analysts average a target of **742**. This is not a fringe view.

**The one piece of evidence that would settle it:** BULL's **Q2-2026 interim statements and
notes** — specifically "other income," any gain on bargain purchase, the OMH consolidation-
scope note, and an updated covenant/going-concern paragraph. Yahoo does not carry it and no
agent could retrieve it. **Everything above is built on the FY2025 audited statements plus
the owner's terminal data.**

**Actionable: no position.** This is a **leveraged, covenant-breaching, spot-exposed bet on
the Strait of Hormuz staying closed** — a legitimate trade, but not a value investment, and
not one this framework can underwrite.

**Alert at IDR 250–300** — the NAV base case, where the price would sit at ~1.0x fleet value
in line with the 0.85–1.05x global tanker peer cluster, and mid-cycle earnings rather than war
earnings would carry the valuation. Today's 1.55x P/NAV is Okeanis territory.

**Watch, in order:** (1) the Q2-2026 filing's other-income and covenant notes; (2)
confirmation or denial of the Sinar Mas 30-vessel rights issue — **and its price**, given a
placement at IDR 135 has already been reported; (3) the quasi-reorganisation vote; (4) Hormuz
traffic normalising, which is the single variable this entire earnings base rests on.
