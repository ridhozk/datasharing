## VERDICT: REJECT (as a value-investing BUY at 464, on the case as currently evidenced)

Not because the business is worthless — because **the specific bull case required to
override the screener's SKIP does not survive contact with the primary documents.** The
qualified-opinion, going-concern-flagged, covenant-breaching FY2025 audited financial
statements — the company's own most recent full-year filing — describe a business in a
different condition than the "deleveraged, repairing" story the price action is telling.
Layered on top is a Q2-2026 profit surge whose net margin *exceeds* its own operating
margin, which is not a number a shipping operator produces from operations. See §1.

**The three facts that would have to be true for the bull case to work:**
1. Q2-2026's IDR ~775bn net income is substantially **operating**, not a bargain-purchase
   gain, FX gain, or other one-off from the Offshore Maritime Holdings (OMH) / Saras Rescue
   acquisitions closing in Q1–Q2 2026 — i.e., the 3.6–5.7x "annualised PE" is real earnings
   power, not one quarter's accounting event divided into a market cap.
2. The going-concern qualification, the covenant breach (current ratio required ≥100%,
   actual well below), and the unresolved 2021-vintage audit-confirmation gap with 5
   non-bank creditors in the FY2025 audited statements are **resolved or immaterial** by the
   time of purchase — not still live.
3. The rumoured Sinar Mas Group 30-LNG-tanker "backdoor" injection via rights issue, if it
   happens, is priced **fairly to BULL's existing minority shareholders** — not a related-
   party asset-for-shares transfer that dilutes today's holders to benefit the vessel seller.

**The single piece of evidence that would most efficiently settle this:** BULL's **Q2-2026
interim financial statements and notes** (not yet in Yahoo's feed, and not fully sourced by
me from IDX/company IR within this pass) — specifically the P&L "other income/expense" and
"gain on bargain purchase" lines, the consolidation-scope note for OMH, and an updated

## 1. PEAK-CYCLE EARNINGS CAPITALISED — and the Q2-2026 margin anomaly (highest priority)

**FACT (screener fact sheet, Yahoo-sourced):** Q1-2026 operating margin 37.3% vs 5-year
average EBIT margin 23.3%; 4-year median operating income IDR 630.8bn vs Q1 annualised to
~IDR 1,175bn; FY2022 operating income was actually **positive** IDR 178.8bn but net income
was a **loss of IDR −787.5bn** (financing-cost driven, per the corrected EBIT computation
already documented in BULL.md).

**FACT (owner-supplied, Stockbit, 2026-09-04, relayed by orchestrator):** Q2-2026 revenue
IDR 1,487bn (vs Q1-2026's 741bn — near-doubling quarter-on-quarter); EPS Q2-2026 IDR 50.04;
net margin Q2 **52.12%**; TTM(Q2) EPS 81.13, annualised 130.97; at price 466, **PE TTM 5.74,
PE annualised 3.59, forward PE 4.40, EV/EBITDA 7.00.**

**This is the kill shot, if it holds up.** A shipping operator's net margin cannot
exceed its operating margin unless something large sits below the operating line —
a disposal gain, a bargain-purchase gain on an acquisition, an FX gain, a debt-
restructuring gain, or a deferred-tax credit. Q1-2026's operating margin was 37.3%; even
generously assuming Q2's operating margin held at that level (revenue mix shifted toward
LNG/FPSO, which the segment note below shows is a real fleet-mix change, so this is not
guaranteed), a 52.12% **net** margin implies roughly **15 points of revenue — on IDR 1,487bn
of Q2 revenue, on the order of IDR 220bn or more** — came from something other than
operating the fleet. *(INFERENCE: exact split unavailable without the Q2 P&L; this is an
order-of-magnitude bound based on the stated Q1 operating margin as a proxy ceiling for Q2
operating margin.)*

**What I can source as a candidate mechanism (FACT, from BULL's FY2025 audited consolidated
financial statements, issued 30 April 2026, auditor Tanubrata Sutanto Fahmi Bambang & Rekan
/ BDO):**

- Note 12 / Note 38(d): subsidiary **Swordfish Maritime Ltd (SFM)** signed a Conditional
  Shares Purchase Agreement in Dec 2024 (amended Dec 2025) to buy **100% of Offshore
  Maritime Holdings Pte Ltd (OMH)**, a Singapore holding company "engaged in investing in
  shipping companies," from third party Pacrim Maritime Investments Ltd, for a maximum
  transaction value of **US$58.8 million**. The deal **closed 27 March 2026** — inside Q1,
  but if OMH's control/consolidation date fell such that its results only entered BULL's
  consolidated accounts starting Q2, or if a bargain-purchase gain was recognised on
  finalisation, this closing is a live candidate for the Q2 anomaly.
- A second, still-pending deal: **PT Anjasmoro Maritime (AMT)**, another BULL subsidiary,
  agreed (30 Dec 2025) to buy 100% of **Saras Rescue FZE** (UAE) for **US$61.2 million**,
  with a US$23.7m advance already paid and completion required within 12 months — i.e., due
  to close some time in 2026, a second possible source of acquisition-accounting gains this
  year.
- Vessel/shipping asset markets in 2026 are independently reported at **decade/record
  highs** — five-year-old VLCCs priced *above* newbuild cost, resale premiums of tens of
  millions of dollars per vessel (Breakwave Advisors, Hellenic Shipping News, Splash247,
  2026 reporting — see Sources). **If the fair value of OMH's or Saras Rescue's underlying
  vessels significantly exceeds the agreed purchase price, PSAK 22/IFRS 3 requires BULL to
  recognise the excess immediately as a "gain on bargain purchase" through profit or loss.**
  This is exactly the kind of one-off, non-cash, non-recurring item that would make net
  margin exceed operating margin in the quarter of closing.
- BULL reports in USD and converts to IDR (FX 17,684 at 2026-09-04, having moved from
  17,908 three weeks earlier per the fact sheet). An FX gain on USD-denominated cash or
  intercompany balances is a second plausible, non-mutually-exclusive contributor.

**I cannot confirm which of these (or some other item entirely) actually drove the Q2
margin** — I do not have the Q2-2026 interim statements or their notes. **Insufficient
evidence — requires further research: the actual Q2-2026 "other income" / "gain on bargain
purchase" line.** But the arithmetic itself (net margin > operating margin, on a quarter
containing a just-closed US$58.8m acquisition in a market where vessel resale values are at
record highs) is a FACT, and it is sufficient on its own to say: **the "3.6–5.7x PE" number
cannot be taken at face value without first subtracting whatever produced it.** If it is
materially a bargain-purchase or FX gain, the true run-rate PE is meaningfully higher than
headline, and the bull case's central number — "cheap on Q2-annualised earnings" — evaporates
in the same way the Q1-annualised 7.1x PE built on peak-margin quarters was already flagged
as suspect in BULL.md.

**Revenue nearly doubling QoQ (741bn → 1,487bn) is itself abnormal for a charter business**
with 5-year revenue volatility of just 9.9% (per BULL.md) — that volatility figure describes
a *pre-OMH* BULL. **INFERENCE:** the most parsimonious explanation is a consolidation-scope
change (OMH, and whatever vessels/contracts it holds, entering the group accounts), not a
doubling of charter rates or utilisation in one quarter. If so, revenue and asset growth are
partly an **accounting boundary change**, not organic value creation — and any JV-level debt
OMH carries would arrive on BULL's balance sheet at the same time. **Insufficient evidence —
requires the Q2 segment note and OMH's opening balance sheet** to confirm.

**Even before Q2:** FY2022 was a **net loss** of IDR −787.5bn on **positive** operating
income of IDR 178.8bn — a company whose bottom line has round-tripped from deep loss to a
52% net margin inside four years is not a company whose latest quarter should be capitalised
at face value. The owner's framework rule — never treat peak-cycle earnings as permanent
earnings — applies with extra force to a quarter that may not even be peak-*cycle* earnings
but a peak-*event* (M&A gain) dressed as peak-cycle earnings.

**Multiple on normalised earnings:** Take the 4-year median operating income (IDR 630.8bn,
per BULL.md), apply a normalised ~20% net margin (roughly the FY2023–2025 range excluding
the 2022 loss and any 2026 one-offs) to BULL's TTM(Q2) revenue base of IDR 3,438bn →
normalised net income of order IDR 690bn, against a market cap of IDR 7,189bn = **~10.4x
normalised earnings** — not far from the screener's own EV/EBIT (Acquirer's Multiple) read
of 12.6x that already said "not cheap for a cyclical." *(This is an illustrative INFERENCE,
not a precise model — the real normalisation requires the actual FY2023-2025 net margins ex
one-offs, which I have only partially sourced.)*

## 2. THE CAPEX BINGE — and the P/CF contradiction

**FACT (fact sheet):** TTM capex IDR 1,724bn — 2.4x TTM EBIT, ~24% of market cap, 5.4x the
FY2023 level; TTM FCF just IDR 78bn (1.1% FCF yield).

**FACT (owner-supplied, Stockbit):** the same source reports **P/CF 5.76 and P/FCF 5.76 —
identical**, which arithmetically means operating cash flow equals free cash flow, i.e.
**zero capex** in whatever TTM window that source used. This is a hard contradiction with
the 1,724bn TTM capex figure. **I cannot resolve it with the evidence in hand.** Candidate
explanations (all INFERENCE, unconfirmed): (a) Stockbit's TTM window (e.g. Q3-25 through
Q2-26) captured a lull between vessel deliveries different from our Yahoo-sourced TTM
window; (b) the data vendor computed "FCF" as operating cash flow without subtracting capex
(a known category of data-vendor error); (c) some capex is being incurred at the JV/associate
level (consistent with the OMH-holding-company structure noted above) and does not appear in
BULL's own consolidated capex line the way it appears in Yahoo's feed. **Insufficient
evidence — requires the Q2-2026 cash flow statement.** What is not in doubt: these two
readings cannot both be right, and the FCF yield the bull case can claim swings between
1.1% and roughly 17% depending on which is correct — that gap is too large to wave away.

**FACT, from press reporting (2025-2026 Indonesian financial media, see Sources):**
management has stated a preference for buying **mid-cycle secondhand vessels** rather than
newbuilds (2-3 year delivery lag cited as the reason), with vessel price points of
**US$26-40m for oil tankers and US$25-35m for LNG carriers**, and a much larger-ticket
**FSRU programme at US$100-300m per unit**. The company began LNG fleet expansion with one
vessel in Dec 2025, a second in Q1 2026, and plans three more in H2 2026 (~US$125m estimated
capex for that batch). "Mid-cycle" is management's own characterisation of the vessels they
are buying, not an independent valuation — and it is being asserted in the same breath as
independent reporting that secondhand tanker and gas-carrier values in 2026 are at
**decade/record highs** (Breakwave Advisors, Splash247, Hellenic Shipping News — 5-year-old
VLCCs priced above newbuild cost; resale premiums of US$45m+ per vessel reported). **THESIS:
"mid-cycle" and "record secondhand asset prices" cannot both be true of the same market at
the same time** — either BULL is finding genuine relative value within a hot market, or
management's framing is doing work the market price data does not support. I could not find
vessel-type-specific (as opposed to VLCC-specific) price series to settle this precisely for
BULL's actual fleet mix (product/chemical/gas). **Insufficient evidence — requires MR/Aframax
and LNG-carrier secondhand price indices, not just VLCC, benchmarked against BULL's disclosed
purchase prices.**

**Is this how shipowners destroy capital?** The mechanism is real and BULL fits the pattern
structurally: debt-funded (see §3) fleet growth at a point in the cycle when asset prices are
elevated, financed partly through a private placement and a planned rights issue rather than
retained cash flow (FCF yield 1.1% on the figure I trust more, given the P/CF contradiction
above cuts against the "self-funding" story). Whether it is *actually* destroying capital
depends on the entry prices relative to normalised (not spot) asset values and the durability
of the charter cover behind each vessel — which I could not fully verify vessel-by-vessel.

## 3. THE BALANCE SHEET — qualified opinion, going concern, and a live covenant breach

This is the section the price-action bull case is least likely to have actually read, and it
matters more than the P&L noise above.

**FACT — directly from BULL's FY2025 audited consolidated financial statements** (auditor
report no. 00316/2.1068/AU.1/06/1642-1/1/IV/2026, BDO Indonesia member firm, financial
statements authorised for issue 30 April 2026):

- **The auditor issued a QUALIFIED opinion**, not a clean one. Basis: "several subsidiaries
  of the Group were unable to fulfill some of the provisions of the loan agreements from
  non-bank financial institutions" — collateral vessels were sold by 5 non-bank creditors and
  proceeds applied to debt, but **the Group has not agreed the reconciliation of these loan
  balances since the 2021 audit confirmation cycle**, carrying a receivable-under-
  reconciliation of **US$7,968,000** as at 31 Dec 2025 (US$9,929,376 at FY2024) that the
  auditor could not independently verify — "we were unable to satisfy ourselves concerning
  the completeness, accuracy, validity, valuation and classification" of this balance. This
  qualification has now persisted across at least the FY2022, FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025
  audits.
- **A separate paragraph discloses "MATERIAL UNCERTAINTY RELATED TO GOING CONCERN."** As at
  31 Dec 2025, the Group's **current liabilities exceeded current assets by US$55.1 million**
  (US$72.4m at FY2024) — a persistent, not one-off, working-capital deficit.
- **Covenant breach, in the company's own words:** the Group is contractually required to
  maintain (among others) a **current ratio of at least 100%**, time-interest-earned ≥2x,
  debt/equity ≤2x, debt-service coverage ≥1x (≥1.1x against vessel fair value), and
  fixed-asset-market-value-to-loan-outstanding ≥125%. The FY2025 statements state plainly:
  **"the Group was unable to meet the required financial ratios for the years ended 31
  December 2025 and 2024."** A creditor granted a waiver/postponement of collateral
  provisions — but **only until 31 January 2026**, a date that has already passed as of
  today (2026-09-04) with no confirmation found that it was extended. As a direct result,
  US$18,070,813 of long-term loan was reclassified to current maturities.
- Accumulated deficit: **US$217,923,482 at 31 Dec 2025** (US$244,739,725 at FY2024) — i.e.
  the deficit *shrank* by roughly US$26.8m during 2025, consistent with the profitable
  2025 the bull case cites, but it remains a very large negative retained-earnings balance
  (≈IDR 3.85trn at current FX), and — see §6 — is the explicit, confirmed legal reason
  BULL has never paid a dividend.

**On the owner-supplied Q2-2026 current ratio of 0.92** (up from 0.597): this is a real,
verifiable improvement in trend, and I should not undersell it — see "What I cannot kill"
below. But 0.92 is **still below the 100% (1.0x) covenant minimum** the company's own FY2025
statements disclose it was in breach of. Unless the covenant itself was renegotiated (not
confirmed), **BULL may still be in technical breach at Q2-2026**, just less severely than at
Q1. I could not source a Q2-2026 waiver update. **Insufficient evidence — requires the
Q2-2026 interim report's liquidity/covenant note.**

**Debt maturity ladder:** I could not extract a full maturity table from the audited
statements within this pass beyond the covenant-driven reclassification above. What I can
confirm from subsequent-events disclosures (Note 38, all dated March-April 2026, i.e. inside
the window the bull case treats as "deleveraging"): (a) 13 March 2026 — new US$24m
non-revolving facility from **PT Bank Sinarmas Tbk**, 60-month tenor, for "loan payment of
Group and working capital" — refinancing, not net deleveraging, secured by land owned by
entities related to the majority shareholder plus **BULL's own shares**; (b) 31 March 2026 —
a subsidiary fully repaid an earlier Bank Sinarmas facility; (c) 13 March 2026 — a subsidiary
obtained a new Rp336bn 60-month facility from **PT Bank Mandiri (Persero) Tbk**, secured by
one vessel and a corporate guarantee; (d) 1 April 2026 — the Company fully repaid a fixed
loan from **PT Bank Oke Indonesia Tbk**. Net effect is ambiguous from what I sourced — some
facilities were repaid, a larger new one was drawn — and I could not compute a clean net-debt
trajectory for Q1-Q2 2026 from press sources alone. **Insufficient evidence — requires the
Q2-2026 balance sheet to state whether gross/net debt actually fell.**

**On PIS in-sourcing (FACT, multiple 2025-2026 shipping-trade press sources — see Sources):**
Pertamina International Shipping (PIS) has stated a target of a **500-vessel fleet by 2034,
split 200 owned / 300 chartered**. That target implies PIS scaling its **owned** tonnage
substantially from a much smaller current base — a genuine structural risk to third-party
charter owners like BULL over a multi-year horizon. It does **not**, on the evidence I could
source, describe an imminent cliff: as of October 2025, PIS was still actively soliciting
14 new chartered vessels for gas/petrochemical distribution, and its more recent (2026)

erosion-of-pricing-power story, not something that shows up as a single-quarter shock — which
means it is exactly the kind of risk a screener's trailing multiples would *not* price in
either direction, and exactly the kind of risk the bull case's "strong tanker cycle" framing
tends to omit. **Could not quantify** what fraction of BULL's specific charters PIS's 200-
owned-vessel target would displace; that requires vessel-type-level detail on both fleets I
do not have. **Insufficient evidence — requires a vessel-type-matched comparison of BULL's
Pertamina-facing fleet against PIS's owned-fleet build plan.**

Note also (FACT, Note 32, audited statements): BULL's reportable segments are **"oil FPSO and
FSO, gas and others"** — i.e. part of the fleet is on FPSO/FSO-type long-duration production
support contracts, which behave differently (longer, more bond-like) than spot/time-charter
tanker exposure. The "strong tanker cycle" narrative doesn't map cleanly onto this whole
book; some of it is genuinely more defensive, some of it (the new LNG/gas push) is a fresh,
unproven-at-BULL business line, not existing tanker capacity re-rating.

## 5. GOVERNANCE

**FACT — control:** As of 31 Oct 2025, controlling shareholders are **PT Delta Royal
Sejahtera** (17.39%, ~2.69bn shares) and **PT Halim Jusuf**; **Halim Jusuf** is the ultimate
beneficial owner and **President Commissioner** (chairman). Per the 23 April 2026 AGM,
**Henry Jusuf** (same family name) is **President Director**; other directors include
Vicky Ganda Saputra, Santoso Salim, and Wong Kevin; commissioners include Andi Aviandha
Triadna Jakile, Achmad Widjaja, and new independent commissioner Ferita Lie. **THESIS/
unconfirmed:** I could not establish a confirmed family relationship between Halim Jusuf and
Henry Jusuf beyond the shared surname and their respective roles as Chairman/President
Director — a classic family-control pattern, but I am labelling the relationship itself as
INFERENCE, not FACT, since I did not find an explicit filing statement of familial
relationship.

**FACT — pre-2019 history (explains the accumulated deficit):** BULL was previously named
**PT Buana Listya Tama Tbk**, renamed to Buana Lintas Lautan in February 2018. On 28
September 2011 the company **defaulted** on interest due to Merrill Lynch (Asia Pacific)
Ltd / Orchard Centar Master Ltd (MLOR); a debt restructuring agreement was reached 24
September 2014; trading, which had been suspended, resumed 16 March 2015 following a capital
increase without pre-emptive rights (220.62m shares at Rp439) **and an 8:1 reverse stock
split**, alongside a US$7.8m debt-to-equity conversion. This restructuring/reverse-split/
dilution history is the direct origin of the multi-hundred-million-dollar accumulated deficit
still on the balance sheet in 2025 (§3) — it is legacy financial-distress history, not a
fresh discovery, but it is the reason the company needs a quasi-reorganisation today, over a
decade later, to become dividend-capable.

**FACT — related-party collateral (pledged shares/assets):** Per the FY2025 audited
statements, Note 30(a)-(b): the Group has used **shares owned by the major shareholders**,
plus shares and land owned by entities related to the majority shareholder, **as additional
collateral for loan facilities from PT Bank Sinarmas Tbk and PT Bank Oke Indonesia Tbk**.
This pattern recurs in the 13 March 2026 subsequent-events disclosure (new US$24m Bank
Sinarmas facility, again secured partly by BULL's own shares and majority-shareholder-linked
land). **This is a real, current, confirmed instance of "pledged shares"** as flagged in the
assignment brief — the controlling shareholder's personal/affiliate balance sheet is
structurally entangled with the company's, which both aligns incentives and creates
foreclosure/margin-call contagion risk in a downturn.

**FACT — Bank Sinarmas relationship since 2018,** described in press coverage as having
"supported BULL's development since 2018," with a market rumour (December 2025) of possible
**acquisition of BULL by Sinar Mas Group** which the company publicly addressed (I could not
retrieve the full text of BULL's response; the article was paywalled). **THESIS — the more
material live governance risk:** multiple 2026 Indonesian financial-media reports (Ajaib,
Bloomberg Technoz, Mikirduit, Kompas, Rikopedia — see Sources) describe an **unconfirmed
rumour that BULL will conduct a rights issue structured as a "backdoor" injection of
approximately 30 LNG tanker vessels owned by Sinar Mas Group** — timed to coincide with Sinar
Mas Group's reported ~Rp43 trillion acquisition of Hyundai LNG Shipping (fleet capacity
~1.43m DWT, ~2.4x BULL's current fleet). As of the most recent sourced reporting, **neither
BULL nor Sinar Mas Group has officially confirmed or denied this**; BULL's director Wong
Kevin did not respond to a direct media query on it. Circumstantial links reported: (a) the
August 2025 private placement investor "Fortune Street Limited" (Hong Kong) — which BULL
states has **no affiliation** with the company, its directors, commissioners, or controlling
shareholders — was suspected by market observers of being Sinar Mas-linked, unconfirmed; (b)
new independent commissioner **Ferita Lie** (appointed April 2026) has a reported 2013-2025
career history across Sinar Mas-affiliated entities; (c) Bank Sinarmas is a recurring lender

injection into a controlled vehicle — and it is live, current, and unresolved.** If it

the accumulated-deficit route is the most likely explanation**, but I could not source IDX's
specific stated reason for BULL's board placement. **Insufficient evidence — requires the
specific IDX announcement citing the criterion BULL failed.**

## 6. NO DIVIDEND — now with a confirmed legal answer

**FACT, directly answering this question:** BULL is **actively planning a quasi-
reorganisation** — an Extraordinary General Meeting (RUPSLB) resolution to reset the
accumulated retained-earnings deficit to zero — **explicitly and solely to create the legal
pathway for future dividend distribution.** Under Indonesian company law, a company generally
cannot distribute dividends while it carries a negative retained-earnings (accumulated
deficit) balance; the FY2025 audited statements confirm that balance stood at
**US$217.9m negative** as of 31 Dec 2025. **This is not covenant restriction or discretionary
capex discipline — it is the accumulated deficit legally blocking distribution**, exactly the
mechanism the brief asked me to check for. Management's own stated rationale for pursuing the
quasi-reorganisation cites "three consecutive years of profit through 2025" as the basis for
the accounting reset. **The dividend, if it ever comes, is conditional on shareholders
approving the reorganisation and on the deficit-reset being accepted** — a corporate action
that has not yet completed as of this research pass (I found no confirmation the
quasi-reorganisation RUPSLB has been held or approved; the 23 April 2026 RUPSLB confirmed in
the audited statements' subsequent events was about **management/board changes**, not the
quasi-reorganisation itself). **Insufficient evidence — requires confirmation the quasi-
reorganisation was actually voted on and approved**, and, separately, whether the loan
covenants (§3) would even permit a dividend once the accounting deficit is cleared — the
covenant package I sourced restricts changes to capital structure and payments to
shareholders/guarantors without creditor consent, which a dividend could plausibly trigger.

## 8. DEPRECIATION POLICY

**FACT — sourced directly from the FY2025 audited consolidated financial statements, Note
2/3 (accounting policies and material estimates):**

> "Penyusutan kapal dihitung menggunakan metode garis lurus berdasarkan taksiran masa manfaat
> dari kapal yaitu **5 - 35 tahun** atau tarif penyusutan sebesar **2,85% - 20%**."
> ("Depreciation of vessels are calculated on a straight line basis over the estimated useful
> life of the vessels between 5–35 years or depreciation rates amounted to 2.85%–20%.")

**Critically, BULL applies the REVALUATION MODEL (PSAK 16) to its vessels, not the cost
model** — carrying them at periodically-reassessed fair value less accumulated depreciation
and impairment since the last revaluation, with the revaluation reserve on eventual disposal
"transferred directly to deficit" (i.e. not recycled through profit or loss). Residual value
is explicitly benchmarked to "the resale or scrap markets, fluctuations in scrap prices" —
i.e. it moves with the market too. Other (non-vessel) fixed assets use the cost model.

**Why this matters more than a simple "is depreciation too slow" check:** because BULL's
vessels are carried at **appraised fair value**, and independent 2026 shipping-market
reporting places secondhand vessel values at **decade/record highs**, **BULL's book value
(and therefore the reported P/B of 1.87-1.89x, or 1.56x on the owner-supplied Q2 figure) is
itself already marked toward a cycle-peak asset value.** A P/B read as "only 1.5-1.9x, not
egregious" is a different — and more dangerous — number if the "B" is a peak-cycle mark than
if it were a conservative historical-cost carrying value. **This is a genuine, sourced
addition to the screener's own flagged concern about NAV vs P/B** (BULL.md §"What the
screener cannot see," item 1): the revaluation-model accounting means the fleet NAV question
and the "has this cycle already peaked" question are **the same question**, not two
independent checks. I could not obtain the actual appraised values or revaluation dates to
quantify how far above historical-average fair value the current carrying amount sits.
**Insufficient evidence — requires the fixed-asset revaluation note's appraiser report date
and methodology, and a benchmark of BULL's specific vessel types against historical (not just
2026-peak) secondhand price series.**

The 5-35 year useful-life range and 2.85-20% depreciation-rate range is wide enough that it is
not, on its face, an obviously aggressive (long-life, low-depreciation) policy relative to
typical shipping industry practice (commonly 20-30 years for tankers) — but combined with the
revaluation model, the *level* the depreciation is charged against is the real lever, and that
level is disclosed as periodically re-based to fair value, which in 2026 is elevated.

## 9. WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS A VALUE TRAP — the specific mechanism

1. Q2-2026's headline earnings surge is **substantially a one-off** (bargain-purchase gain
   on the OMH acquisition, an FX gain, or similar) rather than sustainable operating
   earnings power — so the 3.6-5.7x "cheap" multiples the bull case leans on are **an
   accounting-driven mirage**, not a mispriced cycle. Reported earnings then mean-revert hard
   once the one-off rolls out of the TTM window, exactly as the market is starting to price
   in with the −32% pullback from 685.
2. The FY2025 audited statements' **going-concern qualification and covenant breach are
   still live** (unresolved as of the 31 Jan 2026 waiver expiry, with no confirmed renewal
   sourced) — a company managing an active liquidity/covenant problem is simultaneously
   funding an acquisition-and-fleet-expansion binge at record secondhand asset prices,
   financed by dilutive placements and new debt secured partly by the controlling
   shareholder's own pledged shares and land. If the tanker/LNG cycle turns down before the
   newly acquired vessels' contracted cash flows are proven durable, BULL is levered into the
   downturn with **less** margin of safety than the "deleveraging" narrative claims, not more.
3. The rumoured **Sinar Mas 30-vessel "backdoor" rights-issue injection materialises on terms
   that favour the injecting party** — diluting today's shareholders to absorb assets priced
   in a market the industry press itself describes as at record highs, with the fleet
   revaluation gains flowing largely to the vessel-injecting party's implied stake rather than
   to existing minority holders. This is the textbook related-party value-transfer mechanism
   the brief specifically asked me to investigate, and it is presently an **open, unconfirmed,
   but actively rumoured** risk, not a hypothetical one.
4. **Zero dividend continues indefinitely** even after the quasi-reorganisation, either
   because shareholders do not approve it, because covenant restrictions on distributions to
   shareholders bind even after the accounting deficit clears, or because free cash flow (on
   the 1.1% TTM FCF-yield reading, as against the possibly-erroneous 17%-equivalent P/FCF
   reading) simply is not there once the acquisition/capex programme is funded — leaving
   shareholders no way to realise the cycle even if the underlying business performs.
