## What the business actually is

**FACT.** ESSA operates exactly two production assets:

| Asset | Location | Capacity | Feed gas from | Offtake |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ammonia (PT Panca Amara Utama, "PAU") | Batui, Banggai, Central Sulawesi | ~650–700k MT/yr | JOB Pertamina–Medco E&P Tomori Sulawesi (Senoro field), 55 MMSCFD | Mitsubishi Corporation, long-term, **take-or-pay** |
| LPG + condensate | Palembang, South Sumatra | 174 t/day LPG (~60–70k MT/yr); 410 bbl/day condensate | Pertamina (Cambai pipeline, Lembak field) | Pertamina, FOB plant |

**FACT.** Ammonia is the business. LPG contributes only ~13–16% of revenue
(BRI Danareksa estimate). LPG is effectively a tolling arrangement with
Pertamina: condensate is *returned* to Pertamina in exchange for a handling fee.

**FACT.** PAU began operating in 2018 (construction from 2015, cost ~USD 830mn).
ESSA raised its stake in PAU to 70% in March 2023.

**FACT.** Main ammonia destination markets: South Korea, China, Taiwan.

**INFERENCE.** This is a one-asset company with a minority partner holding 30%
of that asset. Consolidated financials therefore overstate the economic interest
of ESSA shareholders in the ammonia stream. Any per-share valuation must check
the minority-interest deduction.

## Priority 1 — the economics of the asset (the gas contract)

This is the central question, and the answer is unusual and important.

**FACT.** ESSA's own 14 Dec 2012 announcement of ESDM (Ministry of Energy and
Mineral Resources) approval of PAU's gas price states: *"PAU's gas price will be
linked to an indexed Ammonia price."* At the reference ammonia price of
USD 730/MT, the gas price was USD 8.60/MMBTU. Volume allocated: 55 MMSCFD from
the Senoro-Toili working area.

**This is the single most important structural fact about ESSA, and it cuts
against the usual "cheap allocated domestic gas" thesis.**

**INFERENCE — ESSA does not have a subsidised fixed-price gas advantage.** The
task framing asked whether ESSA sits low on the global cost curve because of
cheap contracted domestic gas. It does not, in the way that phrase normally
means. ESSA's gas is *allocated* domestically (an ESDM allocation decision, not
an open-market purchase) but it is *priced by formula against the ammonia
price*. The upstream partner — a Pertamina/Medco joint operating body, i.e. a
state-linked counterparty — captures a share of the ammonia upside.

**INFERENCE — but this is a margin-stabiliser, not a disadvantage.** Indexation
cuts both ways: when ammonia collapses, ESSA's largest input cost falls with it.
The reported history is consistent with *partial* indexation:

| Year | Revenue (IDR bn) | EBIT (IDR bn) | EBIT margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 (ammonia supercycle) | 13,100 | 5,404 | 41.2% |
| 2023 | 6,178 | 1,380 | 22.3% |
| 2024 | 5,398 | 1,469 | 27.2% |
| 2025 | 5,283 | 1,268 | 24.0% |

Revenue fell 60% peak-to-trough; EBIT margin compressed from 41% to ~24% but
**never went negative and has been remarkably stable at 22–27% across three
post-peak years** despite ammonia prices roughly halving. A producer paying
market-linked or fixed gas would have shown far more violent margin swings. A
pure toll would have shown a flat margin. The observed pattern — big margin
compression off the peak, then stability in a wide band of ammonia prices — is
what partial ammonia-indexed feedstock pricing produces.

**THESIS.** ESSA is better understood as a **spread business with a
contractually damped spread** than as a leveraged bet on the ammonia price. This
materially reduces downside risk versus a typical commodity chemical producer,
and materially caps upside in a supercycle. It is a genuine, if unglamorous,
structural feature — and it is probably the reason the screener's Safety Score is
95.6 while the Quality Score is only 61.7.

**FACT.** BRI Danareksa models ESSA's grey ammonia **cash cost at ~USD 160–164/MT**
against a Middle East ammonia price assumption of ~USD 306/MT and US Tampa
~USD 428/MT. On those numbers ESSA is a comfortably profitable, mid-cost
producer — not a top-decile Middle East cost producer, but well inside the
money.

**FACT — plant utilisation is exceptional.** BRI Danareksa reports ESSA ammonia
plant utilisation at **114% of nameplate** (5-year context), guided to ~105% in
FY25 only because of a planned 4-week turnaround, returning to 114%. LPG
utilisation likewise ~114–119%. Running a KBR-licensed ammonia train at 114% of
nameplate sustainably is an operational credential, not an accounting artefact.

**FACT — offtake is contracted, not spot.** Mitsubishi Corporation holds a
long-term offtake agreement on a **take-or-pay** basis.

**INFERENCE.** Take-or-pay offtake + ammonia-indexed feedstock + 114%
utilisation = volume risk and margin risk are both structurally suppressed. What
remains is price level risk (damped) and, critically, **gas supply continuity
risk**.

### The gas contract expiry — the biggest single unresolved risk

**FACT (single source, needs verification).** A project-intelligence database
(DMS Universe) states JOBPMTS supplies 55 MMSCFD to PAU **"up until December
2027."**

**Insufficient evidence — requires further research.** I could not verify the
contract end date, extension terms, or renewal pricing from a primary source
(annual report, IDX disclosure, or ESDM allocation decree). ESSA's own 2012
announcement does not state a tenor.

**THESIS — this is the thesis-critical variable and it is ~18 months away.** If
the Senoro gas contract genuinely expires end-2027 and renewal is uncertain or
repriced adversely, essentially 85% of ESSA's earnings power is at risk on a
short horizon. Conversely, if the contract is long-dated or has been extended
(the blue-ammonia plan implies management assumes gas through at least 2035),
the risk is largely illusory. **The blue-ammonia project itself — a USD 100–150mn
retrofit targeting 2028 startup and modelled by the sell-side through FY35 — is
strong circumstantial evidence that management and its counterparties expect gas
supply well beyond 2027 (INFERENCE).** No rational board sanctions a 2028-startup
retrofit on a feedstock contract expiring in 2027. But this must be confirmed
from filings, not inferred. Agent 5 (Thesis Killer) should treat this as the
primary kill shot to test.

**Also unresolved:** Senoro field reserve life and depletion profile; whether
the ESDM allocation could be redirected to a competing domestic user (fertiliser
SOEs have lobbied hard for gas allocation); whether the HGBT (harga gas bumi
tertentu) subsidised industrial gas price regime applies to PAU. *Insufficient
evidence — requires further research.*

## Priority 2 — the growth projects: committed vs announced

**The clear finding: nothing is committed. Both projects are pre-FID studies.**

### Blue ammonia (retrofit of the existing PAU plant)

- **FACT.** Plan is to convert the existing grey ammonia plant to blue ammonia
  via CCUS, targeting **70% carbon capture ratio**, startup **4Q28F**.
- **FACT.** Estimated capex **USD 100–150mn**.
- **FACT.** Status as of Apr 2025: **Feasibility Study Phase II** (surface and
  subsurface facilities, well integrity). Not FID.
- **FACT.** BRI Danareksa models the project lifting ESSA's earnings run-rate
  from USD 30–40mn/yr (FY25–28F) to **USD 42–52mn/yr (FY29–35F)**, with ammonia
  segment GPM dipping to 27% in FY28F then recovering to 30–33% from FY30F.
- **FACT.** Blue ammonia currently prices only **6–12% above grey** (S&P
  tracker); BRI Danareksa assumes 11–14%; ESSA management hopes for ~20%.
  Incremental cash cost ~USD 34/MT today, ~USD 29/MT by 2028, ~USD 23/MT by 2030.

**INFERENCE.** The blue ammonia economics are *thin and premium-dependent*.
On BRI Danareksa's own table, the FY2029 blue ASP is USD 348/MT vs grey cash
cost USD 164 and blue cash cost USD 184 — i.e. the entire project value rests on
a price premium (14%) that exceeds the cost premium (12%) by a mere 2
percentage points of ASP. **If the blue premium comes in at the low end of the
observed 6–12% range, the retrofit is value-neutral to value-destructive**
(INFERENCE, from the sell-side's own numbers). This is a real capital-allocation
risk, not a free option.

**INFERENCE — but the retrofit route is the sensible one.** Retrofitting grey
capacity is 33–66% cheaper than greenfield blue capacity, at the cost of only
~66–70% capture versus ~95% for a new autothermal reformer. ESSA is choosing the
capital-light path. That is a point in management's favour.

### SAF (sustainable aviation fuel)

- **FACT.** Greenfield HEFA facility planned at **Batang, Central Java**,
  feedstock **used cooking oil**, capacity **~200k MTPA (~260mn litres/yr)**.
- **FACT.** Status as of Dec 2025 public expose: **Pre-FEED just begun.** Still
  selecting site, still selecting technology provider, still to do basic
  engineering design and finalise EPC contractor. Management said construction
  target is "within the next 3 years" and the process needs "2–3 years."
- **FACT.** Management stated at the Dec 2025 public expose that **FY2026 capex
  is only ~USD 20mn, entirely for LPG and ammonia maintenance**, with **zero
  capex budgeted for SAF** ("we still have sufficient cash so it does not
  require capex") and **zero for other projects under exploration**.
- **FACT.** BRI Danareksa explicitly excludes SAF from estimates; a naive
  calculation at USD 1.2/litre unsubsidised US SAF price would give ~USD 312mn of
  incremental revenue — more than doubling FY28F revenue.

**INFERENCE.** SAF is an aspiration, not a project. Pre-FEED with no site, no
technology provider, no EPC contractor and no capex budget is roughly the
earliest stage at which a project can be said to exist at all. The 1Q28 startup
date in the April 2025 note was already implausible and the December 2025 public
expose effectively pushed it out ("construction can be built in 3 years").

**INFERENCE — the SAF economics are unproven and the risk is asymmetric
downward.** SAF sells at ~2x fossil jet fuel and is only viable where policy
mandates or subsidies exist (EU RED III/ReFuelEU, US 45Z/California LCFS).
Indonesia has no comparable mandate. A merchant Indonesian HEFA plant selling
into a policy-driven export market, competing for used cooking oil feedstock
against European and Singaporean buyers (Neste's Singapore plant is the
incumbent regional aggregator), is a substantially harder business than
converting allocated gas into contracted ammonia. **THESIS: this is
diversification away from ESSA's one genuine advantage.**

### On soda ash

**FACT.** Indonesia's first soda ash plant is being developed at **Bontang, East
Kalimantan** (groundbreaking targeted 2025). **I found no evidence that ESSA is
a participant.** The task brief's premise of an ESSA soda ash venture is not
supported by anything I located; ESSA's own communications name SAF and blue
ammonia as the growth agenda, and management listed methanol, hydrogen, urea and
fertiliser as *unevaluated* possibilities. *If an ESSA soda ash link exists it is
not in the public record I reached — insufficient evidence, requires further
research.*

## Industry Structure

**FACT (BRI Danareksa / Bloomberg NEF / IEA data).**

- Global ammonia demand ~195 Mt/yr (2024, +3% yoy), projected 213 Mt/yr by 2030
  (1.4% CAGR). Production 205 Mt/yr against 247 Mt/yr capacity — **~80% global
  utilisation, i.e. persistent structural overcapacity.**
- Fertiliser is **70–80% of demand**. Non-fertiliser (urea derivatives,
  chemicals, explosives) ~20%, growing slightly faster.
- Demand concentration: China 30%, EU 12%, US 11%, India 10%.
- Supply concentration: China ~29% of capacity, Russia 9%, India 8%, US 8%.
  Structure "largely unchanged since 1990."
- **The industry is extremely fragmented: 825 producers; the top five hold only
  14% of global capacity.** Producers are explicit price takers.
- Natural gas is **~90% of ammonia production cost**.
- Only ~20 Mt/yr (~10% of production) is traded internationally. Exporters:
  Trinidad & Tobago 34%, **Indonesia 16%**, Algeria 12%. Importers: US 21%,
  India 18%, Morocco 14%. Russia's ~4.4 Mt/yr export ceased with the 2022 war.
- Price history: ~USD 239/MT (US Tampa) in 2019–20 → peak **USD 1,625/MT in Mar
  2022** → ~USD 285/MT in 3Q23. Never returned to pre-war levels.
- Clean ammonia: blue supply only 1.7 Mt/yr by 2025F, 6.2 Mt by 2027F, 15.6 Mt
  by 2030F, ~80% of it US-based from 2028. Blue demand emerges from 2028F,
  14.4 Mt/yr by 2030F — 66% existing uses (driven by EU CBAM/RED III), 33% power
  generation (Japan/Korea/Singapore co-firing), 1% marine fuel.

**INFERENCE — this is a bad industry with a good niche.** 825 producers, 14%
top-five share, 80% utilisation and 90% input-cost exposure to a single
commodity is a textbook price-taker structure with no pricing power at the firm
level. ESSA cannot escape that. What it *can* do — and does — is sit in the
right geography with contracted offtake and formula-linked feedstock.

**INFERENCE — the hydrogen-carrier story is real but slow and mostly not
ESSA's.** Japan/Korea co-firing and marine fuel are genuine future demand
sources, and Indonesia is well placed to serve North Asia on freight. But 80% of
blue supply through 2028+ is US-based (45Q-subsidised), and the US 45Q credit is
under active repeal discussion. ESSA's 70%-capture retrofit would not meet the
strictest EU CBAM/RED III thresholds that reward ~95% capture. **THESIS: ESSA is
positioned to capture the *middling* clean-ammonia premium, not the top of it.**

**FACT.** ESSA's location is a genuine logistics advantage: Banggai, Central
Sulawesi, ships into South Korea, China and Taiwan — the exact markets driving
clean-ammonia co-firing demand — at far shorter freight distance than Trinidad,
Algeria or US Gulf suppliers. Indonesia is already the world's #2 ammonia
exporter at 16% of trade.

## Competitive Advantage

**Real, narrow, and contractual rather than structural:**

1. **FACT.** ESDM-allocated domestic gas at a formula price — a licence-like
   position that a new entrant cannot simply buy. Indonesian gas allocation is a
   ministerial decision.
2. **FACT.** Take-or-pay offtake with Mitsubishi Corporation removes volume risk.
3. **FACT.** 114% of nameplate utilisation — genuine operating competence.
4. **FACT.** Freight advantage into North Asia versus Atlantic-basin exporters.
5. **FACT.** Net cash. Total debt IDR 3.2bn against cash IDR 2,947bn — the
   company retired essentially all debt between 2022 (IDR 4,835bn) and 2025.

**Not advantages:**

- No pricing power (825-producer fragmented commodity).
- No brand, no switching costs, no network effect, no scale advantage — at
  ~650k MT/yr ESSA is a rounding error against 247 Mt of global capacity.
- Cost position is mid-curve, not low-curve. Middle East and US Gulf producers
  with genuinely cheap associated gas sit below ESSA.

**INFERENCE.** The moat is a *contract and a permit*, not a franchise. That is a
real but **finite and datable** advantage. This is precisely why the gas contract
tenor question dominates everything else.

helpful for permits and gas allocation (a genuine positive for the gas renewal

than minorities.

**Insufficient evidence — requires further research:** actual related-party
transaction disclosures in the annual report; terms of the PAU minority
shareholder agreement; whether the 2023 additional 10% PAU acquisition was done
at arm's length and at what price.

830mn-dollar greenfield ammonia plant, runs it at 114% of nameplate, survived a

**Positive on capital-allocation restraint (FACT).** At the December 2025 public
expose management explicitly declined to set FY26 revenue or profit targets,
budgeted only USD 20mn maintenance capex, and allocated nothing to SAF or other
projects. A management team sitting on IDR 2.9trn of net cash that refuses to
commit it to a fashionable project is behaving better than most.

## Capital Allocation

**FACT.**
- Debt eliminated: IDR 4,835bn (2022) → IDR 1,657bn (2024) → IDR 3.2bn (2025).
- Cash IDR 2,947bn; net cash IDR 2,945bn; current ratio 11.5x.
- Dividend IDR 52/share, ~8.2% yield, payout ratio only 20%.
- Capex has been minimal and *rising modestly*: IDR 25.6bn (2022) → 141.7bn
  (2025). FY26 budget ~USD 20mn (~IDR 358bn) maintenance.
- No dilution: shares 17.0–17.2bn, flat since 2023 (Piotroski "No Dilution" = 1).
- 2023: acquired an additional 10% of PAU, taking the stake to 70%.

**INFERENCE — the record is genuinely good, but the capital is now idle.** The
sequence deleverage → dividend → no dilution → restraint on speculative capex is
close to textbook for a cyclical. But the company now sits on ~26% of its market
cap in net cash, earning deposit rates, with a 20% payout ratio and two
uncommitted projects. **THESIS: the next capital allocation decision is the one
that matters, and it is unmade.** Three outcomes: (a) a disciplined USD 100–150mn
blue retrofit that extends the asset's life and adds a modest premium — decent;
(b) a large greenfield SAF commitment into a policy-dependent export market with
no Indonesian mandate — value-destructive risk; (c) continued accumulation with a
raised payout — fine for holders. The market is arguably discounting (b).

**FACT.** The 20% payout ratio against 20% FCF yield and net cash means the
dividend is covered ~5x by free cash flow. There is substantial room to raise it.

## Industry Outlook

**Near term (FACT/ASSUMPTION).** BRI Danareksa assumes ammonia stabilises at
~USD 428/MT US Tampa and ~USD 306/MT Middle East long term, versus USD 486 and
USD 348 in 2024. Global overcapacity (80% utilisation), returning Chinese
domestic supply, and SEA inventory build cap prices. Fertiliser demand grows
~1%/yr; total ammonia demand ~1.4% CAGR to 2030.

**Medium term.** Clean ammonia demand (14.4 Mt/yr by 2030) is the only source of
above-trend growth, but supply is projected to grow faster than demand in the
blue segment and ~80% of it is US-based.

**INFERENCE.** Neither a boom nor a bust. Volume growth is demographic and slow;
price is capped by fragmentation and spare capacity; the clean-ammonia premium is
real but modest (6–12% today) and contested.

**Industry: NEUTRAL**

Slight negative (chronic overcapacity, price-taker fragmentation, no return to
pre-2021 pricing) offset by slight positive (clean-ammonia demand emerging,
Indonesia's structural export position, Russian supply still absent).

**Company: NEUTRAL**

Rationale: ESSA's operating performance is above industry average (114%
utilisation, take-or-pay offtake, ammonia-indexed feedstock, net cash), which
argues TAILWIND — but this is fully offset by (a) the unresolved 2027 gas
contract question, (b) growth projects that are entirely uncommitted with 3+ year
lead times and unproven economics, and (c) a 30% minority in the only asset that
matters. Recent quarters are genuinely strong (Q1-26 revenue +33.6% yoy, net
income +61.5% yoy, EBIT margin back to 36%), which is a real positive not yet
reflected in the trailing figures.

## Structural Advantages

1. **ESDM-allocated gas at an ammonia-indexed formula price.** A permit-like
   position unavailable to entrants, and — because the input price falls when the
   output price falls — a contractual damper that has held EBIT margin in a
   22–27% band through a 60% revenue collapse. This is the single most
   underappreciated feature of the business.
2. **Take-or-pay offtake with Mitsubishi Corporation plus 114%-of-nameplate
   utilisation.** Volume risk is contractually and operationally removed; the
   plant is demonstrably well run.
3. **Freight and geography.** Banggai sits close to the Korean, Chinese and
   Taiwanese markets that drive both fertiliser imports and prospective
   ammonia co-firing demand, in a country already supplying 16% of world ammonia
   trade — versus Atlantic-basin competitors 8,000+ nautical miles away.

*(Runner-up: a fortress balance sheet — net cash ~26% of market cap, current
ratio 11.5x, Altman Z 11.3, interest coverage 66x.)*

## Structural Problems

1. **A single asset, a single feed-gas counterparty, and a gas contract whose
   tenor I could not verify — with one source indicating December 2027.** ~85% of
   earnings depend on one pipeline from one JOB into one plant. Even if renewal
   is near-certain, the *repricing* of that contract is an uncontrolled variable
   on a short horizon.
2. **No pricing power whatsoever.** 825 producers, top five at 14% of capacity,
   80% global utilisation, and 90% of cost in one input. ESSA at ~650k MT/yr is
   0.26% of world capacity. It will never earn an excess return on price; only on
   cost, contract and operation.
3. **Both growth options are pre-FID studies with weak or unproven economics.**
   Blue ammonia's value rests on a 14% price premium exceeding a 12% cost
   premium — a 2pp margin of error on the sell-side's own table, against an
   observed market premium of only 6–12% and a 70% capture ratio that may not
   qualify for the strictest EU regimes. SAF is a greenfield HEFA plant with no
   site, no technology provider, no EPC contractor, no capex budget, no
   Indonesian mandate, and Neste's Singapore plant already aggregating regional
   used-cooking-oil feedstock.

*(Runner-up: 30% minority in PAU plus overlapping Laroya/Akraya/PAU
directorships — a structure where minority value can leak, and which I could not
fully vet.)*

## Business Verdict: **CYCLICAL**

**Reasoning.** ESSA is a commodity chemical converter in a fragmented,
overcapacitised, price-taker industry. It is not a COMPOUNDER — there is no
reinvestment runway at high returns; ROE is 11.3% (5-year average 10.0%), ROIC
15.5%, and the company cannot deploy its own cash. It is not ASSET VALUE — it
trades at 1.35x book and NCAV is only IDR 180/share against a 655 price, so
there is no asset-based floor. It is not a TURNAROUND — nothing is broken. It is
not a VALUE TRAP on the evidence found: F-Score 7, Altman Z 11.3, net cash,
positive and accelerating recent earnings (Q1-26 net income +61.5% yoy), a
covered 8.2% dividend, and no red flags from the screener.

It is a **CYCLICAL with unusually damped cyclicality** — which is why the
screener's own WATCH (MOS 10%, short of the 30% buy threshold) looks right rather
than harsh.

## Answer to the key question

**A decent-but-not-great business at a fair-to-slightly-cheap price — closest to
"mediocre business at a cheap price", but with the important qualifier that the
gas indexation makes it less mediocre than a commodity ammonia producer normally
is.**

It is *not* a good business temporarily mispriced: at 12x earnings, 6.5x EV/EBIT
and 1.35x book, with 10% ROE and an 11.7% blended upside, the price is roughly
right for what it is. It is *not* structurally cheap for a hidden reason that I
uncovered — the balance sheet is clean and the earnings are real and cash-backed
(FCF yield 20%).

**THESIS.** The market is pricing ESSA as a mid-cycle commodity converter with an
unresolved feedstock question and an uncommitted growth plan, and that is a fair
description. The bull case is not "cheap ammonia gas" — it is (a) confirmation
that the gas contract runs well past 2027 on similar terms, (b) a disciplined
blue retrofit rather than a speculative SAF greenfield, and (c) a payout ratio
lifted from 20% toward something reflecting a business with no reinvestment
needs. The bear case is a 2027 gas repricing, or USD 300mn+ committed to SAF.
