## 2. Priority 1 — the economics of the asset

### 2a. The gas price formula: indexed to ammonia, not subsidised

**FACT.** ESSA's own announcement of ESDM approval of PAU's gas price states:
*"PAU's gas price will be linked to an indexed Ammonia price."* At the reference
ammonia price of USD 730/MT the gas price was USD 8.60/MMBTU.

**The task brief asked whether ESSA is low on the global cost curve because of
cheap allocated domestic gas. The answer is no — and the reality is more
interesting.** ESSA's gas is *allocated* by ESDM (a ministerial decision, not an
open-market purchase, and therefore a licence-like barrier) but it is *priced by
formula against the ammonia price*. The upstream JOB — a Pertamina/Medco
vehicle — participates in ammonia upside. ESSA does not enjoy a Middle-East-style
fixed cheap-gas rent.

**FACT — hard confirmation from the accounts.** Note 25 discloses raw-material
purchases exceeding 10% of revenue:

| | 1Q25 | 1Q26 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ammonia revenue (USD) | 59,477,413 | 85,581,149 | **+43.9%** |
| Gas purchased from JOBPMTS (USD) | 22,725,957 | 28,489,856 | **+25.4%** |
| Gas cost as % of ammonia revenue | 38.2% | **33.3%** | −4.9pp |

**INFERENCE — the indexation is real but partial.** Gas cost rose materially
with ammonia revenue (not fixed) but by well under half as much in percentage
terms (not a pure toll). Across the cycle the same signature appears in the
annual EBIT margin: 41.2% at the 2022 supercycle peak, then **22.3% / 27.2% /
24.0%** in 2023/24/25 as ammonia prices roughly halved — heavy compression off
the peak, then remarkable stability across a wide band of ammonia prices, and
never a loss.

**THESIS.** ESSA is best understood as a **spread business with a contractually
damped spread**, not a leveraged bet on the ammonia price. Downside is
structurally protected (the largest input cost falls with the output price);
supercycle upside is structurally shared away. This is the reason the screener
shows Safety 95.6 against Quality 61.7, and it is the most underappreciated
feature of the business.

**FACT.** The LPG side shows the same shape: Pertamina EP gas cost was 36.1% of
LPG-segment revenue in 1Q25 and 39.3% in 1Q26, and the LPG *selling* price
formula was itself renegotiated with Pertamina Patra Niaga on 12 Nov 2024.

### 2b. Cost position, capacity and utilisation

**FACT.** BRI Danareksa models ESSA's grey-ammonia **cash cost at ~USD 160–164/MT**
against long-run assumptions of USD 306/MT (Middle East) and USD 428/MT (US
Tampa). ESSA is a comfortably in-the-money **mid-cost** producer — not top-decile
Middle East, but far from marginal.

**FACT.** Ammonia plant utilisation runs at **~114% of nameplate** (guided to
105% in FY25 only for a 4-week turnaround); LPG at 114–119%. Sustaining 114% on a
KBR-licensed ammonia train is a genuine operating credential.

**FACT.** 1Q26 ammonia segment margin of 47.2% and net income +61.5% yoy show the
asset is currently earning very well.

### 2c. The customer — and why it is also the minority shareholder

**FACT (1Q26 FS, Notes 24 and 29).** PAU's 24 Jun 2015 offtake agreement requires
**the entire ammonia production to be purchased by Genesis Corporation, Japan, on
an FOB basis**. Genesis is disclosed as a related party **under common control**.
Genesis was **89.88% of consolidated revenue** in 1Q26 (85.43% in 1Q25) and
**79.2% of trade receivables**. The FS state: *"Sales prices are determined based
on agreement."*

**FACT.** Genesis Corporation holds **30.00% of PAU** — i.e. the sole customer is
also the minority partner in the only asset that matters.

**FACT — a genuine mitigant.** On 26 Jan 2023 PAU and Genesis amended the
agreement *"on aligning the ammonia pricing formula with relevant regional price
index."* Pricing is therefore now formula-linked to a published index rather than
negotiated ad hoc.

**INFERENCE — the residual risk is real but bounded.** Genesis buys 100% of the
output while bearing only 30% of PAU's economics, so it captures the entire
downstream trading margin and has a structural incentive to set the FOB transfer
price low; ESSA shareholders bear 70% of any shortfall. The January 2023
index-linkage amendment is precisely the right fix and materially reduces this,
but it does not eliminate the question of *which* index and *what* differential.
This is not classic controlling-family tunnelling — it is ordinary JV
transfer-pricing tension with a Japanese partner — but it is a real leakage
vector that a minority shareholder cannot observe.

**Note on a source conflict (FACT).** BRI Danareksa describes the offtaker as
**Mitsubishi Corporation** on a take-or-pay basis, and a 2015 press report also
records a Mitsubishi offtake. The audited financial statements name **Genesis
Corporation** as the counterparty to the binding agreement. *I could not reconcile
these; the take-or-pay characterisation rests on the sell-side note, not on the
filings I read. Insufficient evidence — requires further research.*

### 2d. The 2027 contract cliff — the dominant fact

**FACT (1Q26 FS, Note 31 — Commitments and Significant Agreements).** Every
material contract expires in 2027:

| Contract | Counterparty | Terms | **Expiry** |
|---|---|---|---|
| PAU Gas Sale & Purchase Agreement (13 Mar 2014, amended 11 Jan 2018) | JOB Pertamina Medco Tomori Sulawesi | DCQ raised 55 → **62 MMSCFD**, effective 14 Feb 2019 | **2027** |
| PAU ammonia offtake (24 Jun 2015) | Genesis Corporation | 100% of production, FOB | **3 Dec 2027** |
| LPG feed gas GSPA, 2nd amendment (20 Sep 2023) | Pertamina EP | 70 MMSCFD, 456.81 BSCF total | **31 Dec 2027** |
| LPG sales agreement (1 Aug 2022, amended 12 Nov 2024) | Pertamina Patra Niaga | 62,000 MT/yr | **31 Jul 2027** |
| PAU technical/advisory/marketing services | PT Akraya International (shareholder) | **4% of PAU EBITDA** | **3 Dec 2027** |
| PAU gas/CSR/government-liaison services | PT Mega Consultindo Perdana | **2% of PAU EBITDA** | **3 Dec 2027** |

**FACT (1Q26 FS, Note 32 — Concentration Risk).** ESSA's own words: JOBPMTS *"is
the sole supplier available at the moment"*, and *"termination of such contracts
with Pertamina EP and/or JOBPMTS could result in the discontinuation of the
Company's and/or subsidiary's business."*

**THESIS — this is the thesis, and it is binary.** As of the most recent filing I
could reach (1Q26, published April 2026), **no renewal or extension of the PAU
gas agreement has been publicly disclosed.** 100% of revenue rests on contracts
expiring within ~17 months.

Arguments that renewal happens on acceptable terms:
- **INFERENCE — management's own capital plan implies it.** A USD 100–150mn blue
  ammonia retrofit targeting 4Q28 startup, modelled through FY2035, is
  incoherent unless the board expects gas well beyond 2027. Boards do not
  sanction 2028-startup retrofits on 2027 feedstock.
- **INFERENCE — policy favours it.** Indonesian *hilirisasi* (downstreaming)
  policy strongly favours domestic value-add over raw LNG export, and PAU is the
  flagship domestic downstream user of Senoro gas.
- **INFERENCE — the pricing formula aligns the counterparty.** Because JOBPMTS
  already participates in ammonia upside through the indexed formula, its
  incentive to redirect gas is weaker than under a fixed-price contract.
- **INFERENCE — Garibaldi Thohir's 14.6% stake** brings political access that has
  historically helped with Indonesian gas allocation.

Arguments the risk is real:
- **FACT.** Senoro gas has a large competing offtaker in Donggi-Senoro LNG.
  Export netbacks compete directly with PAU's allocation.
- **INFERENCE.** Even if volume is renewed, the *price formula* will be
  renegotiated from a position of weakness: PAU has one available supplier and a
  stranded USD 800mn asset; JOBPMTS has an alternative buyer. The renewal risk is
  less "no gas" than "worse gas price."
- **FACT.** Senoro field reserve life and depletion profile — unverified.

**This single variable should be the Thesis Killer's primary line of attack, and
it is the reason a 10% margin of safety is inadequate compensation.**

## 3. Priority 2 — growth projects: committed vs announced

### Blue ammonia (retrofit of the existing PAU plant) — NOT committed

- **FACT.** Convert the existing grey plant to blue via CCUS; **70% capture
  target**; startup **4Q28F**; capex **USD 100–150mn**.
- **FACT.** Status as of Apr 2025: **Feasibility Study Phase II** (surface,
  subsurface, well integrity). **No FID. No capex budgeted for FY2026.**
- **FACT.** BRI Danareksa models the project lifting the earnings run-rate from
  USD 30–40mn/yr (FY25–28F) to **USD 42–52mn/yr (FY29–35F)**.

**INFERENCE — the economics are dangerously thin.** On BRI Danareksa's own
FY2029 table: blue ASP USD 348/MT vs grey USD 305 (a **14% price premium**),
against blue cash cost USD 184 vs grey USD 164 (a **12% cost premium**). The
entire project value sits in a 2-percentage-point gap. Meanwhile the *observed*
market premium is only **6–12%** (S&P Global), and management's hoped-for ~20% is
unsupported — the FS note that the blue-ammonia pricing mechanism *"remains
underdeveloped due to limited blue ammonia production and the absence of a clear
framework outside the EU."* **If the premium lands at the low end, the retrofit
is value-neutral to value-destructive.**

**INFERENCE — a further specification problem.** At 70% capture, ESSA's retrofit
sits below the ~95% capture achievable with a new autothermal reformer, and may
not clear the strictest EU CBAM / RED III thresholds that pay the best premium.
ESSA is positioned to earn the *middling* clean-ammonia premium, not the top of
it. Retrofitting is nonetheless 33–66% cheaper than greenfield blue capacity —
management is choosing the capital-light path, which is to its credit.

### SAF (sustainable aviation fuel) — further along than the public expose implied

**FACT (1Q26 FS, Note 31d and 31e) — the subsidiary is "ESM":**
- **25 Oct 2024:** ESM signed an **Investment Commitment Agreement** with PT
  Kawasan Industri Terpadu Batang (KITB) to lease **103,679 m²** at
  IDR 1,000,000/m² — total land commitment **IDR 103.679bn (~USD 5.8mn)**.
  As of 31 Mar 2026 ESM had paid only a **land booking deposit**; the Industrial
  Land Utilization Agreement (PPTI) was **not yet executed** and no land handover
  had occurred.
- **1 Dec 2025:** ESM signed a **FEED agreement with PT Tripatra Engineers** for a
  SAF plant of **5,000 barrels per stream day** (~200k MTPA / ~260mn litres/yr),
  HEFA technology, used-cooking-oil feedstock, at Batang, Central Java. Scope
  includes the EPC capital cost estimate to support a subsequent EPC tender.

**FACT.** At the 3 Dec 2025 public expose management stated FY2026 capex is
**~USD 20mn, entirely LPG and ammonia maintenance**, with **zero budgeted for
SAF** ("we still have sufficient cash so it does not require capex") and zero for
other projects. Management also said construction is targeted "within the next 3
years" and the process needs "2–3 years."

**INFERENCE.** SAF has crossed from aspiration into early execution — a FEED
contractor is appointed and land is reserved — but **it is still pre-FID**: no
technology licensor confirmed, no EPC contract, no PPTI signed, no capital cost
estimate published, and no capex allocated. The BRI Danareksa "1Q28 startup" is
already obsolete; late-decade is realistic.

**THESIS — SAF is diversification away from ESSA's one genuine advantage.** SAF
sells at ~2x fossil jet fuel (USD 1.2/l vs USD 0.57/l) and is viable only where
mandates or subsidies exist — EU ReFuelEU/RED III, US 45Z, California LCFS.
**Indonesia has no comparable mandate.** A merchant Indonesian HEFA plant selling
into a policy-driven export market, bidding for used cooking oil against European
buyers and Neste's Singapore plant (the incumbent regional UCO aggregator), is a
structurally harder business than converting ESDM-allocated gas into
index-priced contracted ammonia. BRI Danareksa's ~USD 312mn revenue figure is a
capacity × price arithmetic exercise, not an economic forecast, and the analyst
says so.

### Soda ash — no evidence of ESSA involvement

**FACT.** Indonesia's first soda ash plant is being developed at **Bontang, East
Kalimantan**. **I found no evidence that ESSA is a participant**, and nothing in
ESSA's filings, announcements or public expose mentions soda ash. ESSA's growth
agenda is blue ammonia and SAF; management listed methanol, hydrogen, urea and
fertiliser as *unevaluated* possibilities open to "disciplined evaluation."
*The brief's premise of an ESSA soda ash venture is not supported by the public
record I reached — insufficient evidence, requires further research.*

## 4. Industry Structure

**FACT (Bloomberg NEF / IEA / S&P Global data as cited in the BRIDS note).**

- Global ammonia demand ~**195 Mt/yr** (2024, +3% yoy) → **213 Mt/yr by 2030**
  (1.4% CAGR). Fertiliser is **70–80%** of demand, growing ~1%/yr.
- Production 205 Mt/yr against capacity 247 Mt/yr — **~80% global utilisation,
  i.e. chronic structural overcapacity**.
- Demand: China 30%, EU 12%, US 11%, India 10%. Supply: China ~29% of capacity,
  Russia 9%, India 8%, US 8% — *"largely unchanged since 1990."*
- **Extreme fragmentation: 825 producers; the top five hold only 14% of global
  capacity.** Producers are explicit price takers.
- **Natural gas is ~90% of ammonia production cost.**
- Only ~20 Mt/yr (~10% of output) is traded. Exporters: Trinidad & Tobago 34%,
  **Indonesia 16%**, Algeria 12%. Importers: US 21%, India 18%, Morocco 14%.
  Russia's ~4.4 Mt/yr export ceased in 2022 and has not returned.
- Prices: ~USD 239/MT (US Tampa) in 2019–20 → peak **USD 1,625/MT (Mar 2022)** →
  ~USD 285/MT (3Q23). Never back to pre-war levels.
- Clean ammonia: blue supply 1.7 Mt/yr (2025F) → 6.2 Mt (2027F) → 15.6 Mt
  (2030F), **~80% US-based from 2028** (45Q-subsidised, and 45Q is under active
  repeal discussion). Blue demand emerges from 2028F to 14.4 Mt/yr by 2030:
  66% existing fertiliser/chemical uses (EU CBAM, RED III), 33% power generation
  (Japan/Korea/Singapore co-firing), 1% marine fuel.

**INFERENCE — a bad industry with a good niche.** 825 producers, 14% top-five
share, 80% utilisation and 90% of cost in one commodity is a textbook price-taker
structure. ESSA at ~650k MT/yr is **0.26% of world capacity** and will never earn
an excess return on price — only on cost, contract, location and operations.

**INFERENCE — the hydrogen-carrier story is real, slow, and mostly not ESSA's.**
Japan/Korea co-firing and marine fuel are genuine emerging demand, and Indonesia
is well placed to serve North Asia. But blue supply is projected to grow faster
than blue demand, ~80% of it is US-based, and the premium is currently only
6–12%.

## 5. Competitive Position

**Real, narrow, contractual rather than structural:**

1. **FACT.** ESDM-allocated sole-source domestic gas — a licence-like position no
   entrant can simply buy.
2. **FACT.** 100% of output contracted to a single offtaker on an index-linked
   FOB formula — no marketing risk, no spot exposure.
3. **FACT.** 114% of nameplate utilisation, sustained.
4. **FACT.** Freight and geography: Banggai ships into Korea, China, Taiwan and
   Japan — the exact markets driving both fertiliser imports and prospective
   ammonia co-firing — versus Atlantic-basin exporters 8,000+ nm away. Indonesia
   is already the world's #2 ammonia exporter at 16% of trade.
5. **FACT.** Fortress balance sheet: total debt IDR 3.2bn against cash
   IDR 2,947bn; net cash ~26% of market cap; current ratio 11.5x; Altman Z 11.3;
   interest coverage 66x. Debt went from IDR 4,835bn (2022) to nil in three years.

**Not advantages:** no pricing power; no brand, switching costs or network
effects; no scale (0.26% of world capacity); cost position mid-curve, not
low-curve.

**INFERENCE.** The moat is **a permit and a contract, not a franchise** — real,
but finite and *datable*. That is exactly why the 2027 expiry dominates.

### Related-party fee architecture — the main governance concern

**FACT (1Q26 FS, Notes 26 and 29).**

| Item | 1Q26 (USD) | 1Q25 (USD) |
|---|---|---|
| Management fees in G&A (total) | 3,353,561 | 1,939,919 (+72.9% yoy) |
| — of which PT Akraya International | 2,383,146 (20.47% of G&A) | 1,448,558 |
| **Total salaries and benefits, all Directors and Commissioners** | **113,759** | 106,937 |

**FACT — the contractual fee terms.**
- **PT Akraya International** (a *shareholder*): 4% of PAU EBITDA, plus a separate
  1 Nov 2007 management-services agreement with the parent company.
- **PT Mega Consultindo Perdana**: 2% of PAU EBITDA for "gas, CSR and government
  liaising" services.
- **→ ~6% of PAU EBITDA is paid out to related parties before any dividend.**
- **31 Jan 2025:** Akraya signed an agreement with **ESM (the SAF entity)** for
  USD 125,000/quarter, rising after commercial production to **3% of ESM's EBITDA
  for 20 years** from commercial operation.

**INFERENCE — three flags.**
1. The controlling group extracts roughly **29x more through the related-party
   fee agreements than through disclosed board compensation** (USD 3.35mn vs
   USD 0.11mn per quarter). Annualised, related-party management fees of
   ~USD 13mn compare to parent net income of USD 40.3mn (FY2025). Value is being
   routed to controlling shareholders through a channel minorities do not share
   in, in preference to dividends they would.
2. **A 3% EBITDA royalty on the SAF project was locked in for 20 years in January
   2025 — before FID, before FEED, before any capital was committed.** The
   related party captured its economics on the growth project ahead of the
   shareholders who will fund it.
3. Notably, the Akraya and Mega Consultindo PAU agreements also expire 3 Dec
   2027 — so the 2027 event is a renegotiation of the entire commercial
   architecture, fees included. **That cuts both ways and is a genuine
   opportunity**: a 2027 reset without the 6% EBITDA leakage would be materially
   accretive to minorities.

**FACT — a real mitigant on governance.** The gas price is set by an ESDM-approved
formula with a state-linked counterparty, the ammonia price is index-linked since
Jan 2023, and the LPG business transacts with Pertamina entities. The *third-party*
legs of the business are hard to manipulate. The leakage is concentrated in the
service-fee layer, where it is at least disclosed and quantified.

## 7. Capital Allocation

**FACT.**
- Debt: IDR 4,835bn (2022) → 1,657bn (2024) → **3.2bn (2025)**. Net cash
  IDR 2,945bn.
- Dividend IDR 52/share, **~8.2% yield**, **payout ratio only 20%** — covered
  roughly 5x by free cash flow (FCF yield 20%, FCF TTM IDR 2,283bn).
- Capex minimal and rising modestly: IDR 25.6bn (2022) → 141.7bn (2025); FY26
  budget ~USD 20mn maintenance. 1Q26 additions to PP&E only USD 1.74mn.
- **No dilution:** 17,226,975,700 shares, unchanged (Piotroski "No Dilution" = 1).
- 2023: bought an additional 10% of PAU, taking the stake to 70%.

**INFERENCE — the record is good; the capital is now idle.** Deleverage →
dividend → no dilution → restraint on speculative capex is close to textbook for
a cyclical, and management's refusal at the December 2025 public expose to set
revenue or profit targets or to fund SAF prematurely is better behaviour than
most. But the company now holds ~26% of its market cap in net cash at deposit
rates, pays out only 20% of earnings, and has two uncommitted projects.

**THESIS — the decisive capital-allocation decision is unmade, and the market is
discounting the bad version of it.** Three paths: (a) a disciplined USD 100–150mn
blue retrofit that extends the asset's life — acceptable if the premium holds;
(b) a large greenfield SAF commitment into a policy-dependent export market with
no Indonesian mandate and a pre-agreed 3%-of-EBITDA related-party royalty —
value-destructive risk; (c) continued accumulation with a raised payout — good
for holders. There is ample room to raise the dividend.

## 8. Industry Outlook

**ASSUMPTION (BRI Danareksa).** Ammonia stabilises long term at ~USD 428/MT US
Tampa and ~USD 306/MT Middle East, versus USD 486 and USD 348 in 2024. Demand
grows ~1.4% CAGR to 2030; price is capped by 80% utilisation and fragmentation.
Clean ammonia (14.4 Mt/yr by 2030) is the only above-trend growth, but blue supply
is projected to outrun blue demand.

**Industry: NEUTRAL**
Chronic overcapacity, price-taker fragmentation, and no return to pre-2021 pricing
(negative) offset by emerging clean-ammonia demand, Indonesia's structural export
position, and absent Russian supply (positive).

**Company: NEUTRAL**
The net of two large and opposing forces. Operating momentum is a clear tailwind:
1Q26 revenue +33.6% yoy, net income +61.5% yoy, ammonia segment margin 47.2%,
utilisation 114%, zero debt. Against that sits a severe, *datable* headwind: 100%
of revenue on contracts expiring in 2027 with no disclosed renewal, plus a 30%
minority that is also the sole customer, plus ~6% of PAU EBITDA leaking to
related parties. **This rating flips to HEADWIND if the FY2026 annual report does
not disclose a gas-supply extension.**

## Structural Advantages

1. **Ammonia-indexed feedstock pricing under an ESDM-approved formula.** Verified
   in the accounts (gas cost 33–38% of ammonia revenue, moving with but not
   proportionally to it) and in the margin record (EBIT margin held in a 22–27%
   band through a 60% revenue collapse, never a loss). It converts a commodity
   converter into a damped-spread business — the most underappreciated feature of
   ESSA and the reason Safety scores 95.6.
2. **A fully contracted, sole-source, high-utilisation asset.** ESDM-allocated
   gas from the only available supplier, 100% of output pre-sold FOB on an
   index-linked formula, and a plant that runs at 114% of nameplate. Volume risk
   and marketing risk are both structurally removed.
3. **Fortress balance sheet plus North Asian freight advantage.** Zero net debt
   (net cash ~26% of market cap, current ratio 11.5x, Altman Z 11.3), an 8.2%
   dividend covered 5x by FCF, and a Sulawesi location that lands ammonia into
   Korea, Japan, Taiwan and China far cheaper than Trinidad, Algeria or the US
   Gulf.

## Structural Problems

1. **The 2027 cliff.** The PAU gas agreement, the Genesis ammonia offtake, the
   Pertamina EP LPG feed gas, the Pertamina Patra Niaga LPG sales agreement and
   both related-party service agreements **all expire in 2027** — ~17 months
   away, with no renewal disclosed as of the 1Q26 filing. ESSA's own Note 32
   states JOBPMTS is *"the sole supplier available"* and that termination *"could
   result in the discontinuation of the Company's… business."* Even a benign
   outcome (volume renewed) carries adverse repricing risk, because PAU
   negotiates from a stranded-asset position against a supplier with an LNG
   export alternative.
2. **No pricing power, and a subscale position in a fragmented commodity.** 825
   producers, top five at 14% of capacity, 80% global utilisation, 90% of cost in
   one input. ESSA is 0.26% of world capacity.
3. **Structural minority leakage.** ~6% of PAU EBITDA to related-party service
   agreements (Akraya 4%, Mega Consultindo 2%) before any dividend; a further 3%
   of ESM EBITDA pre-committed to Akraya for 20 years *before* the SAF project
   reached FID; related-party management fees running ~29x total board
   compensation; and a sole customer (Genesis) that owns 30% of the asset it buys
   100% of the output from, so it captures the full downstream trading margin
   while bearing only 30% of the upstream cost.

*(Runner-up problem: both growth options have weak or unproven economics — blue
ammonia's value rests on a 14% price premium exceeding a 12% cost premium, a 2pp
margin of error against an observed market premium of only 6–12%; SAF is a
pre-FID HEFA plant with no Indonesian mandate, competing for used cooking oil
against Neste Singapore.)*

## Business Verdict: **CYCLICAL**
### — with a binary 2027 contract-renewal event overlaid

**Reasoning.** ESSA is a commodity chemical converter in a fragmented,
overcapacitised, price-taker industry. Not a **COMPOUNDER**: no reinvestment
runway at high returns — ROE 11.3% (5-year average 10.0%), ROIC 15.5%, and the
company cannot deploy its own cash. Not **ASSET VALUE**: 1.35x book, NCAV
IDR 180/share against a 655 price — no asset floor. Not a **TURNAROUND**: nothing
is broken; 1Q26 was the best quarter in years. Not a **VALUE TRAP** on the
evidence — F-Score 7, Altman Z 11.3, net cash, 47% ammonia segment margins,
accelerating earnings, a covered 8.2% dividend, no screener red flags, and no
accounting concerns found.

It is a **CYCLICAL with unusually damped cyclicality** — which is why the
screener's WATCH (MOS 10%, short of the 30% threshold) is the right call rather
than a harsh one. The **SPECIAL SITUATION** label was considered and rejected:
the 2027 expiry is a risk event, not a value-unlocking catalyst.

## Answer to the key question

**A decent-but-not-great business at a fair price, with a large unpriced binary
risk in 2027. Closest to "mediocre business at a cheap price" — but it is neither
as mediocre nor as cheap as it first looks.**

Not *a good business temporarily mispriced*: at 12x earnings, 6.5x EV/EBIT and
1.35x book with 10% ROE, the price is roughly right for what it is; the screener's
11.7% blended upside is honest. Not *structurally cheap for a hidden reason* in
the accounting sense — earnings are real, cash-backed (20% FCF yield), and the
balance sheet is pristine.

**THESIS.** The market is pricing ESSA as a mid-cycle commodity converter with an
unresolved feedstock question and an uncommitted growth plan. That is a fair
description, and the 10% margin of safety does not compensate for a 2027 event in
which 100% of revenue is renegotiated. The bull case is not "cheap subsidised
gas" — it is three specific things: (1) confirmation that the JOBPMTS gas
agreement is extended beyond 2027 on comparable formula terms; (2) a disciplined
blue retrofit rather than a speculative SAF greenfield; (3) a payout ratio lifted
from 20% toward something appropriate for a business with no reinvestment needs
and 26% of market cap in idle cash — with the 2027 fee-agreement expiry as a
chance to end the 6%-of-EBITDA leakage. The bear case is an adverse gas
repricing, or USD 300mn+ committed to SAF with 3% of its EBITDA pre-promised to a
related party.

**Recommendation to the synthesis agent: hold at WATCH. The gas renewal is a
tradeable, datable catalyst — if it is confirmed on comparable terms, ESSA's
business quality score rises materially (to roughly 65–68) and the risk-adjusted
case improves sharply. Buying ahead of that disclosure is buying a binary.**
