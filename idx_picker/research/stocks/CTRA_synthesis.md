## Resolving the disagreement

The spread runs from "undervalued" (Agent 2) to 72/100 concern (Agent 5). Averaging
would produce a meaningless middle. The two are actually answering different questions,
and both are right.

**Agent 5 said the screener capitalised peak earnings.** Agent 2 tested that and found
it only explains ~20% of the valuation error. The presales-to-revenue transfer is a
stable one-year lag at ~0.89x, so mid-cycle EBIT is ~Rp3,044bn and `Normalised/Trailing`
should read **0.81, not 0.998** — a 10% haircut, not the 14–18% the presales decline
implies, because ~19–21% of revenue is recurring and the record 2024 vintage still feeds
FY2026. Agent 5 was directionally right and quantitatively too harsh.

**The larger error was the comparables lens — ~53% of the gap.** Peer median PE of 12.4x
is contradicted by the same cohort's own 0.77x P/B and by a broker large-cap developer
median of 7.0x PE / 0.6x P/BV. This is a defect in the screener, not in CTRA.

**Agent 1 found what neither of them had, and it is worse than either.** Forward revenue
cover — contract liabilities — fell from Rp11,019bn to Rp7,104bn, i.e. **~12 months of
cover down to ~7**. FY2026's decline is reported; FY2027's second decline is close to
arithmetically locked. Agent 2's one-year lag model implicitly assumes the cycle turns
after one down-year. The advance book says it does not.

**Agent 1 also corrected a premise carried into this research.** The joint operations are
PSAK 66 proportional, so profit is already net of partners and there is no minority
leakage. The real exposure is Rp1,566bn of interest-free, un-provisioned partner
receivables that **grew 19.5% into the downturn**.

**Agent 4 removed the catalyst.** Bank Indonesia raised the policy rate from 4.75% to
5.75% between May and June 2026 and held in July with a hike-biased consensus. Agent 2's
own sensitivity shows ±100bp of cost of equity moves value ±14.4% versus ±7.9% for a
±10% swing in mid-cycle EBIT. **This is a bet on the Indonesian 10-year yield more than
on presales — and the rate path is adverse.** The two findings compound.

## 1. Investment classification

**Cyclical, mid-downturn — not a compounder at a discount, and not a value trap.**

Not a trap because the asset backing is real and verified: independent appraisers valued
certain development land at Rp27,240bn against Rp9,906bn book, ~Rp1,102/share of
unrecognised value; no restricted cash; related-party dealing 0.05% of assets; audited
unqualified. Not a compounder because ROE of 9–11% sits **below** the Indonesian cost of
equity, and management reinvests at sub-COE returns.

## 2. One-line thesis

> The market is pricing a permanent de-rating of Indonesian property, but I believe CTRA
> is worth ~Rp1,000 because Rp1,650/share of marked land value and a 7-month advance book
> mean the downside is asset-protected — **provided** the buyer is paid to wait through
> at least two more down-years, which at Rp630 they are not quite.

## 3. Why is it cheap?

- Marketing sales −14% FY2025, −23% Q1 2026, −18% H1 2026; FY2026 guided down ~10%.
- FY2025 cash conversion of 3.0% (CFO Rp79bn vs net income Rp2,663bn).
- BI tightening 100bp into the most rate-sensitive sector on the exchange.
- A monotonic eleven-year de-rating: the market has paid progressively less for the same
  ~10% ROE every year.
- ROE below cost of equity, so book value compounds slower than the discount rate.

## 4. What is the market missing?

- **Possibly nothing.** This is the honest answer and it is the most important line in
  this document.
- The marked-to-appraisal land value (Rp27.2tn vs Rp9.9tn book) is real but has been real
  for years without closing the discount.
- BI's Q2 SHPR shows national residential sales recovering from −25.67% to −2.36% YoY —
  but **CTRA did not participate** (H1 −18% vs BSDE +1%, SMRA +37%), which reframes the
  problem as idiosyncratic mix/geography, not sector-wide. That is a *worse* diagnosis.

## 5. What changes?

- A BI cut. Consensus probability of any cut in 2026: ~10%.
- Presales inflecting positive — not visible; next likely event is a **formal guidance cut
  at Q3 in October**, ~60% probability per Agent 4.
- Rebuilding the advance book, which requires presales to exceed handovers.

## 6–8. Macro / Industry / Micro

**Macro: HEADWIND.** BI 5.75%, +100bp since May, hold-to-hike skew, KPR rate 7.44%.
**Industry: HEADWIND**, though possibly troughing — PPN DTP extended to 31 Dec 2027, but
an already-running subsidy that failed to arrest the decline is not a catalyst.
**Micro: NEGATIVE and idiosyncratic.** CTRA underperformed peers through the sector trough.

## 9. Fundamental earnings engine

Presales → (one-year lag, ~0.89x transfer) → recognised revenue on handover. 81% of
revenue is residential development at ~48.6% gross margin; ~19% is recurring (malls,
hotels, hospitals) at ~42.8%, and that recurring margin *fell* 4.2pp. **Rp561bn of FY2025
revenue is PSAK 115 imputed interest carrying no COGS** — 14.6% of operating profit — and
it shrinks as the advance book shrinks, so reported EBIT decays faster than trading
profit does.

## 12. Downside

Bear 600 is only −4.8%, and that is the strongest fact in the file — the asset backing
truncates the downside. The realistic risk is not permanent loss but **dead money**: a
0.48x multiple that stays at 0.48x for three years while ROE runs below COE.

## 13. Thesis-breaker

1. Q3 2026 presales failing to stabilise, or a formal guidance cut in October.
2. Contract liabilities falling below ~Rp6tn — forward cover under 6 months.
3. Partner receivables continuing to grow while presales fall (capital lent into a
   downturn), or any provision taken against the Rp1,566bn.
4. Another dividend increase funded by new debt against negative FCF.

## 14. Research gaps

- Presales split by city and price band — needed to test whether the underperformance is
  geographic mix.
- Terms and ageing of the Rp1,566bn partner receivables.
- Basis and date of the independent land appraisal.
- Debt maturity ladder against the tightening rate path.

## Verdict

**WATCH, not BUY.** Downgraded from the screener's BUY.

The margin of safety is real but is 37%, not 58%. The downside is genuinely protected.
What is absent is any reason for the discount to close within a definable period, against
a rate path moving the wrong way and a second earnings decline close to locked.

Agent 2's framing is the one to act on: at Rp630 CTRA clears the "undervalued" bar and
fails the "sufficiently large discount for a mid-downturn cyclical" bar by about one
notch. **Rp560–580 (0.42–0.44x book, printed earlier this year) clears both.** That is a
limit order, not a purchase.
