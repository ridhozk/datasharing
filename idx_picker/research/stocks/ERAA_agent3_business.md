# ERAA — Business / Industry / Competitive Position

Agent 3 output. Ticker: ERAA (Erajaya Swasembada Tbk.), IDX.

## What kind of business is this really?

**FACT** (broker-reported, SSI/Samuel Sekuritas initiation, 26 Jan 2026, sourced
from company data): ERAA's sales split by channel, 9M25: **Retail 71.8% /
Distribution 26.2%**, and this has been drifting toward retail for at least
five quarters (9M24: Retail 69.0% / Distribution 31.0%). Management's own
framing (per the same report): "Originally a distribution-focused business,
ERAA has increasingly shifted its contribution toward the higher-margin
retail segment."

**FACT** (same source): Product-line split, 9M25: Cellphones & Tablets
**78.1%**, Accessories & Other **15.6%**, Computers & Other Electronics
**4.1%**, E-vouchers **2.2%**. Handsets still dominate regardless of
retail/distribution channel mix.

**FACT**: Group margins, 9M25 vs 9M24 — Gross margin 11.2% vs 11.2%
(flat), Operating margin 2.2% vs 2.7% (down), Net margin 1.5% vs 1.6%
(down). This **confirms the CLAUDE.md hypothesis**: a ~11% gross margin at
group level says the P&L is still dominated by low-margin, high-turnover
distribution/retail-of-others'-brands economics, even after several years of
retail mix-shift and vertical diversification. The shift toward retail has
not yet visibly lifted blended operating margin — if anything opex from
store expansion has offset it recently.

**FACT**: ERAA disclosed a separately-run, higher-margin sub-pillar —
**Erajaya Active Lifestyle (ERAL / "SES")** — with a 9M25 EBIT margin of
**3.9%**, materially above ERAA's blended 2.2%. Management targets Active
Lifestyle + F&B + Beauty & Wellness ("segments beyond core handsets") to
reach **25–30% of group revenue** over the medium term, up from **16.1% in
9M25** (broker figure).

**Business-type verdict**: ERAA is not one business but two bolted together —
(1) a large, thin-margin, working-capital-heavy smartphone
distribution+retail core (Erajaya Digital: Erafone, iBox, Samsung/Xiaomi/
Huawei/Honor stores, Erablue) that is ~78% of store count and the large
majority of revenue, and (2) a smaller but faster-growing, better-margin
lifestyle/F&B/beauty portfolio of licensed foreign brands (Marshall, Garmin,
DJI, Under Armour, ASICS, JD Sports, Paris Baguette, Chagee, GrandLucky,
Bacha Coffee, and a new XPENG EV sole-distribution deal) still under 20% of
revenue. **THESIS**: the investment case is a bet on whether (2) scales fast
enough, and profitably enough, to change the blended economics of (1) — not
that (1) itself is a good business as currently mixed.

## iBox / Apple relationship — security and scale

**FACT**: iBox has operated as Apple's Indonesian reseller for **~20 years**
(founded/branded 2005 per the broker report; "20 years" per Erajaya's own PR,
April 2025), and self-reports **185 stores** (as of Sept 2025) spanning Apple
Premium Partner (APP), Apple Premium Reseller (APR), and Apple Authorized
Reseller (AAR) formats — the broker report calls iBox Indonesia's "largest
Apple reseller."

**FACT**: iBox is **not Apple's exclusive Indonesian channel**. Digimap,
Erafone (also ERAA's own), Story-i, and Blibli are also cited as official
Apple resellers/distributors. **INFERENCE**: this materially qualifies the
"moat" framing in the brief — iBox's advantage is scale/incumbency and store
count, not contractual exclusivity. An APR/AAR licence is revocable and
non-exclusive by nature; Apple could in principle expand any competitor's
authorization.

**FACT**: As of this research date, **Apple has not opened, and has no
disclosed concrete plan to open, its own direct retail stores in Indonesia**;
regional media (Detik, 2025) speculates whether Indonesia will eventually get
an official Apple Store, listing it as a live open question, not a stated
Apple intention. **Apple's ~US$1bn investment commitment (Feb 2025 deal,
ending the iPhone 16 sales ban) is for local component/assembly
manufacturing (e.g., AirTag-related production, operations from 2026), to
satisfy TKDN — not for retail.** So the near-term direct-entry risk to iBox
specifically looks low, but the manufacturing investment establishes Apple
operating infrastructure in-country that could in principle be a platform
for a future retail move. **ASSUMPTION-flagged as low-probability/near-term,
non-zero long-term.**

**FACT**: iPhone volumes are a swing factor for ERAA's results — the
iPhone 16 launch was delayed into Indonesia (only available from 11 Apr 2025
after the TKDN dispute resolved), which the broker cites as a driver of a
9M25 handset **volume** decline (7.5mn units, −11.0% YoY) even as revenue
rose (ASP/mix up). The iPhone 17 launch (Sept 2025) reportedly drove strong
queues and a same-store-sales boost into 1Q26 (SSSG +7.2% Jan–May 2026 per
earlier search). **INFERENCE**: this shows ERAA's revenue is meaningfully
levered to Apple's Indonesian launch cadence and to policy/TKDN timing risk,
not just organic demand — a single-brand concentration risk sitting inside
a "diversified distributor" narrative.

## TKDN / local-content policy

**FACT**: Indonesia enforces a Local Content Requirement (TKDN) on 4G/5G
handsets, historically referenced at a 35–40% threshold; Apple's iPhone 16
was blocked from Indonesian sale for roughly a year (through early 2025) for
non-compliance, ending only after Apple's investment commitment and TKDN
certification (standard-scheme certificate, 40% rate, exceeding the ~35%
floor) in March 2025. **FACT**: Minister of Industry Regulation No. 35/2025
(11 Sept 2025) revised certification procedure and **extended TKDN
certificate validity to 5 years** across sectors (up from a shorter cycle
previously).

**INFERENCE**: TKDN is a **brand-level** (manufacturer-level) compliance
burden — it falls on Apple, Samsung, Xiaomi, etc., not directly on ERAA as
distributor. Its effect on ERAA is indirect: it raises the fixed cost and
complexity of doing business in Indonesia for foreign brands, which
plausibly favours brands (and by extension their established local
distribution/retail partners with inventory-financing capacity and physical
footprint, like ERAA/iBox) able to absorb that complexity over new entrants
— but it does **not** by itself protect ERAA's own distribution margins or
prevent a brand from re-allocating volume to a competing local
distributor/retailer. **Insufficient evidence** on whether TKDN compliance
is itself a source of ERAA-specific advantage (e.g., through exclusive
assembly/JV arrangements) versus a market-wide brand-side cost. This is a
policy-driven complexity tailwind for *incumbency in general*, not a
demonstrated ERAA-specific moat.

## Market structure, growth, competition

**FACT** (broker-sourced, SSI Research analysis of ERAA/company data —
treat as company/broker claim, not independently verified): Erajaya Digital's
mobile-device retail market share by value in Indonesia rose from ~20% (2019)
to **~56% (2024)**. **INFERENCE**: if directionally accurate this is a real
scale advantage, but it is a single sell-side source citing company figures
in a BUY-rated initiation report — treat the magnitude with caution pending
independent corroboration (e.g., GfK/Canalys/IDC Indonesia smartphone
channel share data), which was not found in this research pass.
**Insufficient evidence — requires further research** to corroborate market
share independently.

**FACT**: Store network — 2,194 retail outlets, 77 distribution centers,
54,000+ third-party billed outlets across Indonesia/Malaysia/Singapore
(9M25/FY25 figures, broker report), up from 1,053 stores in 2020 (7-year
store-count CAGR ~13.3%). Erafone alone is ~62% of Erajaya Digital's outlet
count.

**FACT**: Same-store-sales growth (SSSG) has been volatile and
launch-cycle-driven: 9M24 +9.2%, FY24 +4.0%, 1Q25 −2.1%, 2Q25 (unclear from
extract), 3Q25 +2.8%, then reportedly +7.2% Jan–May 2026 on iPhone demand.
**INFERENCE**: this volatility, plus consumer research cited in the same
report (Nielsen: share of financially-secure "Thriver" consumers fell from
34% mid-2022 to 19% mid-2024; Kantar: middle-class shoppers trading down,
buying fewer brands/categories) suggests real demand-side pressure on
Indonesia's middle-income consumer, which is ERAA's core smartphone
addressable market. E-commerce and direct-to-consumer competition from
brands were flagged in the mandate as risks; **insufficient evidence** was
found in this pass to quantify e-commerce's share of Indonesian handset
sales or to assess brand direct-to-consumer inroads specifically against
ERAA (beyond the general point that TKDN-driven e-commerce sales blocks for
non-compliant devices, e.g. iPhone 16/Pixel bans on marketplaces, have
historically *protected* authorized physical retail channels like ERAA's).

## Capital allocation, ROIC, and diversification track record

**FACT** (screener baseline, `ERAA.md`): ROIC (TTM) **9.9%**, against the
research mandate's stated ~12% cost-of-capital reference — i.e. **ROIC below
WACC on current trailing figures**, consistent with a capital-intensive,
low-return distribution core. ROE (TTM) 16.5%, but ROE is inflated relative
to ROIC by leverage (Debt/Equity 0.84x, Net Debt IDR 6.67tn).

**FACT** (broker forecast, promotional BUY-rated source — treat as
THESIS/INFERENCE not fact): broker projects group ROE only reaching 14.6%
(2026F) / 15.2% (2027F) / **16.4% by 2030F**, still below the broker's own
cited retail-sector average ROE of **20.7%** — i.e., even in the bullish
broker's own long-range case, ERAA remains a **below-peer-return** business
on ROE, let alone ROIC. This directly supports the "low-return, capital-
hungry middleman" side of the key question, tempered by the fact that
returns are trending up, not down.

**FACT**: Net gearing/leverage is elevated but the broker projects
improvement: Debt/Equity 82.4% (2023A) → 72.9% (2024A) → ~70% (2025F,
broker) → 50% (2026–27F, broker); screener's independently-derived quarterly
D/E is 84.2%, consistent with the higher end of this range. Interest
coverage is thin: screener TTM 3.43x; broker's own historicals show 2.8x in
2023A/2024A. **INFERENCE**: this is a real balance-sheet risk for a
low-margin business — interest coverage in the 3x range leaves limited
cushion if handset demand or margins soften further.

**FACT**: Inventory swings materially with the Apple launch calendar —
screener quarterly data shows inventory rising from IDR 11.64tn (Dec-25) to
14.01tn (Mar-26, pre/post iPhone 17 ramp) then falling to 12.48tn (Jun-26).
FCF is volatile and frequently negative at the quarterly level (e.g. FCF
−IDR 4.23tn in 2Q25, screener data) even though the broker's own DCF-style
operating cash flow table shows OCF positive and growing (IDR 739bn 2023A →
1,503bn 2024A → 1,856bn 2025F → 2,213bn 2026F, broker projection).
**INFERENCE**: the discrepancy is consistent with a genuinely working-
capital-heavy, debt-financed inventory cycle around handset launches — cash
generation is lumpy and launch-timing dependent, not steady-state.

**Diversification track record (F&B, Beauty, Active Lifestyle, EV)**:
**FACT**: since 2020–21 ERAA has built four verticals (Erajaya Digital,
Active Lifestyle, Food & Nourishment, Beauty & Wellness) and in 2024–25
added an EV distribution business (sole XPENG distributor in Indonesia,
reportedly the first XPENG assembly line outside China) plus new F&B brands
(Chagee, Curry Up, Wetzel's Pretzels, Bacha Coffee) and lifestyle brands
(Under Armour, Wilson, ANTA, ASICS — all added 2025). **INFERENCE**: the one
disclosed profitability data point (ERAL's 3.9% EBIT margin vs group 2.2%)
suggests the *retail lifestyle brand-licensing* diversification is, so far,
margin-accretive rather than value-destructive — a genuinely positive signal
against the "capital-hungry, cash-destroying" hypothesis. However,
**insufficient evidence** was found on (a) the EV/XPENG vertical's
profitability or capital intensity (a brand-new, capital-intensive category
with no disclosed unit economics yet), (b) Beauty & Wellness's standalone
economics, or (c) whether new-vertical capex is funded from core distribution
cash flow or from incremental debt — the group's overall D/E trajectory
(rising through 2024–25 per both screener and broker data) is at least
consistent with debt-funded expansion.

## Governance and ownership

**FACT**: Controlling shareholder is **PT Eralink International**, holding
**55.2%** (broker report, Jan 2026) / one data source cites the controlling
vehicle as "Golden Bright Capital Holdings Pte Ltd" at 55.17% — **these two
figures likely describe the same effective control stake through a holding
chain, but the exact relationship between Eralink International and Golden
Bright Capital Holdings is unclear from sources found; insufficient evidence
to reconcile.** Free float ~43.5% (broker figure) / ~44.8% (alternate
source) — broadly consistent.

**FACT**: Board is dominated by the **Halim family**: Alexander Halim Kusuma
(President Commissioner, also CEO of Agung Sedayu Real Estate Indonesia and
VP Director of PT Pantai Indah Kapuk Dua Tbk — a related major property
group), Richard Halim Kusuma (Commissioner), Budiarto Halim (President
Director), Sintawati Halim (Director). Individual board members' direct
share ownership is minimal (0.00–0.05% each) — control runs through the
Eralink/Golden Bright holding vehicle, not direct personal stakes, which is
typical of Indonesian conglomerate structures but means the disclosed
0.04–0.05% figures understate real family control (~55%+ via the holding
company). **Insufficient evidence** — this research pass did not find
disclosure of related-party transaction terms/pricing (e.g., property
leases with PIK-affiliated entities, given the President Commissioner's PIK
Dua role) — a real governance question left open, flagged for further
research given the Commissioner's simultaneous senior role at a major
property developer.

**FACT**: Dividend — FY2024 net profit surpassed IDR 1tn (+25% YoY per
company PR), with **IDR 299.89bn distributed as dividends**, consistent with
the screener's ~25% payout ratio and ~5.4% trailing yield. **INFERENCE**: a
sub-30% payout ratio while leverage and diversification capex are both
elevated is a reasonable, non-aggressive capital-return policy — not
capital-return-starved, not overly generous either; the bulk of profit is
being reinvested into store/vertical expansion.

## Synthesis against the key question

The evidence supports a **middle answer**, not either pole of the framed
question. ERAA is **not** simply "a scaled, defensible distribution platform
mispriced at 4.7x earnings" — the 11% gross margin, sub-WACC ROIC (9.9%),
and below-peer-average ROE even in the bullish broker's 2030 forecast (16.4%
vs 20.7% sector) all say the distribution core is a real structurally
low-return business, and iBox's Apple relationship is scaled-incumbent, not
exclusive/contractually protected. It is also **not** simply "a capital-
hungry middleman destroying cash" — market share gains (if the 56% figure
holds up), the retail-mix shift, positive and improving operating cash
flow trend, a real (if thin) dividend, declining leverage trajectory
(broker-forecast), and a diversification vertical (ERAL) that already shows
better unit economics than the core, are all genuine, evidenced positives.
The cheapness (4.7x P/E, 0.78x P/B) looks like the market pricing in the
low-ROIC distribution economics and balance-sheet/working-capital risk at
face value, rather than being obviously wrong — but the mix-shift toward
higher-margin retail/lifestyle segments is a real, underway change that
could re-rate the multiple if it continues and if TKDN-driven policy
continues to favour incumbents. This reads as a **statistically cheap,
improving-but-still-subpar-return cyclical distributor**, not a clean
compounder and not a clean value trap.
